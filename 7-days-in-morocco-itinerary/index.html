    7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca: The Complete Route, Day by Day

    Plan a 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca: day-by-day route, real driving times, costs, best season and 3 alternative versions.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 27 October 2026

    A well-built 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca gives you the country’s three biggest hits in one clean loop: the imperial cities, the High Atlas Mountains and the Sahara dunes at Merzouga. Casablanca is where most international flights land, so starting there saves you a domestic transfer and an entire wasted day. In other words, you travel less and see more.

    However, one week in Morocco punishes bad planning. Distances look small on a map, yet mountain passes, gorge roads and long desert stretches eat hours. Therefore this guide gives you a realistic route, honest driving times, three alternative versions, a full cost breakdown and the mistakes that ruin most first trips.

    We drive these roads every week at Favorites Morocco Tours, so every timing below reflects the road rather than a mapping app. Let us walk through the week together.

    Why a 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca Beats Every Other Start

    Mohammed V International Airport (CMN) is the country’s main gateway. Consequently, it receives the widest choice of long-haul and European flights, often at the lowest fares. Landing there and building a loop back to the same airport keeps your air ticket simple and cheap.

    The airport advantage

    CMN sits about 30 kilometres south of the city centre and connects to the motorway network within minutes. As a result, your driver can collect you and be on the road to Rabat or Marrakech almost immediately. Meanwhile, arrivals into Marrakech or Fes often cost more and limit your routing options. Our guide to flights to Morocco compares the main gateways in detail.

    A true loop, not a one-way dash

    Because Casablanca sits roughly halfway up the Atlantic coast, it works as both the start and the finish of a circle. You head south to Marrakech, cross the Atlas, reach the dunes, come back north through Fes, and return along the motorway. Nothing gets repeated. That efficiency is exactly why a 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca beats a Marrakech-only trip for first-time visitors.

    Easy onward connections

    Morocco’s rail network runs along this corridor too. ONCF links Casablanca with Rabat, Kenitra, Tangier and Marrakech, so you can mix train and private driver. Check timetables on the official ONCF website.

    Your 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca at a Glance

    Before the detail, here is the shape of the week. Read it once, and the day-by-day sections below will make immediate sense.

    The route in one paragraph

    Casablanca → Marrakech → High Atlas and Ait Ben Haddou → Dades Gorge → Todra Gorge → Merzouga and the Erg Chebbi dunes → Ziz Valley and Midelt → Ifrane, Azrou and Fes → Meknes, Volubilis and Rabat → Casablanca. That circle covers roughly 1,900 kilometres.

    Driving times you can trust

    What this week does not include

    A 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca cannot cover everything. Chefchaouen, Tangier and the Atlantic south sit outside the loop, although the alternatives below show how to swap them in. If you can stretch your holiday, our 10 day Morocco itinerary adds the blue city and the coast without rushing.

    Before You Go: What to Sort Out First

    A little admin now protects the whole week. Fortunately, a 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca asks very little of you on paper.

    Visas and entry

    Citizens of the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand and the European Union enter visa-free for stays of up to 90 days. Nevertheless, rules change, so confirm your nationality on the Moroccan consular services portal before booking.

    Money

    The dirham (MAD) is a closed currency, which means you cannot buy it abroad in any useful quantity. Instead, withdraw cash at the airport or in town. Card payments work in hotels, riads and larger restaurants, yet souks, small cafés and rural stops still run on cash.

    Connectivity

    Buy a local SIM at the airport for a few euros. Coverage stays strong even in the desert. Our article on the SIM card in Morocco explains which operator suits travellers best.

    Clothing and etiquette

    Morocco is relaxed but modest. Cover shoulders and knees in medinas, and pack layers because desert nights turn cold. Read what to wear in Morocco for a season-by-season breakdown.

    Time and language

    Morocco runs on GMT+1 for most of the year, with a shift during Ramadan. Check what time it is in Morocco now before you book transfers. Arabic and Amazigh are official, while French works almost everywhere and English is common in tourism. Our guide to the language spoken in Morocco covers the useful phrases.

    Day 1 — Casablanca to Marrakech: Landing, the Great Mosque and the Red City

    Day one of your 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca sets the tone. You land, you see one genuine wonder, and then you drive south on a fast motorway to the city that will anchor the first half of your week.

    Morning: arrival and Hassan II Mosque

    Your driver meets you at CMN and heads straight to the Corniche. The Hassan II Mosque holds up to 105,000 worshippers, and part of its floor is glass, opening onto the Atlantic below. Guided visits run several times a day, so book a slot.

    Afternoon: the drive south

    The A7 motorway reaches Marrakech in about three hours. Olive and citrus fields give way to open plains, and the Atlas appears ahead. Consequently, you arrive with daylight to spare.

    Evening: Jemaa el-Fna

    Drop your bags at a riad inside the medina, then walk to Jemaa el-Fna as the sun drops. Food stalls appear and drummers set up. UNESCO recognised this gathering as a Masterpiece of Intangible Heritage, and it still feels unstaged.

    Day 2 — Marrakech: Palaces, Souks and a Coastal Option

    The second day of your 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca stays in one place. That single decision pays you back, because Marrakech rewards slow walking far more than a checklist sprint.

    Morning: the historic core

    Begin at the Koutoubia Mosque, whose 77-metre minaret has guided travellers since the twelfth century. Continue to the Bahia Palace, a nineteenth-century vizier’s residence with painted cedar ceilings. Then visit the Saadian Tombs, sealed for centuries and rediscovered in 1917.

    Late morning: the souks

    Head north from the square into the covered markets. Each alley specialises: leather in one lane, brass in another, spices further on. Bargain openly, starting near half the asking price. Our complete Marrakech travel guide maps the medina street by street.

    Afternoon: gardens or hammam

    The Jardin Majorelle and the neighbouring Yves Saint Laurent Museum offer cool shade and cobalt walls. Otherwise, book a traditional steam bath. A Moroccan hammam after a long flight resets your body better than any nap.

    The Essaouira alternative

    Some travellers prefer sea air on day two. Essaouira sits two and a half hours west, and its ramparts, fishing port and UNESCO-listed medina make a superb day out. Our Essaouira full day trip from Marrakech covers the round journey comfortably.

    Other day trips near Marrakech

    Prefer mountains or waterfalls? The Ourika Valley, the Imlil Valley and the Ouzoud waterfalls all work as single days. See the full list of day trips from Marrakech.

    Day 3 — Marrakech to Dades Gorge Across the High Atlas

    Day three of this 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca changes the landscape completely. You climb over the mountains, meet a UNESCO kasbah and finish in a red canyon. Many travellers call this the most beautiful driving day of the whole week.

    Morning: the Tizi n’Tichka pass

    The road climbs to 2,260 metres through switchbacks, walnut terraces and mud-brick villages. Stop often. Recent widening made the pass smoother, although it still demands an unhurried driver.

    Midday: Ait Ben Haddou

    Next comes the highlight. Ait Ben Haddou is a fortified earthen village on the old caravan route to Timbuktu, and UNESCO inscribed it in 1987. You can read its official UNESCO listing for background. Climb to the granary at the top for the valley view, then eat lunch beside the river.

    Afternoon: Ouarzazate and the Valley of Roses

    Ouarzazate is Morocco’s film capital, home to Atlas Studios and the Taourirt Kasbah. Afterwards, the road follows the Dades Valley past Skoura’s palm oasis and Kelaat M’Gouna, the rose-growing town.

    Evening: into the gorge

    You sleep in the Dades Gorge, where the famous hairpin road coils up a red cliff. Dinner is usually tagine on a terrace with almost no light pollution overhead. If this stretch alone appeals to you, our Ait Ben Haddou day trip covers a shorter version.

    Day 4 — Dades to Todra Gorge and the Merzouga Dunes

    Day four of the 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca is the one people remember. You wake in a canyon, walk between 300-metre cliffs, cross the pre-Sahara and finish the evening on a camel in the dunes.

    Morning: Todra Gorge

    Todra narrows to roughly ten metres in places, with walls rising several hundred metres above a cold stream. Climbers come from all over Europe. Walk the flat gorge floor for half an hour, and the scale becomes obvious.

    Afternoon: crossing to Merzouga

    The road runs east through Erfoud and Rissani, the old caravan capital and market town of the Tafilalet. Fossil workshops line the roadside, because this area sits on a seabed that dried out 400 million years ago. Rissani’s souk runs on Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday, so time it if you can.

    Sunset: camel trek into Erg Chebbi

    You reach Merzouga in the late afternoon. Camels wait at the edge of the sand, and the trek takes about an hour into the dunes, some of which rise close to 150 metres. Watching the light change across that much sand is the single strongest memory most travellers bring home.

    Night: the desert camp

    Camps range from simple Berber tents to luxury suites with private bathrooms. Dinner arrives under the stars, drummers play afterwards, and the silence is total. Our article on Merzouga camel trekking explains what to bring.

    Which dunes are these?

    Erg Chebbi is the eastern dune sea, and it is the one this route uses. Erg Chigaga, further south-west, is wilder but needs extra time. We compare both in Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga.

    Day 5 — Sahara Sunrise, the Ziz Valley and Midelt

    The fifth day of your 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca starts early and then turns north. You swap sand for the longest palm valley in Morocco and end in a mountain town between two ranges.

    Dawn: sunrise over the dunes

    Climb the nearest ridge before first light. The sand shifts from grey to pink to orange within twenty minutes, and the temperature stays pleasant. Bring water and a headtorch, because the climb is steeper than it looks.

    Midday: through the Ziz Valley

    North of Erfoud, the Ziz Valley cuts a green ribbon through bare rock. The viewpoint above Aoufous shows thousands of palms packed into a canyon floor, with kasbahs stacked along the edges. It is one of the most photographed panoramas in the country, and rightly so.

    Afternoon: Errachidia to Midelt

    The road climbs through the Tizi n’Talghemt pass into the High Atlas. Midelt sits at 1,500 metres between two ranges, surrounded by apple orchards. Evenings turn cool even in summer, so keep a fleece handy.

    Day 6 — Midelt to Fes Through Cedar Forests and Ifrane

    Day six of your 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca crosses the Middle Atlas and reaches the oldest imperial capital. The contrast between alpine forest and medieval medina, inside a single afternoon, is one of Morocco’s strangest pleasures.

    Morning: Azrou and the cedar forest

    The Azrou cedar forest holds some of the largest Atlas cedars on earth, and troops of Barbary macaques live among them. Please do not feed them, since human food harms the group. Stop at the Gouraud Cedar area for a short walk.

    Midday: Ifrane

    Ifrane looks nothing like the rest of the country. The French built it in the 1930s with pitched roofs and dormer windows, so locals call it the Switzerland of Morocco. It also recorded Africa’s lowest ever temperature, back in 1935.

    Afternoon: arriving in Fes

    Fes el-Bali joined the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1981, and its medina holds around 9,000 lanes. Cars cannot enter, so donkeys still carry goods. Check into a riad, then walk before dark.

    Day 7 — Fes, Meknes, Volubilis and Back to Casablanca

    The final day closes the circle. You spend the morning in the world’s oldest living medina, then travel west through two more UNESCO sites before returning to the coast.

    Morning: the heart of Fes

    Start at the Bou Inania Madrasa, a fourteenth-century college covered in carved cedar and zellij. Continue past Al Quaraouiyine, founded in 859 and widely recognised as the oldest continuously operating university on earth. Then look down over the Chouara tanneries, where workers still dye hides in stone vats.

    Late morning: Meknes

    An hour west, Meknes was the seventeenth-century capital of Sultan Moulay Ismail. Bab Mansour is arguably the finest gate in Morocco, and the granaries at Heri es-Souani show how the sultan fed 12,000 horses. The city joined the UNESCO list in 1996.

    Midday: Volubilis

    Just north sit the Roman ruins of Volubilis, the best-preserved classical site in Morocco. Its mosaics remain in place under the open sky, and the Capitol, basilica and triumphal arch still stand. UNESCO inscribed the site in 1997, and you can read the official listing here.

    Afternoon: Rabat

    Morocco’s capital surprises people. The Kasbah of the Oudayas drops in blue-and-white lanes to the river mouth, while the unfinished Hassan Tower stands beside the Mausoleum of Mohammed V. Rabat is calm, green and easy to walk. Our article on whether Marrakech is the capital of Morocco explains the history behind that common mix-up.

    Evening: Casablanca

    A final hour on the motorway brings you back to Casablanca for your flight or a last night by the sea. That completes the loop, and it completes a full 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca without a single repeated road.

    Three Alternative Versions of This 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca

    You can shape a 7 day Morocco itinerary in several ways. Pick the version that matches your interests rather than the standard loop.

    Version A: the northern route with Chefchaouen

    Swap Marrakech for the blue city. Drive to Rabat and Chefchaouen on day one, wander the blue lanes on day two, reach Fes on day three, run to Merzouga on day four, cross to Dades and Ait Ben Haddou on days five and six, and finish in Marrakech. This version needs an open-jaw flight out of Marrakech.

    Version B: the slow imperial cities loop

    Skip the Sahara entirely. Give two nights to Marrakech, two to Fes, one to Chefchaouen and one to Rabat. You drive perhaps 800 kilometres instead of 1,900. Families with young children prefer this shape, and our family tours in Morocco follow a similar rhythm.

    Version C: the reverse direction

    Run the circle anticlockwise: Casablanca, Rabat, Fes, Merzouga, Dades, Ait Ben Haddou, Marrakech, then the motorway back. Reversing helps if you land late, because Rabat is only an hour away. Our 7 days private tour from Casablanca uses this sequence.

    Starting somewhere else

    Not everyone lands at CMN. If you arrive in the north, look at our 7 days tour from Tangier. Travellers landing inland often prefer a tour from Fes, while a Marrakech arrival suits our 7 days private tour from Marrakech.

    How Much Does a 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca Cost?

    Prices swing with comfort level and group size. The ranges below reflect real 2026 spending, excluding international flights.

    Budget: roughly €450 to €700 per person

    You share a group minivan, sleep in simple riads and standard desert tents, and eat set menus. Everything on the route still happens; only the comfort level drops.

    Mid-range: roughly €900 to €1,400 per person

    The sweet spot for most couples. You get a private car and driver, boutique riads inside the medinas, a comfortable desert camp, and freedom to change the day as you go.

    Premium: €2,000 and upwards per person

    Expect five-star riads, luxury desert suites, licensed city guides in every city, and a modern 4×4. Some travellers also add a domestic flight to save a driving day.

    What you pay for separately

    • Lunches, which typically run €7 to €15 per person
    • Entrance tickets, roughly €2 to €12 per monument
    • Tips for your driver, guides and camp staff

    Where the money is best spent

    Upgrade the desert camp and the Fes riad first. Those two nights shape your memory far more than a fancier car does. Our comparison of private versus group tours in Morocco covers the trade-offs.

    Following the Caravanserai Route: Morocco’s Historic Fondouks

    Every road on this loop follows an older one. For a thousand years, camel caravans carried gold, salt, ivory and manuscripts between the Sahara and the Atlantic, and they stopped each night at a caravanserai. In Morocco these inns are called a funduq or fondouk, and several survive along your exact route.

    What a caravanserai actually was

    The word comes from Persian and means caravan palace. Every one followed the same plan: a fortified square, one gate wide enough for loaded camels, stables below and sleeping rooms on a gallery above. Traders slept over their goods, so theft stayed rare. Consequently, a caravanserai worked as warehouse, hotel, bank and marketplace at once.

    Sijilmassa and Rissani: where the caravans began

    On day four you pass Rissani, built beside the ruins of Sijilmassa. That medieval city was the northern gateway of the trans-Saharan gold trade, and caravans of several thousand camels left here for Timbuktu. Little survives above ground, yet the mud walls and the market still carry the atmosphere.

    Ait Ben Haddou: the caravanserai as a fortress

    Ait Ben Haddou existed because caravans needed a safe night between the desert and Marrakech. Its towers, granary and single defended entrance follow caravanserai logic, scaled up to a whole village. Standing below the kasbahs, you see instantly why the route ran here.

    Funduq al-Najjarine in Fes

    Fes preserves the finest example. The Nejjarine fondouk dates from the early eighteenth century, and craftsmen restored it in the 1990s as a museum of wooden arts. Walk into the courtyard and look up: three storeys of carved cedar galleries. At its peak, Fes counted around two hundred working funduqs.

    Marrakech’s surviving fondouks

    Several fondouks near the Mouassine Mosque still work, now sheltering leather workshops, lantern makers and weavers instead of camels. Entry usually costs nothing, and the artisans welcome visitors. Our Marrakech old town guide points you to the right alleys.

    Why the caravanserai matters to your trip

    Once you notice the pattern, the whole week reads differently. The oases, kasbahs and market towns on this route are not random. They sit exactly one caravan day apart, which is roughly 30 to 40 kilometres. Your 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca is, in effect, a motorised version of a thousand-year-old trade road. Our article on Berber culture in Morocco explores the communities who kept it running.

    The Best Time of Year for a 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca

    Season changes this trip more than anything else, because you cross the coast, the mountains and the desert in one week.

    Spring: March to May

    Spring is the strongest choice. Days reach a comfortable 22 to 28 degrees, the Atlas still holds snow, almond and rose blossom fills the valleys, and desert nights stay mild. Book early, since this is peak season.

    Autumn: September to November

    Autumn matches spring for comfort and brings fewer crowds. October in particular delivers clear light for photography. Read our September weather guide if you travel early in the season.

    Summer: June to August

    Marrakech, Ouarzazate and Merzouga regularly exceed 40 degrees. The route still works, although you must start at dawn, rest at midday and move the camel trek later. Coastal alternatives such as Essaouira stay pleasantly cool.

    Winter: December to February

    Winter days are bright and mild, yet desert nights drop close to freezing and Atlas passes can close briefly after snow. Bring a proper coat. Festive travellers may enjoy our New Year in Morocco departures and the December holiday guide.

    Ramadan

    During Ramadan, restaurants in smaller towns close during daylight while tourist hotels operate normally. The atmosphere at sunset is memorable. The dates shift about eleven days earlier each year, so check before booking. Our full best time to visit Morocco guide covers every month.

    Where to Sleep on Each Night of Your 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca

    Accommodation on this loop is half the experience. Fortunately, Morocco offers a distinctive option nearly everywhere.

    Nights one and two: Marrakech riads

    Sleep inside the medina walls. A riad is a courtyard house built inward around a fountain, which keeps it cool and quiet despite the noise outside. Rooftop breakfast with the Atlas on the horizon is standard.

    Night three: Dades Gorge

    Small family-run hotels line the canyon floor. Rooms stay simple, terraces are wide, and the star visibility is outstanding.

    Night four: the desert camp

    Choose between a standard Berber camp and a luxury camp with en-suite tents, rugs and proper beds. Both include dinner, music and breakfast. Either way, the sky delivers.

    Night five: Midelt

    Kasbah-style hotels with gardens and mountain views dominate here. Nothing is fancy, though the food is often excellent and the quiet is welcome after the desert.

    Night six: a Fes riad

    Fes riads run larger and older than those in Marrakech, with monumental doors and carved plaster. Choose one near Bab Boujloud or Batha so a luggage cart can reach the door.

    What to Pack for a 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca

    You will move through four climates in seven days, so pack for range rather than for one destination.

    Clothing essentials

    • Loose trousers or long skirts, plus tops that cover the shoulders
    • One warm fleece and a windproof layer for the dunes and Midelt
    • A scarf, which works for sun, dust, cold and mosque visits
    • Closed walking shoes for gorges and medina cobbles

    Practical items

    • A refillable bottle, because plastic waste is a real problem in the desert
    • Sunscreen, sunglasses and a wide-brimmed hat
    • A power bank, since camp electricity often runs on a generator
    • Small dirham notes for tips, taxis and souk purchases

    What to Eat, Day by Day

    Moroccan food changes with altitude and region, so a 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca doubles as a culinary route.

    On the coast

    Casablanca and Rabat serve superb grilled fish, plus the tomato-and-pepper salad called taktouka. Try harira too, the tomato and lentil soup eaten at any hour.

    In Marrakech

    Look for tanjia, slow-cooked lamb buried in the ashes of a hammam furnace. Few visitors ever hear about it. Jemaa el-Fna also serves the famous snail soup.

    In the mountains and the desert

    Berber omelette arrives in a clay tagine with tomato, onion and cumin. In the camps, look for medfouna, a stuffed flatbread baked in sand and embers.

    In Fes

    Fes is the culinary capital. Order pastilla, a pigeon or chicken pie dusted with cinnamon and icing sugar. Our guides to cuisine in Morocco and traditional Moroccan dishes go deeper.

    Six Mistakes That Ruin a 7 Days in Morocco Itinerary From Casablanca

    Most disappointing trips fail for predictable reasons. Avoid these six, and your week runs smoothly.

    1. Trying to add Chefchaouen to the desert loop

    The blue city sits four hours north of Fes, in the opposite direction from the Sahara. Adding it to this circle forces a brutal driving day. Choose one or the other, or extend to ten days.

    2. Booking a flight out too early on day seven

    An 11 a.m. departure means leaving Fes at dawn and skipping Meknes, Volubilis and Rabat. Take the evening flight instead.

    3. Underestimating the desert night

    Merzouga can fall below 5 degrees in winter and stays cool even in spring. Travellers who pack only summer clothes regret it within an hour of sunset.

    4. Carrying no cash

    Card machines fail in rural areas, and camps rarely accept cards. Withdraw enough in Marrakech to cover the desert leg.

    5. Ignoring guide licensing

    Fes and Marrakech require licensed guides inside the medinas. Unlicensed ones often steer you to commission shops. Our guide on choosing a tour guide or going independent explains the rules.

    6. Reading alarming headlines without context

    Morocco is one of the safest countries in the region for visitors. We examined the official advisories directly in Morocco travel warnings.

    Private Driver, Group Tour or Self-Drive?

    Transport shapes a 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca more than any other choice, so decide it before you book anything else.

    Private driver: the default recommendation

    A private driver handles mountain passes, parking, police checkpoints and language. Moreover, you can stop wherever the light is good. For a route with 1,900 kilometres and several gorge roads, this is the option we recommend to almost everyone.

    Small group tours

    Group departures cut the price and suit solo travellers who enjoy company. The trade-off is a fixed schedule. Browse our Morocco tours to compare formats.

    Self-drive

    Renting works if you drive confidently. Roads are decent and signage is bilingual. However, medina parking, rural checkpoints and the Tizi n’Tichka switchbacks intimidate many visitors, and a breakdown costs a day you do not have.

    Frequently Asked Questions
    




    Is a 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca enough to see the country?
    




    Yes, for one strong region plus the Sahara. You will not see everything, yet you will experience the imperial cities, the High Atlas and the dunes properly. Travellers who also want the north should plan ten days or more.
    




    How many hours a day will I spend in the car?
    




    Expect three to four hours on light days and around six hours on the two transfer days. Stops break the journey roughly every ninety minutes, so it feels far shorter than the raw numbers suggest.
    




    Can I do this 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca with children?
    




    Yes, although we usually shorten the driving days and add a night in Ouarzazate. Camel rides, film studios and the dunes work brilliantly for children aged six and above. See our family in Morocco page.
    




    What about a morocco in 7 days itinerary starting in Marrakech?
    




    That version works well and saves the Casablanca transfer. Start with two nights in Marrakech, then follow the same desert route to Fes and fly home from there. Our tours from Marrakech use exactly that shape.
    




    Is there a good 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Fez?
    




    Yes. Fes to Merzouga, then Dades, Ait Ben Haddou and Marrakech makes an excellent week. Look at our 6 days Fes to Marrakech tour or the 5 days Fes to Merzouga and Marrakech tour and add days to taste.
    




    Can I combine an itinerary for 7 days in Spain and Morocco?
    




    You can, although one week across two countries is tight. Most travellers ferry from Tarifa or Algeciras to Tangier and stay north. Start from our tours from Tangier instead, because the Sahara sits too far south.
    




    How far is Casablanca from the Sahara desert?
    




    Merzouga lies roughly 900 kilometres from Casablanca by road. Nobody drives it in one go. This route reaches the dunes on day four, breaking the distance with Marrakech, Ait Ben Haddou and the Dades Gorge.
    




    Do I need a visa for Morocco?
    




    Most Western passports do not. American, Canadian, British, Australian and EU citizens enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Always confirm your own nationality before booking.
    




    What is the best month for this route?
    




    April and October give the finest balance of temperature, light and crowd levels. March, May, September and November follow closely.
    




    Is Morocco safe for solo travellers?
    




    Yes, and thousands travel alone every year. Dress modestly, use licensed guides inside the medinas, and book accommodation ahead. A private driver removes almost all of the friction people worry about.
    




    Should I fly home from Casablanca or Marrakech?
    




    Returning to Casablanca keeps your ticket simple and closes the loop cleanly. Nevertheless, an open-jaw flight into Casablanca and out of Marrakech saves you the final motorway run, so compare prices in our flights to Marrakech guide.
    




    Final Thoughts: Build the Week Around What You Love
    




    This 7 days in Morocco itinerary from Casablanca works because it follows geography rather than fighting it. You travel south with the motorway, cross the Atlas once, thread the pre-Sahara from west to east, and come home along the northern plain. Every kilometre earns its place.
