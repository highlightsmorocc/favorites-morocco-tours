    Tetouan Morocco: Things to Do, How to Get There (2026)

    Tetouan Morocco guide 2026: things to do in the UNESCO medina how to get there from Tangier or Spain beaches day trips and is Tetouan worth visiting

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 25 September 2026

    Tetouan Morocco is the white city that looks out over the Mediterranean between Tangier and Chefchaouen. Its medina is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, Spanish is still spoken in many shops, and in 2026 the city is living its biggest cultural year in decades. Yet most travellers only see it through a bus window on the way to the blue city. This guide covers the best things to do in Tetouan, how to get there, how long to stay and how it fits into a northern Morocco trip. It also answers the question many people type into Google: is Tetouan worth visiting?

    Quick answer: Tetouan Morocco sits in the far north of the country, about 60 km from Tangier and 40 km from the Spanish city of Ceuta. Its Andalusian medina has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1997. One full day covers the highlights; two days add museums and the beach. In 2026 it is a Mediterranean Capital of Culture and Dialogue.

    Where is Tetouan Morocco?

    Location and key distances

    Tetouan climbs the slopes of Jebel Dersa at the foot of the Rif Mountains. The Martil valley links it to the sea, which lies only about 10 km away. As a result, the city enjoys a Mediterranean climate and has beaches close by. It also sits at the tip of a very handy triangle: Tangier to the west, Ceuta to the north and Chefchaouen to the south.

    If you want the bigger picture, our guide to where Morocco is explains the country’s geography step by step. And if you are comparing destinations, our overview of cities in Morocco puts Tetouan next to Fes, Marrakech and Tangier.

    Tetouan, Tétouan or Tetuán?

    All three are correct. Tetouan is the usual English and French spelling, while Tetuán is the Spanish one. UNESCO lists the old town as the Medina of Tétouan, formerly known as Titawin. So if you search in several languages, you will find the same city under different names. One tip: Madrid has a district called Tetuán, named after an 1860 war, so add the word Morocco when you look for travel information.

    Why locals call it the White Dove

    From the Martil road, the nickname makes sense at once. Whitewashed houses spill down the hillside like a bird resting above the valley. It is a very different white from the blue of Chefchaouen or the ochre of Marrakech. In fact, the Moroccan tourist board also calls it the white dove of the Mediterranean on its official page about Tetouan.

    Is Tetouan worth visiting?

    Yes, especially if you want an authentic medina without the pressure of the big tourist cities. Here is an honest look at the pros and the trade-offs.

    Why Tetouan is worth it

    • UNESCO describes its medina as one of the smallest in Morocco but also the most complete.
    • There are far fewer hustlers and touts than in Marrakech or Fes.
    • You get a medina, a Spanish art deco quarter and the beach in the same day.
    • It is cheaper than the southern cities for food, riads and transport.
    • In 2026 it hosts a full year of cultural events as a Mediterranean capital.

    What to keep in mind

    • The city wakes up late, so the souks are quiet before 11 am.
    • Signage in English is limited; Spanish and French help more.
    • It is more conservative than Tangier, so dress modestly in the old town.
    • Winter brings frequent rain, and July and August bring crowds to the coast.

    Tetouan or Chefchaouen: which one should you choose?

    Many travellers only have time for one northern city, so here is a simple rule. Choose Chefchaouen for photos, mountain walks and a slow village mood. Choose Tetouan Morocco for history, crafts, a real working medina and the beach nearby. Better still, combine both: they are only 60 km apart, and one night in each is the ideal balance. In fact, most of our northern routes do exactly that.

    A short history of Tetouan Morocco

    Tamuda, the city before the city

    A few kilometres from the centre, near the Martil river, lie the ruins of Tamuda. It began as a Mauretanian town and later became a Roman camp. Therefore, people have lived here for more than two thousand years. Today, many finds from the site are in the Archaeological Museum of Tetouan.

    The Andalusian rebirth

    According to UNESCO, Tetouan mattered from the 8th century onwards as the main point of contact between Morocco and Al-Andalus. However, a Castilian raid left it in ruins around 1400. Later, at the end of the 15th century, refugees from Granada led by Sidi Ali al-Mandri rebuilt it. They added walls, courtyard houses, fountains and orange-tree gardens. That is why the medina still feels like a piece of Granada moved to the Rif.

    Moriscos, Sephardic Jews and Andalusian music

    After 1492, and again after the expulsion of the Moriscos in the early 17th century, more Andalusian families arrived. A large Sephardic Jewish community also settled here. For centuries its members spoke Haketia, a blend of old Spanish, Hebrew and Arabic. Moreover, the refugees brought the Andalusian music that still fills weddings and festivals today, played with lutes, violins and hand drums.

    Capital of the Spanish Protectorate

    Spanish troops first occupied Tetouan in 1860, after the Spanish-Moroccan War, and stayed until 1862. That campaign is also why Madrid and Barcelona both have places named Tetuán. Then, from 1913 to 1956, Tetouan was the capital of the Spanish Protectorate in northern Morocco. The sultan kept sovereignty through a representative, the khalifa, who lived in the city. Meanwhile, Spain ran the administration and built the Ensanche, the new town. In other words, Tetouan was never part of Spain, but Spain left a deep mark on it.

    Things to do in Tetouan Morocco: 12 highlights

    Here is the practical part. We have ordered the sights so you can walk them in one day, starting at Place Hassan II. If you are wondering what to see in Tetouan with little time, focus on the first five.

    1. The UNESCO medina

    The Medina of Tétouan joined the World Heritage List in 1997. The official UNESCO page notes that its buildings have remained largely untouched by later outside influences. There are far fewer tourists than in Fes, so shopkeepers push much less. One tip: life starts late here. Therefore, the souks are busiest between 11 am and 1 pm.

    How to explore the Tetouan medina

    Enter through Bab er-Rouah beside the palace, then let the lanes guide you. The medina is small enough that you always come out near a gate you know. In addition, locals are used to helping lost visitors, often in Spanish. For a first visit to Tetouan Morocco, a two-hour walk with a licensed guide is worth it, because it opens doors to workshops you would never find alone.

    2. The walls and the seven gates

    A wall of about 5 km surrounds the medina, and seven gates lead inside. The most photogenic are Bab el Okla, the Queen’s Gate, and Bab er-Rouah beside the Royal Palace. Meanwhile, Bab Sebta, the Ceuta Gate, opens the road to the north. Use the gates as landmarks: if you get lost, simply ask for the nearest one.

    3. Place Hassan II and the Royal Palace

    This wide white square joins the medina and the Ensanche. The Royal Palace is closed to visitors, but its bronze doors and uniformed guards are worth a stop. It is also the easiest meeting point if you travel in a group or with a guide.

    4. The souks and craft guilds

    Inside the medina, each lane has its trade. You will find carpenters, coppersmiths, weavers and spice sellers. In the Guersa el Kebira, for example, stalls sell fabric and traditional clothing. Near the gates you will also see Jebala women from the Rif in striped red-and-white cloths and wide straw hats with woollen tassels. They sell fresh goat’s cheese, bread and vegetables from their farms. Always ask before taking a photo.

    5. The School of Arts and Crafts (Dar Sanaa)

    Without a doubt, this is the visit that surprises people most. The school was founded in 1919 and moved in 1928 to its current building outside Bab el Okla. Behind its Andalusian courtyard, masters and apprentices work on zellige tiles, carved cedar, plaster, embroidery and marquetry. Thanks to this know-how, Tetouan became the first Moroccan city in the UNESCO Creative Cities Network, for crafts and folk art, in 2017. It opens on weekdays, so check the hours on arrival.

    6. The Ethnographic Museum at Bab el Okla

    The museum sits inside an old bastion of the city wall, next to the school. It displays wedding dress, jewellery, instruments of Andalusian music and scenes of daily life. If clothing interests you, pair the visit with our guide to traditional Moroccan clothing.

    7. The Mellah and Jewish heritage

    The Jewish quarter was laid out in 1807 beside the Royal Palace. Very few Jewish families remain today. Even so, the Mellah keeps its iron balconies, straight lanes and a few closed synagogues. With a local guide, you will notice details that are easy to miss on your own.

    8. The Spanish Ensanche

    Beyond the medina walls lies the new town built by the Protectorate from 1913. Much of Avenue Mohammed V is pedestrian and lined with cafés. On Place Moulay el Mehdi, the former Plaza de España, stands the church of Our Lady of Victories. Look up at the façades as you walk: they mix art deco, modernism and neo-Moorish details.

    9. The Modern Art Centre in the old station

    The building is as striking as the collection. It was once the station of the Ceuta–Tetouan railway, built between 1916 and 1918 in a fortress-like neo-Moorish style. After a long restoration, it reopened as the Tetouan Modern Art Centre on 20 November 2013. Today its rooms trace Moroccan painting from the Protectorate to the present day.

    10. The Archaeological Museum and Tamuda

    In the Ensanche, the Archaeological Museum holds mosaics, bronzes and pottery from Tamuda, Lixus and other northern sites. It is small, so one hour is enough. If you have a car, the ruins of Tamuda are just a few minutes away on the Chefchaouen road.

    11. The tannery and the cemetery viewpoint

    Near Bab el Mqabar, the Cemetery Gate, you will find the tannery. It is smaller than the famous one in Fes, although the work is just as traditional. From the slope of the Muslim cemetery above, you also get one of the best views of the white medina with the sea beyond.

    12. A rooftop at sunset

    End the day on a terrace in the medina or the Ensanche. From above, you can see the Rif, the Martil valley and the minarets. Note that Morocco moved to permanent GMT on 20 September 2026, so sunset now comes an hour earlier on the clock than last summer. Check what time it is in Morocco now before you plan your evening.

    Tetouan Morocco in 2026: a Mediterranean Capital of Culture

    What the title means for visitors

    The Union for the Mediterranean and the Anna Lindh Foundation named Tetouan and Matera in Italy the Mediterranean Capitals of Culture and Dialogue 2026. The announcement came in Barcelona on 28 November 2024. In practice, it means a full year of concerts, exhibitions, talks and street events. Morocco’s culture minister, Mohammed Mehdi Bensaid, chaired the official launch in Tetouan.

    Tetouan Mediterranean Film Festival: 3–10 October 2026

    The festival began in 1985 and is one of the oldest in the country. Its 31st edition runs from 3 to 10 October 2026. Spanish actress Mónica López chairs the main jury, and Moroccan director Hicham Lasri is among those receiving a tribute. If you love cinema, book your room early. For more screen history, read our guide to movies filmed in Morocco.

    Tetouan in The Time in Between

    For many Spanish readers, Tetouan Morocco is the setting of a bestseller. In The Time in Between, published by María Dueñas in 2009, the heroine opens a dressmaking studio in Protectorate-era Tetouan. Later, the TV series brought those streets to a global audience. As a result, many visitors now stroll the Ensanche looking for Sira Quiroga’s world. Indeed, the cafés of Avenue Mohammed V still make that era easy to imagine.

    How to get to Tetouan Morocco

    Flights to Tetouan airport

    Tetouan Sania Ramel airport (TTU) lies about 10 minutes from the centre. Since 27 March 2026, Royal Air Maroc has run a base there, with direct flights to London Gatwick, Paris, Brussels, Madrid, Barcelona and Malaga. In addition, Ryanair flies from Madrid, Malaga, Alicante, Brussels Charleroi and Marseille. Air Arabia Maroc links Tetouan with Amsterdam, and TUI fly serves Brussels. There are no direct flights from North America, so US and Canadian travellers usually connect in Europe. Compare routes in our guide to flights to Morocco.

    How to get from Tangier to Tetouan

    This is the most common route. First, you reach Tangier by plane, train or the one-hour ferry from Tarifa in Spain. Then you have three ways to cover the 60 km to Tetouan. CTM buses are comfortable and cheap. Shared grand taxis leave when full and cost about 35–40 dirhams per seat. Finally, a private transfer can pick you up at the port or airport and drop you at your riad in about an hour.

    From Spain via Ceuta

    Tetouan is only about 40 km from Ceuta. First, take the ferry from Algeciras to Ceuta, which takes around an hour. Next, cross the Tarajal border on foot or by car. On the Moroccan side, in Fnideq, shared grand taxis run to Tetouan for about 25–30 dirhams per seat. On a normal day, allow 1 to 1.5 hours from central Ceuta. In summer and on holidays, however, border queues can be long. See our Morocco tours from Spain if you would rather have everything arranged.

    Can you visit Tetouan on a day trip from Spain?

    Yes, and it is a popular excursion from the Costa del Sol and Gibraltar. First, take the morning ferry from Algeciras to Ceuta. Then cross the border and reach Tetouan Morocco in about 40 minutes. That leaves time for the medina, lunch and the Ensanche before heading back in the afternoon. However, plan your return carefully, because the border sets the pace of the day.

    Tetouan to Chefchaouen

    The blue city is about 60 km south, a scenic mountain drive of 1 hr 15 min to 1 hr 30 min. CTM buses and shared taxis run all day. Moreover, the road climbs through cork oaks and small Jebala villages. Even so, a private car lets you stop at viewpoints along the way.

    Tetouan to Fes and Casablanca

    Tetouan has no railway, because the Rif mountains made it too difficult. The nearest station is in Tangier, linked to Rabat and Casablanca by the Al Boraq high-speed train. To reach Fes, drive about 260 km, or around 4 hr 30 min, with a stop in Chefchaouen. For more on the imperial city, read our guide to Fes, Morocco.

    Travelling to Tetouan from the US, Canada and Australia

    More long-haul travellers now add Tetouan Morocco to a first trip to the country. The good news is that getting there is simple.

    Entry rules

    Citizens of the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and the EU do not need a visa for tourist stays of up to 90 days. Your passport should be valid for your whole stay, and ideally for six months. Still, always check the latest rules with your own government before you fly.

    Best routing

    There are no direct flights from North America or Australia to Tetouan. Instead, fly to Madrid, London or Paris and take a direct connection to TTU. Alternatively, fly to Casablanca with Royal Air Maroc and ride the Al Boraq train to Tangier. For a full planning checklist, see our guide to planning a trip to Morocco.

    Tetouan beaches: Martil, Cabo Negro and Tamuda Bay

    One big advantage of Tetouan Morocco is that you can mix medina and sea on the same day. Within 15 minutes you are on the sand. Our guide to beaches in Morocco covers the rest of the coast.

    Martil, the city’s own beach

    Martil lies about 10 km away and has a long, lively seafront promenade. In July and August it fills with Moroccan families and Moroccans visiting from Europe. Outside peak season, however, it is calm and perfect for a seafood lunch facing the water.

    Cabo Negro and M’diq

    Cabo Negro offers coves, green hills and holiday homes. M’diq keeps its working fishing port, with colourful boats and fresh fish at fair prices. Moreover, its promenade is ideal for a slow afternoon walk.

    Tamuda Bay, the luxury coast

    Between M’diq and Fnideq stretches Tamuda Bay, about 15 km of fine sand lined with upscale resorts such as the Royal Mansour. It is the right choice if you want a pool, a spa and five-star service. Besides, prices drop sharply outside July and August.

    Day trips from Tetouan

    Tetouan Morocco also makes an excellent base for the north. These are the three trips we recommend most.

    Chefchaouen, the blue city

    This is the classic excursion. In just over an hour, you swap white walls for blue ones. A day trip works, although staying the night is much better. Read everything in our guide to Chefchaouen, Morocco.

    Oued Laou and the Rif coast

    To the east, the coastal road hugs the cliffs as far as Oued Laou, about 45 km away. It is a quiet fishing town with a wide beach and few foreign visitors. Moreover, in summer it fills with Moroccan families on holiday. The drive itself also has beautiful viewpoints.

    Tangier and Asilah

    If you arrive via the Strait, it makes sense to combine Tetouan with Tangier and the Atlantic coast. Asilah, with its murals and Portuguese ramparts, is about 90 minutes away. Our guide to Asilah, Morocco helps you plan that stop.

    What to eat in Tetouan

    The food of Tetouan Morocco is Andalusian, Mediterranean and mountain cooking all at once. That is why it tastes different from Marrakech.

    Dishes and flavours to try

    • Fried and grilled fish at the harbours of M’diq and Martil.
    • Pastilla with chicken or seafood, sweet and savoury at the same time.
    • Bissara, a dried fava bean soup with cumin and olive oil, best in winter.
    • Fresh goat’s cheese from Jebala farmers, eaten with wood-fired bread.
    • Andalusian pastries made with almonds, honey and orange blossom water.
    • Sfenj, fresh doughnuts for breakfast with mint tea.

    For more ideas, see our list of traditional Moroccan dishes. Keep in mind that the medina serves almost no alcohol. You will find it in some hotels and restaurants in the Ensanche or on the coast, as we explain in alcohol in Morocco.

    Where to stay in Tetouan

    Tetouan Morocco offers three very different styles of stay. Choose according to your pace and budget.

    A riad in the medina

    This is the most authentic option. You sleep in a tiled courtyard house and have breakfast on the roof. Some riads can only be reached on foot, though, so pack light.

    A hotel in the Ensanche

    This is the most practical choice if you arrive by car or late at night. Restaurants, cafés and taxis are at the door, and the medina is a ten-minute walk away. Besides, parking is much easier than near the medina.

    A resort on the coast

    If you travel in summer or with children, stay in Cabo Negro, M’diq or Tamuda Bay. Then drive up to Tetouan in about 20 minutes for the cultural sights.

    Tetouan weather and the best time to visit

    Tetouan Morocco has a Mediterranean climate. Winters are mild but wet, with daytime highs around 16°C (61°F). Summers are warm and humid, with highs near 30°C (86°F). Therefore, spring and autumn are the best seasons.

    Month by month

    • March to June: green hills, pleasant temperatures and few crowds.
    • July and August: heat, busy beaches and higher prices on the coast.
    • September and October: warm sea, the film festival and a lively cultural scene.
    • November to February: frequent rain, but a medina without tourists.

    Time difference with the UK and the US

    Since 20 September 2026, Morocco has stayed on GMT all year. As a result, the UK is one hour ahead of Morocco until 25 October, and then both share the same time. New York is 4 hours behind Morocco until 1 November and 5 hours behind after that. For the rest of the country, see the best time to visit Morocco.

    Practical tips for visiting Tetouan Morocco

    Language

    Because of the Protectorate and the nearby Spanish border, many people speak Spanish, especially in shops and cafés. French is common too, while English is growing among young people. Still, a simple salam alaikum and shukran go a long way. Learn more in our guide to the language spoken in Morocco.

    What to wear

    Tetouan is more conservative than Tangier. For that reason, covering shoulders and knees in the medina is a good idea. If you are unsure what to pack, follow our guide on what to wear in Morocco. Also, respect prayer times, and note that non-Muslims cannot enter mosques.

    Safety

    Tetouan is a calm city for visitors. Normal precautions are enough: watch your wallet in busy souks and politely decline unofficial guides. At night, the medina empties early, especially now that it gets dark sooner. For the wider picture, read our honest answer to is Morocco safe.

    Getting around

    The centre of Tetouan Morocco is easy to explore on foot, since the medina, the Mellah and the Ensanche sit side by side. For Martil or the bus station, however, use the small city taxis. Always ask the driver to switch on the meter. Fares also rise a little at night, which is normal across the country.

    Cash and cards

    In the medina, almost everything is paid in cash. By contrast, hotels and many restaurants in the Ensanche accept cards. You will find ATMs on Avenue Mohammed V and near Place Hassan II. So keep some small notes for taxis, tips and pastries.

    Photos and respect

    The medina is very photogenic, but people live and work here. So always ask before photographing someone, especially the Rif women in the markets. Likewise, avoid pointing your camera at the Royal Palace or its guards. Doors, fountains and rooftops, on the other hand, make perfect subjects.

    Try a local hammam

    After a day of walking, a traditional bathhouse is the best way to relax. Neighbourhood hammams in Tetouan Morocco are cheap and very local, while riads and resorts offer a gentler private version. Bring a change of clothes and a small towel. To know what to expect, read our guide to the Moroccan hammam.

    Fridays and Ramadan

    On Friday around midday, many shops close for prayers. During Ramadan, by contrast, the city sleeps by day and comes alive after sunset. It is a beautiful time to visit if you plan ahead, so do your shopping in the morning.

    Tetouan Morocco FAQ

    Is Tetouan worth visiting?

    Yes. Tetouan has one of the most authentic medinas in Morocco, little tourist pressure and the sea just 10 km away. In 2026 it is also a Mediterranean Capital of Culture, with events all year.

    What is Tetouan known for?

    It is known for its UNESCO-listed Andalusian medina, its Spanish colonial quarter, its crafts school and its nickname, the White Dove.

    What are the best things to do in Tetouan?

    Walk the medina and its souks, visit the School of Arts and Crafts, see the Ethnographic Museum, stroll the Ensanche and watch the sunset from a rooftop.

    How far is Tetouan from Tangier?

    About 60 km. By CTM bus, shared grand taxi or private transfer, the trip takes around one hour.

    How do you get from Tetouan to Chefchaouen?

    By CTM bus, shared taxi or private car. The 60 km mountain road takes 1 hr 15 min to 1 hr 30 min.

    Does Tetouan have an airport?

    Yes. Tetouan Sania Ramel (TTU) has direct flights to London Gatwick, Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam, Madrid, Barcelona and Malaga.

    Is Tetouan safe for tourists?

    Yes. It is a calm city. Normal precautions in crowded souks and saying no to unofficial guides are usually enough.

    Is there a beach in Tetouan?

    The city itself is inland, but Martil beach is only 10 km away. Cabo Negro, M’diq and Tamuda Bay are within 20 minutes.

    Was Tetouan part of Spain?

    No. It was the capital of the Spanish Protectorate in northern Morocco from 1913 to 1956, but sovereignty stayed with the Moroccan sultan.

    Do people speak English in Tetouan?

    Some do, mostly younger people and hotel staff. Spanish and French are far more widespread, especially among shopkeepers.

    What is the best time to visit Tetouan?

    From March to June and in September and October. Summer beaches are crowded, and winter is often rainy.

    Is there a train to Tetouan?

    No. The nearest station is Tangier, linked to Rabat and Casablanca by the Al Boraq high-speed train. So the easiest plan is train plus road.
