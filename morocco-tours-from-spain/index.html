    Morocco Tours from Spain: The 2026 Guide to Crossing from Madrid, Barcelona and Andalusia

    Morocco tours from Spain: flights and ferries from Madrid, Barcelona and Tarifa, desert routes, 2026 prices and licensed agencies.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 30 September 2026

    Quick answer: Morocco tours from Spain start with a two-hour flight from Madrid or Barcelona, or a one-hour ferry from Tarifa to Tangier. Most tours then use a private vehicle and driver-guide to link the imperial cities with the Sahara. A private 8-day tour costs €750–1,100 per person (two sharing). Also, UK, US, Canadian and Australian travellers need no visa for stays of up to 90 days.

    Every year, thousands of travellers who are already in Spain look across the Strait of Gibraltar and wonder how to add Morocco. That makes sense, since Tangier sits only 14 kilometres from the Spanish coast. However, Morocco itself is large, the medinas are confusing, and the desert needs a well-prepared vehicle. In short, that is exactly the gap that Morocco tours from Spain fill.

    This guide comes from the local team at Favorites Morocco Tours, with bases in Marrakech, Fes, Casablanca and Tangier. Above all, it explains how to cross from Madrid, Barcelona or Andalusia, which routes work, what they cost and how to spot a reliable operator. If you are still at the idea stage, start with our complete trip to Morocco planning guide and come back when you are ready to book.

    Morocco tours from Spain: the 30-second summary

    • Morocco welcomed a record 19.8 million visitors in 2025 and 14.1 million between January and August 2026 (+4.5%), according to the Ministry of Tourism.
    • Madrid and Barcelona have direct flights to Marrakech of about two hours.
    • The Tarifa–Tangier Ville ferry takes about 60 minutes.
    • UK passports must be valid for at least three months after arrival; a six-month buffer is safer for everyone.
    • From 20 September 2026, Morocco runs on GMT all year.
    • The most popular desert routes run from Marrakech or Fes to the Merzouga dunes.
    • Days spent in Morocco do not count towards your Schengen 90-day allowance.
    • Moroccan travel agencies must hold a Ministry of Tourism licence under Law 11-16.

    What Morocco tours from Spain actually include

    Generally speaking, a packaged tour bundles transport, accommodation and sightseeing into one price. In Morocco, however, it usually means a private vehicle and a driver who stays with you from city to city. As a result, most Morocco tours from Spain feel closer to a tailor-made trip than to a 50-seat coach tour.

    What is normally included

    In general, Morocco tours from Spain booked with a local operator include the following:

    • Pick-up at the airport or at Tangier port.
    • A private air-conditioned vehicle (4×4 or minivan) and fuel.
    • A driver-guide who speaks English, Spanish or French.
    • Hotels, riads and one night in a desert camp.
    • Breakfasts and several dinners on the road.
    • A camel ride across the dunes.
    • Official local guides in Fes and Marrakech.

    What is usually extra

    By contrast, your flight from Spain is rarely included when you book with a Moroccan agency. Similarly, lunches, monument tickets and tips are usually extra. Therefore, always ask for the inclusions in writing. That way, there are no surprises on day one.

    The 2026 context for Morocco tours from Spain

    Before you book, it helps to understand the moment. In particular, three facts change real decisions this year.

    Record demand from Europe

    First, Morocco closed 2025 with 19.8 million visitors, a national record. In addition, France and Spain are its two largest source markets, and Spanish holidaymakers alone number more than four million a year. Consequently, the best riads fill up fast around Easter, the October long weekends and Christmas. For those dates, book Morocco tours from Spain two to three months ahead.

    The 2026 Ceuta crisis and your crossing

    Second, in late July 2026, a mass entry of people into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta triggered a diplomatic dispute between Madrid and Rabat. Then, in August, the US State Department raised its advisory for Ceuta to Level 3. Morocco itself, however, stays at Level 2, “Exercise Increased Caution”, the same level as Spain and France.

    In practice, the Strait ferries kept running normally. Spain’s summer crossing operation closed on 15 September with 1,687,957 passengers heading to North Africa, 3.9% fewer than in 2025. In fact, the Tarifa–Tangier line alone carried 331,075 passengers. So our advice is simple: while tensions last, avoid the Ceuta land border and enter through Tangier instead. For the full picture, read our guide to whether Morocco is safe.

    Morocco’s new time zone

    Finally, on the night of 19–20 September 2026, Moroccan clocks go back one hour and the country returns to GMT for good. As a result, Morocco will be two hours behind mainland Spain until 25 October and one hour behind in winter. Against London, it is one hour behind until late October and on the same time in winter.

    Because it gets dark earlier on the clock, plan afternoon visits sooner. You can check the current time on our Morocco time page.

    That said, schedules change every season, so always confirm on a flight search engine before you fix dates. Airlines, airports and fare tips are covered in our guide to flights to Morocco.

    By ferry across the Strait

    However, the ferry is the most memorable way to arrive. First Tarifa fades behind you, and an hour later the white medina of Tangier appears. Here are the useful routes:

    • Tarifa–Tangier Ville: about 60 minutes. FRS and Baleària run several daily sailings. You land beside the medina. A one-way foot passenger ticket usually costs €37–70, depending on the season.
    • Algeciras–Tanger Med: 90 minutes to two hours. It suits drivers best, although the port lies 45–50 km from Tangier.
    • Barcelona–Tanger Med: 28 to 30 hours with GNV, about three sailings a week. It only makes sense with your own car.

    A practical warning: when the easterly Levante wind blows hard, Tarifa cancels before Algeciras. For that reason, leave half a day of slack if you connect with a tour. If you are staying in Gibraltar, the short drive to Algeciras or Tarifa gives you the most sailings.

    From Tangier port by train

    If you arrive on foot, the train is a great ally. Then, from Tangier, the Al Boraq high-speed line reaches Rabat in 1 h 20 and Casablanca in 2 h 10. After that, the conventional network continues to Marrakech and Fes. You can check timetables on the website of ONCF, Morocco’s national railway. Even so, many Morocco tours from Spain simply collect you at the port and continue by road to Chefchaouen.

    Driving across with a hire car

    Taking your own car across is legal and common. Hire cars are a different story, because most Spanish rental contracts do not allow the vehicle to leave Spain. For that reason, check the small print before you book. Even so, in most cases, it is easier to cross on foot and let a local driver take over in Tangier.

    Border formalities on the Spanish side

    Also, non-EU travellers should remember the Schengen rules. The EU Entry/Exit System has been fully operational since 10 April 2026, so your biometrics are recorded when you leave and re-enter Spain. As a result, allow extra time at Tarifa and Algeciras. In addition, the ETIAS travel authorisation is expected to launch in the last quarter of 2026, with a transition period. Check its status before your trip.

    How to choose a Morocco travel agency

    Indeed, choosing the right operator matters more than choosing the hotel. After all, a good agency fixes problems in minutes. A poor one, on the other hand, turns a delay into a lost day.

    Check the official licence

    Moroccan travel agencies are governed by Law 11-16 and its implementing decree 2.21.80. Specifically, the law sets out two licences: travel organiser-distributor (ODV) and travel distributor (DV). In short, only a licensed agency can legally sell Morocco tours from Spain or anywhere else. Moreover, the Ministry of Tourism publishes an official directory of travel agencies where you can check the name.

    Insist on official guides in the medinas

    Similarly, the tour guide profession is regulated by Law 05-12. An official guide therefore carries a visible professional card. Of course, drivers can explain the route, but the guided visit of Fes or Marrakech belongs to an accredited local guide.

    Eight questions to ask before you pay

    Before booking Morocco tours from Spain, send these questions by email:

    1. What is the agency’s licence number?
    2. Is the tour private or shared?
    3. Which languages does the driver speak?
    4. Which vehicle model will you use, and how old is it?
    5. Are the hotels confirmed by name?
    6. Is the desert camp standard, or luxury with a private bathroom?
    7. Which meals are included?
    8. How do cancellations and refunds work?

    Red flags

    • Prices far below the market with no explanation.
    • Full payment upfront in cash or to a personal bank account.
    • No written itinerary and no hotel list.

    Morocco tours from Spain for families, older travellers and solo visitors

Finally, solo visitors often join a shared tour to cut costs. In every case, tell the agency early, because it changes the vehicle, the pace and the hotels.

    Money and tipping

    First of all, the currency is the Moroccan dirham. Currently, in September 2026, one euro buys about 10.9–11 dirhams. Note that you cannot take more than 2,000 dirhams in banknotes out of the country. As a tipping guide, allow 100–150 MAD a day for your driver-guide, 50–100 MAD for a local guide and 20–50 MAD for hotel staff. Everything else is in our Morocco currency guide.

    Also, cards work in most hotels and larger shops. In the souks, however, cash is king. Also note that Moroccan ATMs often charge 20–40 MAD per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often.

    Moroccan food on the road

    Moroccan cuisine is another great reason to go, because it blends Amazigh roots, Arab spices, Andalusi technique and Mediterranean produce. In addition, UNESCO inscribed the knowledge and practices of couscous, shared by Morocco and its Maghreb neighbours, as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2020.

    Seven Moroccan dishes you should not miss

    • Tagine: the most famous Moroccan dish. Meat or chicken slow-cooked with vegetables, prunes or preserved lemon.
    • Couscous: traditionally served on Fridays, with the family.
    • Pastilla: thin pastry filled with pigeon or chicken, almonds and cinnamon. On the coast, cooks make it with seafood.
    • Harira: tomato, lentil and chickpea soup that breaks the fast in Ramadan.
    • Tanjia: meat slow-cooked for hours in the embers of the hammam furnace, a Marrakech speciality.
    • Rfissa: shredded msemen with chicken, lentils and fenugreek.
    • Mechoui: whole roast lamb, served at celebrations.

    To go further, read our guide to cuisine in Morocco and our dish-by-dish list of traditional Moroccan dishes.

    Where you eat best on tour

    In fact, on most tours, the best meals happen outside the cities. For instance, a tagine in a Dades valley kasbah, or a Berber pizza, medfouna, in Merzouga. Likewise, the evening food stalls on Jemaa el-Fnaa are an experience in themselves. Our restaurant Marrakech guide helps you choose.

    Eating with peace of mind

    • Drink sealed bottled water.
    • Choose stalls with a fast turnover of local customers.
    • Alcohol is served only in licensed venues.
    • During Ramadan, many local restaurants open at sunset.

    Entry requirements for Morocco

    Morocco’s entry rules are simple for most Western visitors, whether you travel independently or on Morocco tours from Spain. Even so, one document mistake can leave you at the port.

    Do you need a visa for Morocco?

    For example, citizens of the UK, the US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland and EU countries can visit Morocco without a visa for up to 90 days. Nationals of other countries should check with the nearest Moroccan consulate before booking.

    Passport validity

    The UK government states that British passports must be valid for at least three months after the date you arrive. Spain’s foreign ministry, meanwhile, asks its own citizens for more than six months. Therefore, the safest rule for any nationality is six months of validity. Also note that Spanish citizens need a passport too; the Spanish ID card is not accepted.

    Insurance, connectivity and packing

    • Buy travel insurance with medical and cancellation cover.
    • Get a local SIM or an eSIM. We explain how in SIM card in Morocco.
    • Pack clothes that cover shoulders and knees. The full list is in what to wear in Morocco.
    • Learn a few basic words with our guide to the language spoken in Morocco.

    Are Morocco tours from Spain safe?

    Yes. In fact, Morocco is one of the most stable destinations in North Africa. Still, it helps to understand the nuances.

    What the official advisories say

    The US keeps Morocco at Level 2, “Exercise Increased Caution”, like France and Spain. Likewise, the UK Foreign Office does not advise against travel to Morocco. Spain’s foreign ministry recommends travelling with caution, a common level for many countries. As mentioned above, only Spain’s enclave of Ceuta moved to Level 3 in August 2026. For a line-by-line reading, see our article on Morocco travel warnings.

    The real risks

    • Fake guides in the medinas who offer “help” and then ask for money.
    • Petty theft in crowded areas.
    • High commissions in shops you are taken to without asking.

    Fortunately, a guided tour removes most of these risks. On one hand, the driver knows the roads; on the other, the official guide keeps middlemen away. For emergencies, dial 19 (police in cities), 177 (Royal Gendarmerie) or 15 (civil protection and ambulance).

    Women travellers

    In fact, many women travel alone or in groups in Morocco without problems. Moreover, Law 103-13, in force since 2018, penalises street harassment. Even so, a trusted driver-guide adds peace of mind in the medinas and on long drives.

    Morocco tours from Spain: frequently asked questions

    What do Morocco tours from Spain include?

    They usually include a private vehicle with a driver-guide, hotels or riads, one night in a desert camp, breakfasts, several dinners and a camel ride. However, flights, lunches and tips are normally extra.

    How much do one-week Morocco tours from Spain cost?

    A private 7- or 8-day mid-range tour costs €750–1,100 per person, based on two sharing and without flights. With flights from Madrid or Barcelona, a couple spends about €1,400–1,900 in total.

    Can you visit Morocco as a day trip from Spain?

    Yes. Day trips from Tarifa or the Costa del Sol reach Tangier in about an hour by ferry. However, two or more nights give a far better picture of the country.

    How long is the ferry from Tarifa to Tangier?

    About 60 minutes, arriving at Tangier Ville next to the medina. By contrast, Algeciras to Tanger Med takes between 90 minutes and two hours.

    Do I need a visa for Morocco if I am American or British?

    No. US and UK citizens can stay up to 90 days without a visa. However, British passports must be valid for at least three months after arrival.

    Is Morocco safe to visit in 2026?

    Yes, for normal tourist travel. For example, the US keeps Morocco at Level 2 and the UK does not advise against travel. After the 2026 Ceuta crisis, enter through Tangier by plane or ferry.

    Can Morocco tours from Spain be done in four days?

    Yes. In general, the most balanced option is to fly to Marrakech and join a 3-day desert tour to Merzouga. Alternatively, cross by ferry and combine Tangier, Chefchaouen and Tetouan.

    Which desert route is best on Morocco tours from Spain?

    Marrakech to Merzouga in three or four days. It also crosses the Tizi n’Tichka pass, Ait Ben Haddou and the Dades and Todra gorges, and ends at the Erg Chebbi dunes.

    How can I check that a Moroccan travel agency is legal?

    First, ask for its licence number and check it in the Ministry of Tourism’s official directory. Agencies operate under Law 11-16 with ODV or DV licences.

    Does time in Morocco count towards my Schengen 90 days?

    No. Morocco is outside the Schengen area, so days spent there do not count towards the 90-in-180-day limit.

    Which city is the best starting point for Morocco tours from Spain?

    It depends on your route. Overall, Madrid and Barcelona suit Marrakech-based tours thanks to frequent direct flights. Seville, Malaga and Tarifa suit northern routes, because the ferry to Tangier leaves from the Strait. Barcelona also offers direct flights to Fes and Tangier.

    Is there a combined Spain and Morocco tour?

    Yes. Typically, the most common combination links Seville or Malaga with Tarifa, the ferry to Tangier, Chefchaouen, Fes, the desert and Marrakech in about ten days.

    Final thoughts

    In the end, Morocco tours from Spain are the most efficient way to discover a large, varied country that sits right next to Europe. After all, a two-hour flight or a one-hour ferry takes you from routine to medinas, mountains and dunes. So choose a licensed agency, get everything in writing and respect the driving times.
