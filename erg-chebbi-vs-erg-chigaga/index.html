    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: Which Moroccan Sahara Dunes Should You Choose?

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: which Sahara dunes in Morocco should you choose? Compare access, dunes, camps, prices and the best route.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 30 September 2026

    Almost everyone planning a Sahara trip in Morocco eventually hits the same fork in the road: Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga. Both are true sand seas, both deliver towering dunes and silent nights, and both sit at the end of a long drive from Marrakech. Yet they offer noticeably different experiences, and choosing badly can cost you a day of driving or leave you disappointed by crowds.

    This guide compares them honestly. You will also find real driving times, dune heights, camp standards, prices, crowd levels and seasonal notes, followed by a straightforward recommendation for each type of traveller. By the end, the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga question should answer itself.

    The Short Answer

    If your time is limited, if you are travelling with children or older relatives, or if you want a paved road right up to the sand, choose Erg Chebbi near Merzouga.

    If you want isolation, far fewer camps, a genuine off-road journey and a stronger sense of being nowhere at all, choose Erg Chigaga near M’Hamid.

    Neither is a compromise. Erg Chebbi is the accessible classic; Erg Chigaga is the adventure. The rest of this article explains exactly where the differences lie.

    First, What Is an Erg?

    Before comparing Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga in detail, it helps to know the term. An erg is a sand sea: a large desert area where wind has piled loose sand into dunes over thousands of years. In fact, most of the Sahara is actually rocky hamada or gravel plain, and true ergs cover only a fraction of it. In short, that scarcity is exactly why travellers cross Morocco to reach these two.

    Morocco has several ergs, but only two are practical for visitors: Erg Chebbi in the east near the Algerian border, and Erg Chigaga in the south beyond the Draa Valley. Everything else is either too remote, too small or inside restricted zones.

    Why Agafay does not count

    Marketing sometimes calls the Agafay area near Marrakech a desert. Agafay is genuinely beautiful, yet it is a stony, lunar landscape with no dunes at all. It works well for a sunset dinner half an hour from the city, but it is not part of the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga conversation.

    Erg Chebbi in Detail

    Erg Chebbi is the dune field most people picture when they think of Morocco. It rises abruptly from a flat plain outside Merzouga, and the transition is startling: one moment you are on hard gravel, the next you face a wall of orange sand 150 metres high.

    Location and access

    The erg sits in south-eastern Morocco, close to the Algerian border and roughly 50 kilometres from the town of Rissani. Crucially, a sealed road runs right to the western edge, so a standard car reaches the dunes without difficulty. Hotels and camps line that edge, meaning you can be sipping tea with a dune view fifteen minutes after arriving.

    The landscape

    Overall, Erg Chebbi is compact and dramatic. Moreover, its dunes are steep, brilliantly coloured and beautifully sculpted, and the highest ridges give sweeping views across the plain. Because the field is narrow, you can walk from one side to the other in a few hours.

    What there is to do

    • Camel trekking at sunrise or sunset, typically one to two hours.
    • Sandboarding down the western slopes.
    • Quad biking and buggies on the plain, though not on the dunes themselves.
    • Khamlia village, where Gnawa musicians perform daily.
    • Dayet Srji, a seasonal lake that attracts flamingos after rain.
    • Rissani market, held on Sundays, Tuesdays and Thursdays.
    • Fossil workshops around Erfoud, where marine fossils are cut and polished.
    • Nomad family visits arranged through local guides.

    The trade-off: popularity

    Erg Chebbi’s accessibility is also its weakness. Well over a hundred camps operate around the dunes, and sunset ridges can hold several camel caravans at once. In high season you will hear other camps’ drumming across the sand. If solitude is your priority, this matters.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: Erg Chigaga in Detail

    By contrast, Erg Chigaga is bigger, wilder and considerably harder to reach. It lies south-west of the Draa Valley, beyond the last town on the road, and no sealed surface goes anywhere near it.

    Location and access

    The nearest settlement is M’Hamid El Ghizlane, where the tarmac simply ends. From there, a 4×4 then covers 50 to 60 kilometres of piste in about two hours, crossing gravel plains, dry riverbeds and scattered small dunes. An alternative route runs from Foum Zguid to the west and takes roughly three hours.

    Still, that journey is part of the appeal. You arrive having genuinely travelled, and the camps sit far enough apart that most of them see nothing but sand in every direction.

    The landscape

    In addition, Erg Chigaga covers a much larger area than its eastern rival, and its tallest dunes reach around 300 metres. As a result, the field is broader and more varied, with long ridgelines, wide sandy corridors and stretches of black volcanic rock at the margins. Because of the scale, climbing the main ridge for sunrise feels like standing on the roof of the desert.

    What there is to do

    • Camel trekking across empty dune corridors.
    • Sandboarding on very long, unbroken slopes.
    • 4×4 dune driving on the approach and departure routes.
    • Lake Iriqui National Park, a dry lakebed that occasionally floods and draws birdlife.
    • Nomad encampments, still genuinely inhabited in this region.
    • Stargazing, with essentially zero light pollution.
    • Oasis stops along the Draa Valley on the drive in.

    The trade-off: time and cost

    Reaching Erg Chigaga costs a full extra half-day and requires a 4×4 with a driver. Consequently, a two-night trip there is more expensive than the equivalent at Erg Chebbi, and it does not suit travellers who dislike rough roads.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: Getting There

    Travel time drives most decisions, so this section deserves careful attention. In any case, the numbers below assume normal traffic and reasonable rest stops.

    From Marrakech

    RouteDriving timeNotes
    Marrakech → Erg Chebbi9–10 hoursOver the Tizi n’Tichka pass, via Ait Ben Haddou, Ouarzazate, Dades and Todra
    Marrakech → Erg Chigaga8–9 hours to M’Hamid, then 2 hours pisteVia Ouarzazate, Agdz and the Draa Valley

    Above all, nobody should attempt either as a day trip. In practice, two nights is the realistic minimum, and three nights makes the journey genuinely enjoyable rather than a marathon.

    From Fes

    Erg Chebbi wins decisively here. The drive takes seven to eight hours through the Middle Atlas, Ifrane and the Ziz Valley. Erg Chigaga, by contrast, requires eleven to twelve hours, which makes little sense unless you are already heading south.

    Flying and other shortcuts

    Alternatively, Errachidia airport lies about two hours from Merzouga and receives domestic flights from Casablanca. Zagora also has a small airport with limited services, roughly two hours from M’Hamid. Flying trims a long drive, but schedules are sparse, so confirm before building an itinerary around them.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: Crowds and Atmosphere

    This is where the two diverge most sharply, and for many travellers it decides the question outright.

    At Erg Chebbi

    Expect company. For example, camel caravans leave the same access points at similar times, sunset ridges attract dozens of people, and the sound of drumming carries between camps. The upside is energy and easy logistics; the downside is that your photographs may include other travellers.

    At Erg Chigaga

    Expect quiet. By contrast, camps are separated by kilometres of sand, and it is entirely normal to see no one else all evening. As a result, silence at night is total, and the Milky Way is startlingly bright. For some visitors that isolation is magical; for others it feels remote and a little exposed.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: Desert Camps

    Camp quality varies enormously in both locations, so read descriptions carefully rather than assuming a category.

    Standard camps

    Basic tents, shared bathrooms or simple facilities, mattresses on rugs, and dinner around a fire. Still, these are perfectly comfortable for one night and cost the least. Both ergs have plenty.

    Comfort and deluxe camps

    Private tents with real beds, en-suite bathrooms with running water, solar power and proper dining tents. In fact, this is the most common choice for couples and families. However, Erg Chebbi has far more of them.

    Luxury camps

    Rugs, brass lanterns, hot showers, full menus and occasionally a pool. Erg Chigaga has several outstanding examples, precisely because operators there compete on quality rather than volume.

    What to ask before booking

    • How far is the camp from the nearest road or access point?
    • Is there a private bathroom, and does the shower have hot water?
    • How many tents are on site, and how many other camps are visible?
    • Are blankets and heating provided in winter?
    • Is the camel trek included, and how long does it last?
    • Does the price include dinner, breakfast and transfers?

    Where the money goes

    In other words, at Erg Chebbi, you pay mainly for the camp. At Erg Chigaga, roughly a third of your budget covers transport, because a driver and vehicle are dedicated to you for the piste crossing. Groups therefore get much better value at Chigaga, since the 4×4 cost is shared.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga for Photography

    Photographers usually lean one way, and it is worth understanding why.

    Why Erg Chebbi photographs well

    The dunes are steep, the sand is a vivid orange, and the field rises straight out of a flat plain, which creates strong graphic separation. For example, sunrise from the western side gives clean, ridged shapes. However, footprints accumulate quickly near the camps.

    Why Erg Chigaga photographs better for landscapes

    Scale is the difference. Wide corridors, unbroken ridgelines and virtually no footprints let you frame pure sand to the horizon. Night photography is exceptional too, because there is no light pollution whatsoever. On the other hand, the sand is slightly paler, so colours are less saturated at sunset.

    Practical camera notes

    • Change lenses inside a bag or a tent, never on an open dune.
    • Bring more batteries than you think, since cold nights drain them.
    • Use a lens cloth and a blower daily; fine sand gets everywhere.
    • Walk wide around camp before shooting, so your frame stays clean.
    • Blue hour lasts longer than you expect at these latitudes.

    Families, Accessibility and Comfort

    Travelling with children

    In that case, Erg Chebbi is the sensible choice. The drive is long but ends on tarmac, camps sit close to hotels with pools, and you can leave quickly if a child becomes unwell. Erg Chigaga’s two-hour off-road crossing is uncomfortable for toddlers and stressful for parents.

    Travelling with older relatives

    Again, Erg Chebbi. However, several camps are reachable by 4×4 directly, avoiding a camel ride entirely, and medical help in Rissani or Erfoud is far closer.

    Motion sickness and rough roads

    The piste to Erg Chigaga is genuinely bumpy. Therefore, anyone prone to car sickness should take medication beforehand, sit in the front, and ask the driver to keep a steadier pace.

    Heat and cold

    Summer daytime temperatures exceed 45 °C at both ergs. By contrast, winter nights fall close to freezing. Camps provide heavy blankets, but a warm layer and a hat make a large difference between December and February.

    A note on sandstorms

    The chergui wind blows most often in spring and can reduce visibility for a day or two. It affects both ergs equally. Camps rarely close, though camel treks may be shortened, so keep your itinerary slightly flexible.

    What a Night in a Desert Camp Is Actually Like

    Descriptions online tend toward the poetic, so here is the practical version. You arrive in late afternoon, either by camel or by 4×4. Next, staff show you to a tent and serve mint tea. Before sunset, you climb the nearest dune, which takes far longer than it looks because sand absorbs every step.

    Dinner is usually soup, a tagine, bread and fruit, served around eight. Afterwards, someone lights a fire and plays drums. By ten the sky is spectacular, and by eleven most guests are asleep, because there is nothing else to do and that is precisely the point.

    Wake-up comes early, often before six, so you can watch sunrise from a ridge. Breakfast follows, then you leave. The whole experience lasts under twenty hours, which is why many travellers choose two nights rather than one.

    What you will not find

    • Reliable Wi-Fi, particularly at Erg Chigaga
    • Alcohol, unless you bring it and the camp permits it
    • Charging points beyond a shared solar panel at basic camps
    • Hot water at the cheapest camps
    • Any light at all outside the camp perimeter

    Camel Trekking: The Honest Version

    Camel treks appear in almost every Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga itinerary, and they are memorable. They are also less comfortable than photographs suggest, so set expectations correctly.

    What it feels like

    In practice, dromedaries walk with a rolling gait that puts pressure on your hips and lower back. One hour is pleasant, ninety minutes is fine for most people, and anything beyond two hours becomes genuinely tiring. Also, wear long trousers to avoid chafing, and keep a scarf handy for wind.

    Alternatives if you would rather not ride

    Every reputable operator at both ergs can transfer you by 4×4 instead. You will still reach the camp, still climb the dunes and still see sunrise. Say so when booking rather than on the day.

    Animal welfare

    Standards vary. Above all, look for animals with good body condition, clean padding under the saddle and reasonable group sizes. Refuse a trek if an animal looks distressed, and tell your operator why. Demand shapes behaviour faster than complaints after the fact.

    Can You Visit Both Ergs?

    Yes, although the logistics need care. The two sand seas sit roughly 400 kilometres apart by road, and connecting them means a long transfer through Zagora and the Draa Valley or north through Tazzarine.

    A realistic combined route

    1. First day: Marrakech to Ait Ben Haddou and Ouarzazate, then Dades Gorge.
    2. Second day: Todra Gorge, then on to Merzouga and a night at Erg Chebbi.
    3. Third day: Sunrise in the dunes, then drive west to Zagora.
    4. Fourth day: Draa Valley to M’Hamid, then 4×4 into Erg Chigaga.
    5. Fifth day: Cross the piste to Foum Zguid and return to Marrakech.

    For this reason, five days is the minimum for that loop, and six is more comfortable. Most travellers, however, are better served by choosing one erg and spending two nights there instead of rushing both.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga Itineraries

    Three days, Marrakech to Erg Chebbi and back

    First day: Tizi n’Tichka pass, Ait Ben Haddou, Ouarzazate, overnight in Dades Gorge.
    Second day: Todra Gorge, Ziz Valley, arrive Merzouga, camel trek, camp at Erg Chebbi.
    Third day: Sunrise, breakfast, long return drive to Marrakech.

    This is the most popular Sahara itinerary in Morocco. It works, though day three involves nine hours in a vehicle.

    Two days, Marrakech to Erg Chigaga

    First day: Ouarzazate, Agdz, the Draa Valley palm groves, M’Hamid, then 4×4 to camp.
    Second day: Sunrise on the big ridge, cross the piste to Foum Zguid, return to Marrakech.

    Two days is tight but feasible, because the Foum Zguid exit shortens the return considerably.

    Four days for a calmer pace

    If time allows, add a night in Skoura or the Draa Valley, and spend two nights in the desert rather than one. Consequently, you gain a full day in the dunes instead of a rushed sunrise before departure.

    What to Pack for the Sahara

    • A scarf or shemagh for sun, wind and blowing sand
    • Sunglasses, a wide-brimmed hat and high-factor sunscreen
    • Long, loose trousers, which beat shorts on a camel
    • A warm fleece and thermal layer from November to March
    • Closed shoes for the drive, plus sandals for the sand
    • A head torch, since camps go dark early
    • A power bank, because charging is limited
    • At least two litres of water per person, per day
    • Wet wipes and hand sanitiser
    • Cash in small notes for tips and roadside stops

    What to leave behind

    Also, skip the hard suitcase; a soft bag travels better in a 4×4. Leave valuables at your Marrakech hotel, since camp tents rarely lock. Finally, do not bring a drone unless you have checked current Moroccan regulations, which are restrictive.

    Other Desert Options in Morocco

    Erg Chebbi and Erg Chigaga dominate the conversation, yet a few alternatives deserve a mention.

    Erg Lihoudi

    A small dune field about an hour from M’Hamid, reachable without a serious off-road crossing. It suits travellers who want a desert night without the full Chigaga journey, although the dunes are modest.

    Zagora and Tinfou

    Often sold as a cheap two-day desert trip from Marrakech. However, the dunes near Zagora are small, and many visitors leave disappointed. Choose it only if time is extremely limited.

    Agafay

    Thirty minutes from Marrakech, Agafay offers rocky hills, luxury camps and sunset dinners. In short, it is a lovely evening out and a poor substitute for real dunes.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga vs the rest

    In short, only the two main ergs deliver the classic Sahara experience. If your trip depends on standing among genuinely tall dunes, restrict your shortlist to those two and plan the drive properly.

    The Road South: What You Actually See on the Way

    In the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga comparison, the drive deserves as much attention as the destination. You spend more hours on the road than in the dunes, so the route itself should influence your choice.

    The eastern route to Erg Chebbi

    Leaving Marrakech, the road climbs the Tizi n’Tichka pass at 2,260 metres, with switchbacks and Atlas panoramas. Descending, you reach Ait Ben Haddou, the fortified earthen village used in dozens of films and listed by UNESCO.

    Beyond Ouarzazate, the landscape opens into the Valley of Roses and Skoura’s palm groves. Next, the Dades Gorge follows, famous for its zigzag road and red rock walls. Next comes the Todra Gorge, where cliffs rise 300 metres above a narrow river. Finally the Ziz Valley leads east toward Merzouga through a corridor of date palms.

    The southern route to Erg Chigaga

    This road runs through Agdz and then follows the Draa Valley for roughly 200 kilometres. The Draa is Morocco’s longest river, and its palm groves stretch almost continuously between kasbahs of packed earth.

    After that, Zagora marks the traditional caravan point, complete with the famous sign announcing 52 days to Timbuktu by camel. M’Hamid, the last village, sits where the tarmac stops. Consequently the southern route feels less varied than the eastern one, yet more like an approach to genuine emptiness.

    Which drive is more interesting?

    Most travellers rate the eastern route higher, because it packs in mountains, gorges and a UNESCO village. The southern route wins on atmosphere, since the Draa Valley delivers a slow, steady transition from oasis to open desert.

    Where to Stay Near Each Erg (Besides a Camp)

    You do not have to sleep in a tent. Indeed, both areas have permanent hotels, and combining one hotel night with one camp night works well.

    Around Erg Chebbi

    Merzouga and the neighbouring hamlet of Hassi Labied hold dozens of kasbah-style hotels facing the dunes. Many also have pools, proper bathrooms, air conditioning and terraces looking straight at the sand. Prices range from 300 to 2,000 dirhams per night, and you can walk to the dunes in minutes.

    Around Erg Chigaga

    Options are far thinner. M’Hamid has a handful of simple auberges, and a few larger properties sit along the Draa Valley near Zagora. Because these are an hour or more from the dunes, most visitors sleep at the camp itself rather than commuting.

    A useful hybrid plan

    Arrive in the afternoon, check into a hotel, shower, and rest. Head into the dunes for the camp night, then return to the hotel for a second night on the way back. Therefore, this approach suits families and anyone who dislikes two consecutive nights without plumbing.

    How to Book Without Getting Caught Out

    Desert trips are sold at every price point in Morocco, and the cheapest options cut corners in predictable ways. However, a few checks protect your trip.

    Read what is actually included

    Compare like with like. Some quotes cover only transport and a shared tent, while others include all meals, private bathrooms and a camel trek. A price that looks 40 per cent lower usually reflects a different product.

    Ask about group size

    Shared minibus tours to Erg Chebbi can carry sixteen passengers, which slows every stop. By contrast, private vehicles cost more and save hours across three days.

    Confirm the vehicle for Erg Chigaga

    Moreover, only a proper 4×4 handles the piste safely. If an operator promises to reach Erg Chigaga in a minivan, the camp is almost certainly closer to M’Hamid than advertised.

    Get the camp name in writing

    Camps differ enormously. Insist on the exact name, then look it up. Vague phrases such as “standard desert camp” leave too much room for substitution.

    Check the return timing

    On three-day Erg Chebbi trips, ask what time you arrive back in Marrakech. Nine or ten in the evening is normal, so avoid booking a same-night flight.

    Health and safety in the dunes

    Dehydration is the most common problem, and it arrives faster than people expect because sweat evaporates instantly. Drink steadily rather than waiting for thirst, and add electrolyte sachets in summer.

    Sunburn is the second issue. Reflected light off pale sand catches the underside of the chin and nose, so apply sunscreen more thoroughly than you would at the beach.

    Finally, never walk far from camp alone at night. Besides, dunes look identical in the dark, and footprints vanish in wind. Whichever side of the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga choice you land on, tell someone before you wander off with a camera.

    Responsible Travel in the Dunes

    Both ergs are fragile environments supporting small communities, and visitor numbers have grown quickly. In practice, simple choices make a real difference.

    • Carry your rubbish out, including cigarette ends and tissues.
    • Use water sparingly, since every litre is trucked in.
    • Stay on established tracks rather than driving across open sand.
    • Buy from local cooperatives instead of roadside resellers.
    • Ask before photographing nomad families, and accept a refusal.
    • Choose camps that employ people from the nearest village.

    Common Mistakes People Make

    • Booking a one-night trip from Marrakech to Erg Chebbi. The driving overwhelms the desert time.
    • Assuming Zagora means dunes. It rarely does.
    • Underestimating winter nights. Temperatures near freezing catch people out.
    • Expecting Wi-Fi at Erg Chigaga. Plan to be offline.
    • Wearing shorts on a camel. Chafing ruins the ride.
    • Ignoring the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga distinction when booking. Some listings simply say “Sahara camp”, which tells you nothing.

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: A Five-Point Recap

    Before you book, run through these five points. They settle the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga question for almost everyone.

    1. Time. Erg Chebbi saves you roughly half a day in each direction.
    2. Road. Tarmac reaches Erg Chebbi; Erg Chigaga needs two hours of piste.
    3. Solitude. Erg Chigaga has a fraction of the camps and almost no crowds.
    4. Scale. Erg Chigaga is wider and taller; Erg Chebbi is steeper and more colourful.
    5. Budget. Erg Chebbi is cheaper for couples; Erg Chigaga is competitive for groups.

    Anyone still undecided after that list should default to Erg Chebbi for a first Moroccan Sahara trip, then save Erg Chigaga for a return visit. Indeed, the contrast between the two is reason enough to come back.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: which is better?

    Neither is objectively better. Erg Chebbi is easier to reach, cheaper and more suitable for families and short trips. Erg Chigaga is quieter, wilder and better for photography and solitude, but it requires a 4×4 and extra travel time.

    Which erg has the biggest dunes?

    Erg Chigaga does. In fact, its highest dunes reach roughly 300 metres, compared with about 150 metres at Erg Chebbi. Erg Chebbi’s dunes, however, look more dramatic because they rise straight from a flat plain.

    How far is Erg Chigaga from Erg Chebbi?

    Around 400 kilometres by road, which takes six to seven hours of driving via Zagora or Tazzarine. Therefore, combining both requires at least five days.

    Do you need a 4×4 for Erg Chebbi?

    No. A sealed road reaches the western edge of Erg Chebbi, so a standard car is sufficient. Only Erg Chigaga demands a 4×4.

    How many nights should you spend in the desert?

    Two nights gives a far better experience than one, because you gain a full day among the dunes rather than a single sunset and sunrise.

    Is the Sahara too hot in summer?

    July and August regularly exceed 45 °C, and nights stay warm. Trips still run, but September through May is far more comfortable.

    Are there scorpions and snakes?

    Both exist, though encounters are rare around camps. Shake out shoes in the morning, keep tents zipped, and avoid walking barefoot at night.

    Can you visit the Sahara from Marrakech in one day?

    Not the real dunes. Erg Chebbi is nine to ten hours away and Erg Chigaga is even further off-road. Agafay, thirty minutes from the city, is the only same-day desert-style option.

    Final Verdict on Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga

    Choose Erg Chebbi when logistics matter: a shorter drive, a paved arrival, more camps at more price points and a straightforward route from Fes or Marrakech. It is the right answer for most first-time visitors, and the dunes are genuinely spectacular despite the crowds.

    Choose Erg Chigaga when the journey is part of the appeal: two hours of piste, camps scattered across a sand sea three times wider, silence after dark and a horizon with nothing on it. On the other hand, repeat visitors and photographers rarely regret the extra effort.

    Whichever way the Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga decision falls, give the desert two nights rather than one. The Sahara reveals itself slowly, and the second morning is almost always the one people remember.
