    Attractive Tours in Morocco: Top Attractions and the Best Guided Tours (2026)

    Discover the most attractive tours in Morocco: top attractions, Sahara and imperial city routes, day trips from Marrakech, best seasons and booking tips.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 26 September 2026

    Attractive tours in Morocco combine the country’s best attractions into one smooth trip: the souks of Marrakech, the medina of Fes, the kasbahs of the south and a night under the stars in the Sahara. Yet many travellers struggle to choose. Should you book a day trip or a week-long circuit? Which tours and attractions are really worth your time?

    This guide answers those questions. It lists Morocco’s top attractions, explains which guided tours include them and helps you pick the right route for your budget and travel style. It was written in September 2026 by the local team at Favorites Morocco Tours, who plan private trips across the country all year round.

    Quick answer: what are the most attractive tours in Morocco?

    The most attractive tours in Morocco are the Marrakech to Sahara desert circuits (3 to 7 days), the imperial cities tours linking Fes, Meknes and Rabat, and the full-country loops of 10 to 15 days. For short stays, the best day trips from Marrakech go to Essaouira, the Ourika Valley, Ait Ben Haddou and the Ouzoud Waterfalls. Private guided tours are the easiest way to see several attractions in one trip.

    What makes a tour “attractive”?

    An attractive tour is not just a list of famous places. It is a route that connects the right attractions in the right order, without long empty hours on the road. It also leaves time to breathe, eat well and meet people.

    From our experience, the best tours and attractions share five qualities:

    • Variety: cities, mountains, desert and coast in one trip.
    • Logical routing: no zig-zags and no unnecessary backtracking.
    • Local guides: people who explain the history, not just the directions.
    • Good stops: kasbahs, viewpoints and villages between big destinations.
    • Quality nights: charming riads and a real desert camp.

    In addition, attractive tours are flexible. You should be able to add a day in Fes, skip a museum or stop for photos whenever you want. That is why many visitors now prefer private tours over fixed group departures. Our comparison of private vs group tours in Morocco explains the differences in detail.

    Top attractions in Morocco you should include in your tour

    Before choosing a route, it helps to know the main attractions. Morocco has nine sites on the UNESCO World Heritage List, plus mountains, gorges and dunes that no list can capture. Here are the highlights most travellers want to see. You can check the full list on the official UNESCO page for Morocco and find practical information on Visit Morocco, the national tourism board.

    Of course, no single trip can include everything. The trick is to choose a handful of attractions that match your interests, then build the route around them. A good local agency will tell you honestly what fits into your days and what does not. That is the first step towards truly attractive tours.

    1. Jemaa el-Fna and the Marrakech medina

    Jemaa el-Fna is the beating heart of Marrakech. By day, it hosts orange-juice sellers and henna artists. By night, it fills with food stalls, musicians and storytellers. UNESCO recognised the square’s oral traditions in 2001, and the medina itself has been a World Heritage Site since 1985. Our complete Marrakech travel guide covers the square, the souks and the palaces.

    2. Jardin Majorelle and the YSL Museum

    This cobalt-blue garden was created by the French painter Jacques Majorelle and later restored by Yves Saint Laurent and Pierre Bergé. Next door, the Musée Yves Saint Laurent opened in 2017. Arrive early, because queues grow quickly after 10 am.

    3. The medina of Fes el-Bali

    Fes el-Bali is one of the largest car-free urban areas in the world and a UNESCO site since 1981. Inside its walls, you will find the Chouara tannery, medieval madrasas and the Al Quaraouiyine, founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri. Read our Fes travel guide and our list of things to do in Fez before you go.

    4. The Erg Chebbi dunes in Merzouga

    For many travellers, the Sahara is the main reason to visit Morocco. The golden dunes of Erg Chebbi rise near the village of Merzouga, and some reach about 150 metres. A camel ride at sunset and a night in a desert camp are the most attractive experience of any Morocco tour. Discover more in our guide to the Sahara Desert in Morocco and our page on camel trekking in Merzouga.

    5. Erg Chigaga, the wild alternative

    Erg Chigaga lies beyond M’Hamid, near Zagora, and requires a 4×4 journey across the desert. It is quieter and more remote than Erg Chebbi. Not sure which one suits you? Read our comparison Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga.

    6. Ait Ben Haddou

    This fortified village of earthen houses is one of Morocco’s most photographed attractions. It became a UNESCO site in 1987 and has appeared in many films and series. Our article on movies filmed in Morocco tells the story of its cinema fame.

    7. The Dades and Todra Gorges

    Between Ouarzazate and Merzouga, the road follows valleys full of palm groves and old kasbahs. The Dades Valley is known for its winding road and rock formations. The Todra Gorge narrows to a canyon with walls hundreds of metres high, a favourite with walkers and climbers.

    8. The Atlas Mountains and Toubkal

    The High Atlas separates Marrakech from the desert. Jebel Toubkal, at 4,167 metres, is the highest peak in North Africa. Berber villages such as Imlil offer easy walks, mint tea and dramatic views. Learn more in our guide to the Atlas Mountains of Morocco.

    9. Chefchaouen, the Blue City

    Founded in 1471 in the Rif Mountains, Chefchaouen is famous for its blue-washed streets. It is calm, photogenic and perfect for a relaxed day. Our Chefchaouen guide explains when to visit and where to stay.

    10. The Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca

    Completed in 1993, the Hassan II Mosque stands on the Atlantic shore with a minaret about 210 metres tall. It is one of the few mosques in Morocco that non-Muslims can visit, on guided tours at set times. Plan about one hour for the visit, and dress modestly, covering shoulders and knees, as in any place of worship.

    11. Essaouira and the Atlantic coast

    Essaouira is a windy port with white walls, blue boats and fresh grilled fish. Its medina joined the UNESCO list in 2001. Further south, Agadir offers long beaches, while Asilah in the north is known for its murals. See our guides to the best beaches in Morocco and to Asilah.

    12. Volubilis and Meknes

    Volubilis holds the best-preserved Roman ruins in Morocco, with mosaics still in place. Nearby, Meknes was the capital of Sultan Moulay Ismail and has monumental gates and granaries. Both are UNESCO sites and fit perfectly into a Fes itinerary.

    Are there guided tours available for these attractions?

    Yes. Every major attraction in Morocco can be visited with a guided tour. In fact, a guide is highly recommended in the medinas of Fes and Marrakech, where the streets form a maze. Official guides hold a licence from the Ministry of Tourism and can explain monuments in several languages.

    There are three main ways to visit these attractions with a guide:

    • Private multi-day tours with a driver who stays with you throughout, plus local guides in each city.
    • Shared group tours that leave on fixed dates, mostly from Marrakech.
    • Half-day or full-day city tours with a licensed local guide.

    Some sites also have their own guided visits. For example, the Hassan II Mosque can only be visited by non-Muslims on guided tours. At Ait Ben Haddou and Volubilis, local guides wait at the entrance.

    What is usually included in a Morocco tour?

    Inclusions vary from one company to another, so always read the details before booking. A good private tour usually includes:

    • A private air-conditioned vehicle with an experienced driver.
    • Hotel or airport pick-up and drop-off.
    • Accommodation in riads, hotels or kasbahs.
    • A night in a desert camp with dinner and breakfast.
    • A camel ride into the dunes.
    • Fuel, tolls and parking.

    Usually not included: flights, lunches, entrance fees, local guides in some cities, drinks and tips. Ask for a clear list before you pay a deposit.

    What affects the price of a tour?

    Several factors decide the final cost of attractive tours in Morocco:

    • Group size: private tours cost more per person for solo travellers, less for families.
    • Accommodation level: standard riads, boutique riads or luxury hotels.
    • Desert camp: standard, comfort or luxury tents with private bathrooms.
    • Season: Easter, Christmas and New Year are the busiest periods.
    • Extras: 4×4 trips, hot-air balloons, cooking classes or trekking guides.

    To compare offers fairly, ask each agency for the same route, the same number of nights and the same accommodation level. If you need help, contact us for a free, detailed quote.

    Tips for travellers from Germany, Austria and Switzerland

    Interestingly, many searches for attractive tours come from German-speaking countries. Morocco is a natural choice: it is only about three to four hours by plane from Frankfurt, Munich, Düsseldorf, Berlin, Vienna or Zurich, with direct flights to Marrakech, Agadir and other cities.

    • Citizens of Germany, Austria and Switzerland do not need a visa for stays of up to 90 days.
    • Your passport must be valid for your whole stay; check current rules before travelling.
    • Morocco uses the Moroccan dirham, and cash is still important in the souks.

    Hidden attractions that make a tour special

    The famous sites deserve their reputation, but lesser-known places often become the highlight of a trip. Good attractive tours include a few of these hidden gems between the big stops.

    Skoura and its palm grove

    Just east of Ouarzazate, Skoura hides a vast palm grove dotted with old earthen kasbahs. Kasbah Amridil, one of the best preserved, is open to visitors and shows how wealthy families lived in the south.

    Khamlia and Gnawa music

    Near Merzouga, the village of Khamlia is home to a Gnawa community with roots in sub-Saharan Africa. Musicians welcome visitors with hypnotic rhythms, iron castanets and mint tea. It is a moving cultural stop on the way to the dunes.

    Rissani market

    Rissani, near Erfoud, holds a large traditional market several days a week. You will see spice sellers, livestock traders and the famous “donkey parking”. It feels far from the tourist souks of the big cities.

    Moulay Idriss

    This white hillside town near Volubilis is one of the holiest places in Morocco. It holds the tomb of Idriss I, founder of the first Moroccan dynasty. The views from the upper terraces are superb.

    Tetouan

    Tetouan, in the north, has a beautifully preserved medina listed by UNESCO in 1997. It shows strong Andalusian influences, with white houses and green doors. It fits easily into a tour starting in Tangier.

    Ifrane and the cedar forest

    Nicknamed “the Switzerland of Morocco”, Ifrane surprises visitors with sloping roofs and tidy parks. Nearby, the cedar forest shelters wild Barbary macaques. These tour attractions are especially popular with families.

    Attractive tours for every budget

    Morocco suits all budgets. The key is to adapt the route and accommodation, not to cut the experiences.

    • Budget: shared desert tours, simple riads and local restaurants. Ideal for backpackers.
    • Mid-range: private driver, charming riads and a comfort desert camp. The best value for most travellers.
    • Luxury: boutique hotels, luxury tents with private bathrooms, private guides and special experiences such as a hot-air balloon over Marrakech.

    Even on a modest budget, attractive tours are possible. Travelling as a group of four or more makes a private vehicle surprisingly affordable per person.

    FAQ: attractive tours in Morocco

    What are the most attractive tours in Morocco?

    The most attractive tours in Morocco are the 3-day Marrakech to Sahara tour, the Marrakech to Fes desert crossing and the 10 to 15-day grand tours from Casablanca or Tangier.

    What are the top attractions in Morocco?

    The top attractions are Jemaa el-Fna and the Marrakech medina, Fes el-Bali, the Erg Chebbi dunes, Ait Ben Haddou, the Todra Gorge, Chefchaouen, the Hassan II Mosque, Essaouira and Volubilis.

    Are there guided tours available for these attractions?

    Yes. Every major attraction in Morocco can be visited on a guided tour, either on a private multi-day circuit, a group tour or a city tour with a licensed local guide.

    How many days do you need for a Morocco tour?

    Plan at least 7 days to see the main cities and the Sahara. With 10 to 15 days, you can add Chefchaouen, the coast and slower days in the medinas.

    What is the best tour from Marrakech?

    The 3-day desert tour to Merzouga is the best tour from Marrakech for most travellers. For a single day, Essaouira and Ait Ben Haddou are the most popular choices.

    Is a private tour better than a group tour?

    A private tour is better for flexibility, comfort and personal attention. A group tour is cheaper and suits solo travellers who want to meet people.

    When is the best time for tours in Morocco?

    March to May and September to November are the best months. The weather is pleasant in the cities, mountains and desert.

    Can I combine Spain and Morocco on one tour?

    Yes. Ferries link Tarifa and Algeciras to Tangier in about one to two hours, depending on the route. Many travellers start their Morocco tour in Tangier after a holiday in Andalusia.

    Do I need a visa to join a tour in Morocco?

    Citizens of the EU, Switzerland, the UK, the US and many other countries do not need a visa for tourist stays of up to 90 days. Check the latest rules for your nationality.

    Is it safe to travel around Morocco on a tour?
