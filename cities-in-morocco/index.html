    Cities in Morocco: The Complete 2026 Guide to the Imperial, Coastal and Desert Gateways

    A 2026 guide to the cities in Morocco: imperial Atlantic Rif and desert gateways 2024 census proven routes real prices and private luxury travel

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 15 September 2026

    Quick answer: the cities in Morocco fall into four imperial capitals (Marrakech, Fes, Meknes and Rabat), an Atlantic axis led by Casablanca, a northern belt with Tangier and Chefchaouen, and a line of desert gateways such as Ouarzazate and Merzouga. Casablanca is the largest. Rabat is the capital.

    Your 30-second summary

    • Morocco counted 36,828,330 people on 1 September 2024, according to the High Commission for Planning (RGPH 2024).
    • Only four places pass one million: Casablanca (≈3.23m), Tangier (≈1.27m), Fes (≈1.18m) and Marrakech (≈1.15m).
    • Tangier overtook Fes and became the country’s second city, a genuine change since the 2014 census.
    • The political capital is Rabat, not Marrakech and not Casablanca.
    • Morocco welcomed 9.4 million visitors in the first half of 2026, an all-time record.
    • From 20 September 2026, the country stays on GMT permanently, so dusk arrives an hour earlier on the clock.
    • Seven days cover three cities properly. Ten to twelve days cover five plus the Sahara.
    • Africa’s only high-speed line links Tangier, Rabat and Casablanca; everything south of that runs on road.

    How many cities in Morocco are there, and how do they fit together?

    The cities in Morocco are not one place repeated twenty times, and they are not a hundred either. In practice, travellers move between some twenty urban centres that hold the history, the airports and the good hotels. Everything else is a large village, a mud-brick ksar or a local beach town.

    The 2024 census in numbers

    The High Commission for Planning published the RGPH 2024 results with a reference date of 1 September 2024. Moreover, the legal population came to 36,828,330, up 8.80 % on 2014. In addition, the urban population now sits near 23.1 million.

    That figure matters more than it looks. Likewise, it means almost two in three Moroccans live in a town or city. The medinas are therefore working neighbourhoods, not museums.

    The four million-plus cities

    Four centres pass the million mark. Casablanca stands near 3.23 million. Tangier reaches 1.27 million. Fes holds 1.18 million. Marrakech closes the group at roughly 1.15 million.

    Behind them come Salé (about 945,000), Meknes and Rabat. Then follow Agadir, Oujda, Kenitra, Tetouan, Temara and Safi.

    Tangier overtakes Fes: the change nobody mentions

    For decades, any list of cities in Morocco opened with Casablanca and continued with Fes. However, the 2024 census broke that habit. Tangier grew on the back of Tanger Med port, new industrial zones and the high-speed train, and moved into second place.

    For you as a traveller, that means new hotels, better restaurants and more frequent flights. It also makes the north as valid an entry point as Marrakech. You can see the options on our tours from Tangier page.

    How to read a map of Morocco before you book

    Look at any map and you will see three bands. The Atlantic coast runs from Tangier down to Dakhla. Moreover, the inland axis links Fes, Meknes, Marrakech and the Atlas. To the south-east, a line of oases marks the Sahara.

    Almost every itinerary combines two of those bands. Try all three in a week and you will spend the trip in the car. Our guide to where Morocco is sets out the real distances with numbers.

    A quick note on spelling

    The same place appears under several names. Fes is also Fès or Fez. Meknes is Meknès. Ouarzazate keeps its French spelling everywhere. Marrakech is sometimes Marrakesh, and Tangier is Tanger or Tanja.

    That detail matters when you book. If a hotel or a train does not show up, try the French spelling, because Moroccan booking systems still default to it.

    The five families of Moroccan cities

    Grouping them helps you decide. Instead of memorising a list, think in families. Each one offers a different kind of trip and, above all, a different pace.

    1. The imperial cities

    Marrakech, Fes, Meknes and Rabat each served as capital at some point. Besides, they keep walls, palaces, medersas and mausoleums. Moreover, they form the cultural backbone of the country.

    2. The Atlantic cities

    Casablanca, Rabat, El Jadida, Essaouira, Agadir and Dakhla face the ocean. Here the climate is milder, the breeze constant and the seafood excellent.

    3. The Mediterranean and Rif cities

    Tangier, Tetouan, Chefchaouen, Al Hoceima and Nador mix mountain and sea. Spanish is still widely spoken here, a legacy of the Protectorate.

    4. The desert gateways

    Ouarzazate, Zagora, Errachidia, Rissani and Merzouga are transit towns on the way to the dunes. Small, sun-baked and blessed with extraordinary night skies.

    5. The Atlas and deep south

    Ifrane, Azrou, Midelt, Taroudant and Tiznit sit among cedars, apple orchards or adobe walls. Likewise, few visitors stop, which is exactly why they reward you.

    The four imperial cities in Morocco

    If you remember one idea from this guide, make it this one. In short, the imperial cities explain the country. In practice, each dynasty picked a capital, walled it and filled it with craft. The result is four irreplaceable historic centres.

    What “imperial” actually means

    The term is historical rather than marketing. Marrakech was Almoravid and Almohad. Fes was Idrisid and Marinid. Meknes was Alaouite under Moulay Ismail. Rabat became capital again in 1912 and has stayed there.

    How many days the four need

    Eight days cover all four without rushing. Six days cover three. With four days, choose Marrakech and Fes, then save the rest for another trip.

    The classic imperial mistake

    Many travellers try to see all four in four days. In fact, the outcome is predictable: blurred photos and exhaustion. Cut one city and gain a night in the desert instead.

    Marrakech, the Red City

    Of all the cities in Morocco, Marrakech holds the largest number of hotel beds. It is also the natural gateway to the High Atlas and the Sahara. Most trips therefore start here.

    What to see in two days

    • Jemaa el-Fna at dusk, when the food stalls go up.
    • The northern souks: babouches, rugs, leather and lanterns.
    • Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs and Ben Youssef Medersa.
    • Jardin Majorelle and the Yves Saint Laurent museum in Gueliz.
    • The Mellah, the old Jewish quarter, and its spice market.

    Where to stay at the top end

    Inside the medina, palace riads offer private courtyards, their own hammam and butler service. In Hivernage and the Palmeraie you will find resorts with golf and large spas. Both work; the feeling differs.

    Day trips from Marrakech

    The best short excursions in the country leave from here. Besides, the Ourika Valley is an hour away. Imlil and Toubkal take ninety minutes. Essaouira sits two and a half hours west.

    Browse them all on day trips from Marrakech, including the Ait Ben Haddou day trip and the Ourika Valley.

    The airport that is changing everything

    Marrakech Menara airport is under expansion until 2028. Moreover, capacity will rise from 7.4 to 16 million passengers a year. Skytrax already named it Africa’s best regional airport in 2026.

    Long routes that start in Marrakech

    The classic Sahara circuits leave from here. In addition, the most requested are the 3-day desert tour, the 4-day Sahara tour and the 5-day version. Likewise, the full list sits on tours from Marrakech.

    Fes, the spiritual heart

    Fes is one of the cities in Morocco where history is still a working trade, and it keeps the largest, best-preserved medieval medina in the Arab world. UNESCO listed it in 1981 across 280 hectares. Nothing else in the country compares.

    Why Fes confuses you at first

    Fes el-Bali has thousands of lanes and no logical grid. That apparent chaos is the point, though. In practice, a local guide saves you two hours of wandering and opens doors you would never find.

    The essentials in Fes

    • Chouara tanneries, seen from a leather-shop terrace.
    • Bou Inania and Al Attarine medersas, with zellige and carved cedar.
    • The blue gate of Bab Boujloud and its square.
    • Al Qarawiyyin University, founded in 859.
    • The potters’ quarter at Ain Nokbi.

    What is new in Fes in 2026

    Since 26 March 2026, the city has run free cultural performances at Bab Boujloud from Friday to Sunday. The historic Palais Jamaï also reopened this year within the Legend Collection of Preferred Hotels & Resorts.

    Fes as a desert base

    Many travellers forget that Merzouga is one long driving day from Fes. In fact, the route crosses the Middle Atlas, with cedar forest and Barbary macaques. See the 3-day Fes to Merzouga tour or the wider tours from Fes.

    Before you go, read our guide to religion in Morocco. Fes makes far more sense with that background.

    Meknes and Volubilis, the pair almost nobody books

    Among the cities in Morocco, Meknes usually drops off short itineraries. That is a mistake. At the same time, the city of Moulay Ismail keeps colossal walls, monumental granaries and a calm that Fes lost years ago.

    What to see in Meknes

    Start at Bab Mansour, arguably the most elegant gate in the country. Continue to the Heri es-Souani granaries and the sultan’s mausoleum. Finish on El Hedim square, far quieter than Jemaa el-Fna.

    Volubilis, Morocco’s Rome

    Half an hour away lie the Roman ruins of Volubilis, also UNESCO listed. Besides, the mosaics remain in the open air on a hill of olive trees. Late afternoon light lifts them off the ground.

    Moulay Idriss Zerhoun

    This whitewashed town sits on two hills nearby. It is among the most revered places in Moroccan Islam. In addition, an hour is enough, and it changes the rhythm of the day.

    Rabat, the capital that surprises people

    Rabat has been the political capital since 1912 and is one of the easiest cities in Morocco to walk. Likewise, it is clean, green, orderly and served by a tram. Its historic core is also World Heritage.

    What to see in Rabat in one day

    • Kasbah of the Udayas, with houses painted blue and white.
    • Hassan Tower and the Mausoleum of Mohammed V.
    • Chellah necropolis, Roman and Marinid at once.
    • Mohammed VI Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art.
    • A walk along the Bou Regreg estuary towards Salé.

    Rabat at the luxury end

    The capital is in a new hotel cycle. The country’s first Ritz-Carlton opened here, and Marriott plans seven properties in Morocco by 2027. For high-end travellers, Rabat is no longer just an administrative stop.

    Why Rabat fits a route well

    It sits on the high-speed line. Tangier is 1 hour 20 minutes away. Casablanca is 50 minutes. Furthermore, it slots neatly between the north and the centre.

    Casablanca, the economic engine

    Casablanca is the largest of all the cities in Morocco and the country’s economic motor. It has no monumental medina, but it does have an energy nowhere else matches. It is also the main intercontinental airport.

    The Hassan II Mosque

    This is the city’s great landmark and one of the few mosques non-Muslims may enter. In addition, its minaret rises 210 metres. Book the guided visit ahead in high season.

    Art deco and mauresque

    Downtown holds a remarkable art deco ensemble from the 1920s and 1930s. Boulevard Mohammed V, the central market and the old Place de France concentrate the best of it. Allow two very photogenic hours.

    Casablanca as an arrival point

    Long-haul flights almost always land here. At the same time, a train runs from Mohammed V airport straight to the city and on to Rabat. Our tours from Casablanca are built around that reality.

    Recommended routes from Casablanca

    The week-long and ten-day circuits work best. Look at the 7-day itinerary from Casablanca, the 8-day private tour or the 10-day route.

    Tangier, the gateway between two continents

    Among the cities in Morocco, Tangier is having its best century in a hundred years. Tanger Med port moved more than 11 million containers in 2025, and the city reinvented itself as an urban destination. Spain lies 14 kilometres away.

    What to see in Tangier

    • The Kasbah and its museum, overlooking the Strait.
    • Café Hafa, open since 1921, above the sea.
    • The Grand Socco and Petit Socco in the late afternoon.
    • Cap Spartel and the Caves of Hercules.
    • The city beach and its rebuilt seafront promenade.

    Tangier and the Spanish connection

    Spanish is a street language here, not a textbook subject. Many traders speak it fluently because of the Protectorate and the proximity of Spain. Besides, it gives the north a very different feel.

    Routes from the north

    From Tangier you drop south to Chefchaouen, Fes and the desert. Moreover, the 7-day tour from Tangier and the 10-day circuit both work well. With more time, there is the 15-day grand tour.

    Chefchaouen, the blue city of the Rif

    Chefchaouen is probably the most photographed of the cities in Morocco. In addition, its indigo walls cover a small, walkable old quarter. It sits at 600 metres, so nights turn cool.

    How long to stay

    One night is enough to see it well. Two nights let you climb to the Spanish Mosque at sunrise and walk to the Ras El Maa waterfall. More than two nights is usually too much.

    How to get there

    Tangier is about two and a half hours by road. Fes is roughly four. There is no train, so a private car is the comfortable option.

    The best hour of the day

    Go between 7am and 9am. Furthermore, the coach excursions arrive around midday. Our full Chefchaouen guide maps the corners and the timings.

    Spanish cities in Morocco: what they are and how to visit

    Search results for “Spanish cities in Morocco” mix two very different things. In practice, it helps to separate the current Spanish territories from the Moroccan cities with a Spanish heritage.

    Ceuta and Melilla

    Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish autonomous cities on the North African coast. They are not part of Morocco, although Moroccan territory surrounds them. Rabat maintains a long-standing claim over both.

    Tetouan, the daughter of Granada

    Tetouan was the capital of the Spanish Protectorate from 1913 to 1956. In fact, its medina is World Heritage and its new town keeps Spanish modernist buildings. Spanish is heard constantly here.

    Larache, Asilah and Sidi Ifni

    Larache has a circular Plaza de España and the cemetery where Jean Genet is buried. Asilah pairs Portuguese ramparts with contemporary murals. Sidi Ifni, far south, stayed Spanish until 1969 and keeps its blue-and-white art deco.

    If that coast appeals, read our Asilah guide. To understand why the language survived, see what language is spoken in Morocco.

    The Atlantic cities: ocean, wind and seafood

    The cities in Morocco that face the Atlantic are the perfect counterweight to the interior. At the same time, temperatures drop, humidity rises and the seafood arrives. For many travellers, this is the most relaxed part of the trip.

    Essaouira

    Old Mogador keeps eighteenth-century ramparts and a harbour of blue fishing boats. It is the capital of wind and windsurfing. Moreover, one day works from Marrakech; two nights work better.

    Book the Essaouira day trip from Marrakech if time is tight.

    El Jadida

    Its Portuguese cistern, with columns mirrored in still water, is one of the strangest sights in the country. In addition, old Mazagan is World Heritage too. Casablanca lies ninety minutes away.

    Agadir

    Agadir was rebuilt after the 1960 earthquake and is now a pure beach destination. Likewise, it offers nine kilometres of sand, large hotels and sunshine most of the year. Do not look for an old medina, because there is none.

    Dakhla

    Far south, Dakhla has become a kitesurfing mecca. Furthermore, it combines a turquoise lagoon, dunes and design camps. It needs a domestic flight, so it suits longer trips.

    The desert gateways

    Among the cities in Morocco, one group is rarely a destination in itself. These are the Sahara bases. In fact, you sleep one night, leave at dawn and continue to the dunes.

    Ouarzazate

    Known as the door of the desert and the capital of Moroccan cinema. Atlas Studios and Taourirt Kasbah are here. Ait Ben Haddou, the country’s most famous ksar, sits 30 kilometres away.

    Merzouga and Erg Chebbi

    Merzouga is the village at the foot of the Erg Chebbi dunes, which reach around 150 metres. At the same time, it is the most accessible place to sleep in a desert camp. See our Sahara Desert Morocco guide.

    Zagora and M’Hamid

    Zagora opens the route to Erg Chigaga, wilder and far less visited. Besides, the comparison sits in Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga. Moreover, the 2-day Zagora tour is the express version.

    Errachidia and Rissani

    Errachidia has an airport and cuts the drive to the dunes dramatically. Rissani keeps its donkey market and the origins of the Alaouite dynasty. Look at the 3-day desert tour from Errachidia.

    Less touristy cities worth a stop

    On a second trip, these cities in Morocco give you the country that standard circuits never touch. None of them needs more than a day.

    Taroudant

    Nicknamed “little Marrakech” for its seven kilometres of adobe walls. It stands in the Souss Valley among orange groves, ninety minutes from Agadir.

    Tiznit and Tafraoute

    Tiznit is the capital of Amazigh silverwork. Tafraoute, further south, appears among pink granite boulders and almond blossom every February.

    Ifrane and Azrou

    Ifrane looks like an Alpine village with pitched roofs. Azrou, twenty minutes on, opens the cedar forest where the macaques live. Likewise, both sit between Fes and the desert.

    Oujda and Saidia

    Oujda is the great city of the east, beside the Algerian border closed since 1994. Saidia offers fourteen kilometres of Mediterranean beach barely known abroad.

    Safi and Ouezzane

    Safi produces the most famous glazed pottery in the country. Ouezzane, in the western Rif, was the seat of an influential Sufi brotherhood and sees almost no foreign visitors.

    Luxury travel across the cities in Morocco

    Morocco runs one of the strongest high-end offers in Africa. Furthermore, this is not only about expensive hotels. In practice, it is a travel model: a private driver, licensed city guides and access to spaces closed to the public.

    What actually changes at the top end

    • A private vehicle with a driver-guide for the whole route, never a group.
    • Palace riads of five to twelve rooms, with personal service.
    • Official city guides rather than general escorts.
    • Private dinners on rooftops, in the dunes or in family homes.
    • Fast-track transfers and airport assistance.

    Where the high end concentrates

    Marrakech leads by a distance, followed by Rabat, Casablanca and Tamuda Bay in the north. Fes has spectacular historic riads, though fewer of them. In the desert, luxury camps now include private bathrooms and air conditioning.

    How many days you need: four proven routes

    Choosing a route across the cities in Morocco is easier when you count nights rather than days. Overall, these four work, and we run them every week.

    Route A · 5 days: Marrakech and the Sahara

    Marrakech (2 nights), Dades, Merzouga and back. Moreover, it is the best-selling format and the one that balances city and desert best. Details in the 5-day Marrakech to Sahara itinerary.

    Route B · 7 days: a loop from Casablanca

    Route C · 10 days: north and south

    Tangier, Chefchaouen, Fes, Merzouga, Ouarzazate, Marrakech and Essaouira. The most complete option for a first long trip.

    Which cities in Morocco suit your kind of trip

    There is no single route. The best cities in Morocco to visit depend on what you want: history, beach, desert or rest. These combinations work in practice.

    Classic first trip

    Marrakech, Ait Ben Haddou, Merzouga and Fes. In practice, it covers medina, kasbahs, dunes and craft in one clean line. In fact, we run it more than any other, and for good reason.

    Deep cultural trip

    Fes, Meknes, Volubilis, Rabat and Tetouan. At the same time, the focus falls on medersas, Roman mosaics and Andalusian architecture. Besides, it needs strong local guides.

    Travelling with children

    Casablanca, Rabat, Essaouira and Marrakech. Short legs, a beach and only one long driving day. Add the camel ride in Agafay as a taster.

    Honeymoon or luxury trip

    Marrakech, the Dades Valley, a private camp in Erg Chebbi and Essaouira. In addition, it means few stages, exceptional hotels and private dinners. Likewise, we compare formats in private vs group tours in Morocco.

    Second trip

    Tangier, Chefchaouen, Al Hoceima, Oujda and Saidia. Furthermore, this is the north almost nobody drives, with mountains, the Mediterranean and very little international tourism.

    Photography trip

    Chefchaouen at sunrise, the Fes tanneries mid-morning and the dunes at dusk. In practice, those three give the strongest frames in the country.

    Practical planning for international travellers

    The cities in Morocco are unusually easy to organise. Flights are short from Europe, visas are simple for most passports and the infrastructure keeps improving.

    Flights and connections

    London, Manchester, Paris, Madrid and Lisbon all offer direct flights to Marrakech, Casablanca, Fes, Tangier and Agadir. From North America, Royal Air Maroc flies non-stop from New York, Washington, Miami and Montreal to Casablanca. Details sit in flights to Morocco.

    Ferries from Spain

    Tarifa to Tangier Ville takes about an hour. Algeciras to Tanger Med takes ninety minutes. At the same time, both suit travellers combining Andalusia with the Moroccan north.

    Entry rules

    British, US, Canadian, Australian and EU passport holders enter without a visa for up to 90 days. Confirm your own case on the Moroccan consular website before booking.

    Time and daylight

    From 20 September 2026, Morocco stays on GMT all year. It therefore matches UK winter time and sits one hour behind mainland Spain in summer. Check what time it is in Morocco now.

    Money

    The currency is the dirham (MAD), which is not convertible outside the country. In September 2026, one pound buys roughly 12.8 dirhams and one dollar about 9.45. Change only what you need at the airport.

    Five planning mistakes to avoid

    1. Chaining too many cities

    Five cities in seven days reads well on paper. In practice you sleep in five hotels and see none of them. Cut the list and gain depth.

    2. Confusing Agafay with the Sahara

    Agafay is a stony desert thirty minutes from Marrakech. It is fine for an afternoon, but it has no dunes. By contrast, the real Sahara needs at least three days.

    3. Booking only chain hotels

    Historic riads are part of the experience. Alternate: two riad nights in the medina and one resort night to recover.

    4. Forgetting the September clock change

    With permanent GMT, October dusk falls around 6.30pm. Plan outdoor visits earlier and leave dinner for afterwards.

    5. Underestimating distances

    Marrakech to Merzouga is about 560 kilometres of mountain road. In addition, that means nine or ten hours with stops. Never do it in one push if you can avoid it.

    Turning the list into a map

    Draw a triangle. Tangier sits at the top, Marrakech at the bottom left and Merzouga at the bottom right. Almost every itinerary travels two of those three sides. That is all the geometry you need.

    Which cities to leave out on a first visit

    Agadir, Dakhla, Oujda and Nador are excellent, yet each needs a flight or a long detour. Save them for a second trip and keep the first one inside the triangle.

    What makes the cities in Morocco different from each other

    Travellers often assume one medina is much like the next. In reality, the cities in Morocco were shaped by different dynasties, trades and trade routes, and it shows on the ground.

    Founding dates and founders

    Fes dates from 789-808 under the Idrisids. Marrakech was founded in 1070 by the Almoravids. Meknes rose under Moulay Ismail in the late seventeenth century. Casablanca, by contrast, is largely a twentieth-century creation.

    Crafts define the character

    Fes means leather, brass and zellige. Marrakech means rugs, lanterns and babouches. Safi means pottery, Tiznit means silver and Essaouira means thuya wood. Each souk reflects a local trade, not a tourist catalogue.

    Architecture you can date by eye

    Marinid medersas use carved cedar and stucco. Almohad minarets are square and plain. Alaouite gates are enormous and ceremonial. French-era new towns bring wide boulevards and art deco façades.

    Why this matters to your itinerary

    Pairing two similar cities wastes a day. Pair contrasting ones instead: Fes with Chefchaouen, Marrakech with Essaouira, Casablanca with Rabat. The trip then keeps changing shape.

    Frequently asked questions about the cities in Morocco

    What is the capital of Morocco?

    Rabat has been the political and administrative capital since 1912. However, many travellers assume it is Marrakech or Casablanca. We explain it in Marrakech capital.

    Which is the largest of the cities in Morocco?

    Casablanca, with roughly 3.23 million residents at the 2024 census. It is also the biggest port and the financial centre of the kingdom.

    How many imperial cities are there?

    Four: Marrakech, Fes, Meknes and Rabat. In fact, each served as capital at some point and each keeps walls and palaces.

    Which city is best to start in?

    Marrakech if the desert and the Atlas are your priority. Casablanca if you arrive on a long-haul flight. Tangier if you want the north and Chefchaouen.

    How many days do the main cities need?

    Seven days cover three cities and one desert night. Ten days cover five. At the same time, fourteen days cover the whole triangle without rushing.

    Can you travel between cities by train?

    Yes, on the Tangier–Rabat–Casablanca–Marrakech axis. There is no rail service to the south or the desert, so a private car takes over.

    Which cities in Morocco are World Heritage sites?

    UNESCO lists the medinas of Fes, Marrakech, Meknes, Tetouan and Essaouira, plus Rabat, Volubilis, Ait Ben Haddou and Portuguese Mazagan at El Jadida.

    Is it safe to travel between cities?

    Yes. Morocco ranks first in Africa on the 2026 safety index and 42nd worldwide. We set out the official advisories in is Morocco safe?.

    Where is Spanish still spoken?

    Mainly in Tangier, Tetouan, Larache, Al Hoceima and Nador. Moreover, it is a legacy of the Protectorate and of daily contact with Spain.

    What should I wear in the medinas?

    Shoulders and knees covered, natural fabrics and closed shoes. In addition, the full guide is in what to wear in Morocco.

    How much does an imperial cities trip cost?

    A private eight-day circuit runs from £650 to £950 per person at the standard tier. At the luxury tier, expect £1,550 to £2,750.

    What is the best time of year?

    March to May and September to November. Avoid July and August inland, where Marrakech and Fes regularly pass 40°C.
