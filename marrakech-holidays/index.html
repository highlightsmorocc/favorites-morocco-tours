    Marrakech Holidays: The Complete Planning Guide

    Planning Marrakech holidays? Real costs, the best months where to stay day trips and Sahara add-ons, written by a local Morocco travel agency

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 29 September 2026

    Marrakech holidays sit at the top of almost every Morocco wish list, and the reason is simple. In fact, the city packs a UNESCO-listed medina, snow-capped mountains on the horizon and the gateway to the Sahara into one compact package. For example, you can land in the morning, drink mint tea on a rooftop by lunchtime and watch the storytellers of Jemaa el-Fna the same evening.

    However, a great trip still needs a plan. Prices swing hard by season, neighbourhoods differ more than photos suggest, and the desert sits further away than most first-timers expect. This guide walks you through every decision, from choosing dates to booking excursions, so your holiday runs smoothly from the first minute.

    We are Favorites Morocco Tours, a local agency based in Morocco. We drive these roads every week, so the advice below reflects what actually works on the ground rather than what looks good in a brochure.

    What Marrakech Holidays Look Like Today

    A city built for short and long stays

    Marrakech works for a three-day break and for a two-week grand tour. The old city keeps you busy for two or three days. After that, the mountains, the coast and the dunes take over. Because of that flexibility, Marrakech holidays suit couples, families and groups of friends equally well.

    The four faces of the city

    First, the medina holds the souks, the palaces and most riads. Second, Gueliz brings wide avenues, cafés and modern shops. Third, Hivernage offers large hotels, gardens and nightlife. Finally, the Palmeraie spreads palm groves and pool resorts across the northern edge.

    In other words, each district changes the feel of your trip. Consequently, choosing the right one matters more than choosing the right hotel star rating.

    What changed recently

    Morocco moved to permanent GMT on 20 September 2026. As a result, the sun now sets roughly one hour earlier on the clock than travellers remember. In practice, you should start day trips earlier and plan photography around a 5pm to 6pm winter sunset.

    Meanwhile, the country keeps investing in tourism ahead of the 2030 World Cup. In addition, new hotels, restored monuments and better roads all help. Yet the medina keeps its old rhythm, and that contrast is exactly what makes Marrakech holidays memorable.

    Who this guide serves

    This guide targets first-time visitors and returning travellers alike. Furthermore, it answers the practical questions that generic listicles rarely cover well: real budgets, honest timings and the trade-offs between package deals and private travel. In short, it turns Marrakech holidays from a vague idea into a working plan.

    For background on the city itself, our article on Marrakech and the question of Morocco’s capital explains the history behind the red walls.

    The Best Time for Marrakech Holidays

    Spring: March to May

    In general, spring delivers the finest weather of the year. Daytime highs sit around 22C to 28C, gardens bloom and the Atlas still wears snow. Consequently, spring ranks as the busiest and priciest season alongside autumn. Book riads two to three months ahead if you travel in April.

    Summer: June to August

    Summer brings serious heat. In fact, afternoon temperatures often pass 38C, and July and August can approach 45C. Nevertheless, summer works if you adapt. Therefore, start early, rest between 1pm and 5pm, and choose a riad with a plunge pool.

    Many travellers also escape to the coast. Alternatively, the Essaouira full day trip from Marrakech drops the temperature by fifteen degrees in three hours of driving.

    Autumn: September to November

    Autumn is our quiet favourite. September still feels hot, yet October and November settle into perfect sightseeing weather. Additionally, the light turns golden, which flatters the ochre walls and your photographs. For a wider seasonal view, read our guide to the best time to visit Morocco.

    Winter: December to February

    On the other hand, winter days stay mild, usually 18C to 20C, but nights drop close to 5C. Therefore, pack a proper jacket and check that your riad has heating. Christmas and New Year fill fast, so reserve early. Our notes on Morocco tours in December explain the festive period in detail.

    Ramadan and religious holidays

    Ramadan shifts about eleven days earlier each year, so it falls across February and March in 2027. During the month, many restaurants close in daytime while evenings turn festive. Monuments stay open, although hours shorten. If you want a calmer, more spiritual city, that period rewards you. Our article on religion in Morocco covers the etiquette in depth.

    The short answer

    All in all, for most travellers, mid-March to mid-May and late September to mid-November give the best mix of weather, prices and daylight. Marrakech holidays inside those windows rarely disappoint.

    How Many Days Do You Need?

    Three days: the city break

    Three days cover the essentials. You explore the souks, visit two or three monuments, eat well and enjoy one hammam. Short Marrakech holidays of this length suit a long weekend from Europe.

    Five days: city plus mountains or coast

    By contrast, five days let you add excursions without rushing. For example, you might take the Ourika Valley day trip on one day and the Ouzoud waterfalls day trip on another. Our five days in Morocco guide shows several ways to build this length.

    Seven days: city plus the Sahara

    Seven days unlock the desert without pain. A classic loop runs Marrakech, Ait Ben Haddou, Dades, Todra, Merzouga and back. The 7 days in Morocco itinerary lays out the stages day by day.

    Ten days or more: the grand circuit

    Furthermore, ten days add Fes, the coast or the deep south. Similarly, travellers with two weeks often finish in Casablanca or Tangier. See the 12-day tour from Marrakech to the Sahara for the widest version of this route.

    The mistake to avoid

    Do not try to squeeze Fes, Chefchaouen and Merzouga into five days from Marrakech. In fact, distances run long and roads climb over mountain passes. Fewer stops always produce better Marrakech holidays.

    How Much Do Marrakech Holidays Cost?

    The three budget levels

    Costs vary widely, yet three broad levels help you plan. Budget travellers spend roughly 40 to 60 euros per person per day. Mid-range travellers spend 90 to 150 euros. Luxury travellers spend 250 euros and upwards.

    Accommodation

    For instance, a simple medina riad costs 35 to 60 euros per room per night. By comparison, a beautiful mid-range riad with a pool runs 80 to 160 euros. Finally, five-star palaces and Palmeraie resorts start near 250 euros and climb quickly. Rates rise around Christmas, New Year and Easter.

    Food and drink

    Street food and local canteens charge 3 to 7 euros for a full plate. A mid-range restaurant dinner costs 12 to 25 euros. Rooftop restaurants with a view charge more for the view than for the tagine. Alcohol carries heavy tax, so a glass of wine often costs 8 to 12 euros.

    Transport and excursions

    Petit taxis inside the city cost 2 to 4 euros per ride when metered. A private airport transfer costs 15 to 25 euros. Group day trips run 25 to 45 euros per person, while private day trips with a driver cost 90 to 160 euros for the whole car.

    A realistic weekly budget

    For one week, two people travelling mid-range should budget around 1,400 to 1,900 euros including flights from Europe. That figure covers a good riad, daily meals, two excursions and shopping money. Marrakech holidays therefore cost less than most European city breaks of the same standard.

    Where people overspend

    Travellers usually overspend on three things: airport taxis without a fixed price, rooftop tourist restaurants on the main square, and rushed souk purchases on the first day. Fix those three and your budget stretches noticeably further.

    Flights, Airport and Arrival

    Flying into Marrakech Menara

    Marrakech Menara Airport carries the code RAK and sits only six kilometres from the medina. Direct flights connect the city with London, Paris, Madrid, Barcelona, Brussels, Milan, Lisbon and many other European hubs. Flight time from Madrid runs about two hours and twenty minutes. From London, expect three and a half hours.

    Choosing your route

    Low-cost carriers dominate the market, so book early for the best fares. Tuesdays and Wednesdays usually price lower than weekends. Our detailed guide to flights to Marrakech compares routes, seasons and booking windows.

    Arriving through Casablanca

    Some long-haul travellers land in Casablanca instead. From there, a train reaches Marrakech in about three hours. Alternatively, you can start a full circuit from the coast, as our tours from Casablanca show.

    Passport, visa and entry

    Citizens of the European Union, the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Australia and most Latin American countries enter Morocco without a visa for stays up to ninety days. Your passport should stay valid for at least six months. Officers stamp the passport and sometimes ask for your hotel address, so keep the booking handy.

    The first hour on the ground

    Arrange your transfer before departure. Cars cannot enter deep into the medina, so your driver will stop at the nearest gate. A porter with a cart then carries the luggage to the riad. That short walk surprises many visitors, yet it forms part of the charm.

    Money on arrival

    The dirham is a closed currency, which means you cannot buy it easily abroad. Withdraw cash from an ATM in the arrivals hall or exchange a small amount there, then use city bureaux for better rates later.

    Where to Stay on Marrakech Holidays: Riads, Hotels and Resorts

    Riads inside the medina

    A riad is a traditional house built around an interior courtyard. Most hold only six to twelve rooms, so service feels personal. Breakfast on the terrace, a small pool in the patio and thick walls that block the heat define the experience. For many guests, the riad becomes the best memory of their Marrakech holidays.

    On the other hand, riads sit down narrow lanes. Taxis stop at the gate, and night arrivals can feel confusing. Ask your host to send someone to meet you.

    Hotels in Gueliz and Hivernage

    Gueliz suits travellers who want pavements, parking and modern cafés. Hivernage suits those who want space, gardens, spas and easy access to restaurants. Both districts sit ten minutes from the medina by taxi.

    Resorts in the Palmeraie

    The Palmeraie offers large pools, golf courses and quiet nights. However, you will depend on taxis for every trip into the old city. Families with small children often prefer this trade-off.

    How to choose your base

    Choose the medina for atmosphere. For comfort, choose Hivernage. Choose the Palmeraie for space. Many visitors split the week: three nights in a riad, then two nights beside a pool. That combination gives balanced Marrakech holidays without repeating the same view every morning.

    Booking tips that save money

    Book directly with the riad when possible, because owners often include the airport transfer. Ask for a room away from the courtyard if you sleep lightly, since sound travels upward. Finally, confirm whether the pool is heated, as unheated pools feel icy from November to March.

    Inside the Medina: What to See

    Jemaa el-Fna

    The great square anchors the city. By day, orange juice carts and henna artists work the edges. By night, food stalls, musicians and storytellers fill the centre. UNESCO recognised this living tradition as intangible cultural heritage, and the atmosphere still justifies the fame.

    Koutoubia Mosque

    The Koutoubia minaret rises 77 metres and dates from the twelfth century. Non-Muslims cannot enter, yet the gardens around it stay open and make a fine evening walk. The tower also serves as your compass whenever you lose direction.

    Bahia Palace

    Bahia Palace shows nineteenth-century craftsmanship at its peak. Painted cedar ceilings, zellige tilework and shaded courtyards follow one after another. Arrive at opening time, because tour groups fill the rooms by mid-morning.

    Ben Youssef Madrasa

    This restored Quranic school ranks among the most beautiful buildings in Morocco. Carved stucco, marble and cedar surround a still pool. Photographers love the early light here.

    Saadian Tombs and El Badi Palace

    The Saadian Tombs stayed sealed for centuries until 1917. Today the marble chambers draw long queues, so go early. El Badi Palace next door lies in ruins, yet the scale and the storks nesting on the walls impress in a different way.

    Getting lost on purpose

    Finally, allow one afternoon with no plan. The medina rewards wandering. Streets narrow, workshops appear, and a dyers’ alley or a coppersmith’s corner turns up when you least expect it. Our Marrakech old town and new town guide maps the areas in more detail, and the longer complete Marrakech travel guide covers the monuments one by one.

    Gardens, Museums and Modern Marrakech

    Jardin Majorelle and the YSL Museum

    Jacques Majorelle painted this garden in a cobalt blue that now carries his name. Yves Saint Laurent later saved it from developers. Book tickets online, because the queue outside grows long by ten o’clock. The Yves Saint Laurent Museum stands next door and pairs naturally with the visit.

    Le Jardin Secret

    Hidden inside the medina, this restored riad garden offers shade, water channels and a tower with a rooftop view. It stays calmer than Majorelle, which makes it a smart mid-afternoon stop.

    Menara Gardens and the Agdal

    The Menara basin, framed by olive groves and the Atlas, has appeared on postcards for a century. Sunset there feels genuinely local.

    Museums worth your time

    The Musée de Marrakech, the Dar Si Said museum of woodwork and the Maison de la Photographie each deserve an hour. The photography museum also serves lunch on a terrace facing the Atlas.

    Modern culture in Gueliz

    Contemporary galleries, concept stores and coffee roasters have multiplied in Gueliz. Consequently, modern Marrakech holidays can easily mix twelfth-century monuments with a design bookshop and a specialty flat white.

    Eating Well in Marrakech

    The dishes to try first

    Start with tagine, then move to couscous, which locals traditionally eat on Fridays. Add harira soup, pastilla, mechoui lamb and tanjia, the Marrakech speciality slow-cooked in a clay urn at the neighbourhood furnace. Our guide to traditional Moroccan dishes explains each one, while our overview of cuisine in Morocco puts them in context. Food, honestly, ranks among the strongest reasons to plan Marrakech holidays at all.

    Street food on the square

    The night stalls of Jemaa el-Fna serve grilled meat, snails, sardines and steamed sheep’s head. Choose a busy stall, because turnover means freshness. Agree on the price before you sit.

    Rooftop restaurants

    Rooftops deliver the view of the Koutoubia and the sunset call to prayer. Food quality varies, so read recent reviews rather than trusting the terrace alone.

    Where locals actually eat

    Small canteens near Bab Doukkala and around Gueliz serve honest food at low prices. A plate of tanjia with bread and olives costs a few euros. Additionally, the bakeries near residential streets sell msemen and harcha for breakfast.

    Dietary needs

    Vegetarians eat well, since vegetable tagines, zaalouk, taktouka and lentil dishes appear everywhere. Vegans should ask about butter and honey. Meanwhile, gluten-free travellers can rely on grilled meats, salads and rice, though bread arrives with almost every meal.

    Food safety in one line

    Drink bottled or filtered water, eat where crowds gather, and peel your own fruit. With those habits, most visitors never have a problem.

    Hammam, Spa and Slow Afternoons

    The public hammam

    Every neighbourhood has one. You bring a bucket, black soap and a kessa glove, then move between hot and warm rooms. The experience costs very little and feels wonderfully local, though it offers no privacy.

    The private spa hammam

    Riads and spas offer the same ritual with towels, tea and a massage. Prices range from 25 to 90 euros. Book the late afternoon slot, because it turns the transition into the evening into something calm.

    Why it belongs in your plan

    Days in the medina tire the feet and the senses. A hammam resets both. Our article on the Moroccan hammam explains the etiquette step by step, including what to wear and how much to tip.

    Shopping the Souks Without Stress

    What to buy

    Look for leather babouches, hand-knotted rugs, argan oil, brass lanterns, ceramics from Safi and Tamegroute, spices, and woven baskets. Quality varies, so compare three stalls before you commit.

    How bargaining actually works

    Bargaining is a conversation, not a fight. Ask the price, smile, and offer roughly half. Meet somewhere in the middle. If the number does not work for you, thank the seller and walk. Nobody takes offence.

    Rugs and carpets

    Rug sessions last an hour and involve tea. Ask whether the rug is hand-knotted, check the back for knot density, and request a receipt.

    Argan oil and cosmetics

    Buy culinary argan oil for food and cosmetic oil for skin. Women’s cooperatives on the Essaouira road sell both with clear labelling. The oil should smell nutty, not rancid.

    Two rules that protect your budget

    First, never shop seriously on day one, since you still lack price references. Second, avoid accepting a guide who offers to show you “his cousin’s shop”, because commission gets added to your price. Follow both rules and shopping becomes one of the highlights of your Marrakech holidays.

    Day Trips to Add to Your Marrakech Holidays

    Ait Ben Haddou and Ouarzazate

    This UNESCO-listed kasbah appears in countless films. The drive crosses the Tizi n’Tichka pass at 2,260 metres, which alone justifies the day. See the Ait Ben Haddou day trip from Marrakech for timings.

    Ourika Valley and Setti Fatma

    Only ninety minutes away, the Ourika Valley offers river cafés, Berber villages and seven waterfalls. It suits families and hot summer days.

    Imlil and Mount Toubkal

    Imlil sits at the foot of Toubkal, the highest peak in North Africa at 4,167 metres. Walkers reach mule tracks, walnut groves and viewpoints within an hour of leaving the car. Our Imlil Valley day trip covers the route, and the wider Atlas Mountains guide explains the region.

    Ouzoud waterfalls

    These falls drop 110 metres in three tiers. Barbary macaques live in the olive groves above, and small boats approach the spray below. Expect a full day, since the drive takes around three hours each way.

    Essaouira on the Atlantic

    Essaouira brings ramparts, blue boats, seafood grills and steady wind. Kitesurfers love it, and so do travellers escaping the heat.

    Agafay desert

    Agafay is a stony desert, not a sand sea, yet it lies only forty minutes from the city. Camel rides, quad bikes and dinner camps under the stars work brilliantly for short stays. Check the Agafay quad bike and camel ride for the classic version.

    Choosing between them

    With three days, pick one excursion. With five days, pick two and keep a rest day. Overloading the schedule is the fastest way to spoil otherwise excellent Marrakech holidays.

    Adding the Sahara Desert to Your Trip

    How far the dunes really are

    Merzouga, home of the Erg Chebbi dunes, lies roughly 560 kilometres from Marrakech. The drive takes nine to ten hours with stops. Zagora sits closer, about 360 kilometres, yet its dunes stay smaller.

    Three days versus four

    The 3-day tour from Marrakech to the desert remains the most popular option. It works, although two of the three days involve long driving. The 4-day version adds breathing room in the Dades or Todra gorges, and most guests prefer it.

    Five to seven days

    With more time, the route opens up. The 5-day tour adds the Draa Valley or extra hiking. The 6-day tour and the 7-day private tour add Skoura, Rissani and slower mornings among the dunes.

    Ending in Fes instead

    Many travellers cross the country rather than return. The 3-day Marrakech to Fes tour and its 4-day and 5-day versions finish in the north, which suits open-jaw flights.

    Zagora for very short stays

    If you only have two days, the 2-day tour to Zagora still delivers a camp night under the stars. Manage expectations about dune size and you will enjoy it.

    What a desert night involves

    You reach the dunes in late afternoon, ride a camel or a 4×4 to camp, then eat tagine around a fire while Gnawa drums play. Nights turn cold from November to March, so bring a fleece even in summer. Sunrise over Erg Chebbi remains the single strongest memory most guests take home.

    Package Marrakech Holidays, Private Tours or Independent Travel?

    Package holidays

    Tour operators sell flight-and-hotel bundles that look cheap in low season. They suit travellers who want zero planning. However, packages often place you in a large hotel outside the medina and add optional excursions at high margins.

    Group tours

    Shared minibus tours cost the least per person. You travel with eight to sixteen strangers and follow a fixed schedule. Sociable travellers enjoy them, though photo stops feel rushed.

    Private tours

    A private driver, a private vehicle and a flexible schedule cost more per person for couples and less per person for families of four. You choose departure times, stop where you like and change the plan mid-route. Our full list of private tours from Marrakech shows how the pricing scales with group size.

    Fully independent travel

    Buses and trains reach most cities cheaply. Yet no public transport serves the desert route efficiently, so independent travellers usually still hire a car or join a tour for that section.

    Our honest recommendation

    Book the city independently and the long routes privately. That mix produces the best value for most Marrakech holidays. Browse the wider catalogue of Morocco tours, or start from tours from Fes and tours from Tangier if your flights land elsewhere.

    Marrakech Holidays by Traveller Type

    Couples

    Choose a small riad with a private terrace. Add a spa hammam, a rooftop dinner and one night in the desert. Four to six days works beautifully.

    Families with children

    Pick a hotel with a pool and take shorter excursions. Ourika, Agafay and Ouzoud all suit children. Moroccans adore children, so expect warm attention everywhere.

    Groups of friends

    Rent a whole riad, which often costs less per head than separate rooms. Then split days between the souks, quad bikes and a camp night.

    Solo travellers

    Marrakech works well solo, especially in riads where breakfast becomes social. Solo women should expect some street attention, though it usually stops at words. Confident, brief refusals work best.

    Older travellers

    Medina lanes are uneven and often crowded. Consequently, a hotel in Hivernage with taxi access can make the trip far more comfortable.

    Luxury travellers

    Palace hotels, private guides, helicopter transfers to the Atlas and dinner in a luxury desert camp all exist. Marrakech holidays at this level compete with anywhere in the Mediterranean.

    Money, Connectivity and Getting Around

    Cash and cards

    The dirham is closed, so change money on arrival. Cards work in hotels, larger restaurants and modern shops. However, souks, taxis and street food remain cash businesses. Carry small notes, because change is often scarce.

    SIM cards and data

    Maroc Telecom, Orange and Inwi sell tourist SIM cards at the airport and in city shops. Bring your passport. Data plans cost only a few euros for the week. Coverage stays strong in the cities and along main roads, although it fades in the deep desert and on high mountain passes.

    Taxis

    Petit taxis are beige in Marrakech and carry three passengers. Ask for the meter. Grand taxis handle longer routes and airport runs for a fixed price. Agree the fare first, always.

    Walking and orientation

    You will walk more than you expect. Bring comfortable shoes with a solid sole, since the medina paving is uneven. Save your riad’s location offline, because signal drops between high walls.

    Language

    Moroccan Arabic and Amazigh dominate daily life, while French serves business and tourism. Spanish helps in the north. English spreads quickly among younger guides. Our article on the language spoken in Morocco gives useful phrases.

    Safety, Health and Common Scams

    General safety

    Marrakech ranks as a safe destination, and most Marrakech holidays pass without any incident. Violent crime against visitors stays rare, and tourist police patrol the main areas. Nevertheless, pickpockets work crowded squares, so keep bags closed.

    The scams you will meet

    Someone may tell you a street is closed, then guide you elsewhere for money. Another may lead you to a tannery and demand a fee. Henna artists sometimes grab a hand and then charge. A polite but firm “no, thank you” solves nearly all of it.

    Health basics

    No vaccinations are required for entry. Pharmacies are excellent and pharmacists give solid advice. Bring sun protection year-round, because the altitude and dry air burn skin faster than expected.

    Travel insurance

    Take a policy that covers hiking, camel riding and quad bikes. Standard policies sometimes exclude them.

    Useful contacts

    Dial 19 for police in cities and 15 for ambulance services. Additionally, keep your riad’s phone number written on paper, since a dead battery in the medina creates problems.

    What to Wear and Local Etiquette

    Dress code in practice

    Morocco is relaxed, yet modest clothing earns easier interactions. Shoulders and knees covered works well for everyone in the medina. Swimwear belongs at the pool only. Our guide on what to wear in Morocco breaks it down by season, and our piece on traditional Moroccan clothing explains the djellaba, the caftan and the babouche.

    Photography manners

    Always ask before photographing people. Water sellers and snake charmers on the square expect payment, so agree the amount first.

    Greetings and small talk

    “Salam alaikum” opens doors, and “shukran” closes them warmly. Handshakes are common, though some people prefer a hand on the heart instead. Follow the lead of the person in front of you.

    Tipping

    Tipping is normal and appreciated. Leave 10 percent in restaurants, 20 to 50 dirhams per day for a driver, and a few coins for porters. Generosity is noticed, but so is being taken advantage of, so keep it proportional.

    Alcohol and Ramadan

    Licensed restaurants, hotels and supermarkets sell alcohol. During Ramadan, avoid eating, drinking or smoking in the street in daylight as a courtesy.

    Sample Itineraries for Marrakech Holidays

    Three days in the city

    Day one covers the medina, Bahia Palace and Jemaa el-Fna at night. Then, day two takes in Ben Youssef Madrasa, the souks and a hammam. Finally, day three visits Jardin Majorelle, then Gueliz for lunch and shopping.

    Five days with excursions

    Days one and two follow the city plan above. Day three heads to Ourika or Imlil. Next, day four rests by the pool and explores Le Jardin Secret. Finally, day five drives to Agafay for sunset and dinner.

    Seven days with the Sahara

    Days one and two cover the city. Days three to five run the desert loop through Ait Ben Haddou, Dades and Merzouga. Day six returns through the Draa or the gorges. Day seven closes with shopping and a spa. This structure makes the most complete of all short Marrakech holidays.

    Ten days across Morocco

    Add Fes and Chefchaouen after the desert, then fly home from Fes or Casablanca. Our trip to Morocco planner explains how the pieces connect, and our guide to flights to Morocco helps you pick open-jaw routes.

    Ten Mistakes to Avoid

    Planning and logistics

    First, do not underestimate driving distances. Second, do not book a riad without checking how far it sits from a taxi-accessible gate. Third, do not schedule an excursion on your arrival day, because flights slip.

    Money and shopping

    Fourth, do not change large sums at the airport. Fifth, do not buy a rug in the first hour. Sixth, do not accept unsolicited “guides” in the street.

    Comfort and health

    Seventh, do not travel in August without a pool. Eighth, do not skip the hat and sunscreen. Ninth, do not walk the medina in new shoes.

    Expectations

    Tenth, do not expect silence. Marrakech is loud, busy and alive. Accept that rhythm and the city gives you everything.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Marrakech Holidays

    How many days should I spend in Marrakech?

    Three days cover the city comfortably. Five days allow two excursions. Seven days let you add the Sahara without rushing.

    Are Marrakech holidays expensive?

    No. Mid-range travellers typically spend 90 to 150 euros per person per day, excluding flights. That figure sits below most European city breaks of similar quality.

    Is Marrakech safe for tourists?

    Yes, it is generally safe. Petty theft and persistent touts are the main annoyances. Standard precautions handle both.

    What is the best month to visit?

    April, May, October and November offer the best balance of temperature and daylight. March and September also work well.

    Can I visit the Sahara in three days?

    Yes. A three-day round trip to Merzouga is possible, though four days feels far more comfortable.

    Do I need a visa?

    Most European, British, American, Canadian, Australian and Latin American passport holders enter visa-free for up to ninety days.

    Is Marrakech suitable for families?

    Absolutely. Children are welcomed everywhere. Choose a pool, keep excursions short and plan afternoon rests.

    What should women wear?

    Loose trousers, long skirts or dresses with covered shoulders work well. A scarf helps with sun and mosque courtyards alike.

    Is alcohol available?

    Yes, in licensed restaurants, hotels and some supermarkets. It is not sold in the souks.

    Which airport should I use?

    Marrakech Menara serves the city directly. Casablanca works for long-haul flights, with a three-hour train connection.

    Should I book excursions in advance?

    Yes, in high season. Good private drivers fill their calendars weeks ahead, especially around Easter and New Year.

    What currency should I bring?

    Bring euros, pounds or dollars and exchange them locally, since the dirham cannot be bought easily abroad.
