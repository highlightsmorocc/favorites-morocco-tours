    Royal Theatre of Rabat & Morocco’s Best Theatres

    Royal Theatre of Rabat guide : Zaha Hadid design 1,800 seats tickets on Events.ma plus Tangier's Cervantes Casablanca and Marrakech dinner shows

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 7 October 2026

    Quick answer: the Royal Theatre of Rabat is a Zaha Hadid-designed performing arts centre on the Bouregreg River, with a 1,800-seat main hall and a 7,000-seat open-air amphitheatre. It was officially inaugurated on 29 October 2024 and opened to the public on 22 April 2026. Tickets are sold through Events.ma. It is now Morocco’s leading theatre.

    Summary in 30 seconds

    • The Royal Theatre of Rabat was designed by Zaha Hadid Architects.
    • Its curves are inspired by the Bouregreg River and Arabic calligraphy.
    • Capacity: 1,800 seats inside, plus a 7,000-seat amphitheatre by the river.
    • Official inauguration: 29 October 2024. Public opening: 22 April 2026.
    • Tickets: through the official website, which links to Events.ma.
    • Other key theatres in Morocco: the Mohammed V National Theatre in Rabat (1962), the Gran Teatro Cervantes in Tangier (1913) and the Royal Theatre of Marrakech.
    • The Grand Theatre of Casablanca was finished in 2019 but was still closed in May 2026.
    • The oldest theatre in Morocco is open-air: the halqa storytelling circles of Jemaa el-Fna, listed by Unesco.

    Why the Royal Theatre of Rabat matters

    For decades, Morocco had no major modern opera house. The Royal Theatre of Rabat changes that. It is one of the last large projects conceived by Zaha Hadid’s studio and one of the most striking cultural buildings in Africa.

    Moreover, it sits at the heart of Rabat’s new riverside district. Within a short walk you will find the Hassan Tower, the Mausoleum of Mohammed V and the new Mohammed VI Tower, the tallest building in Morocco. Together they form one of the most impressive skylines in North Africa.

    The design: a river and a line of calligraphy

    Seen from the river, the building looks like a single flowing form. Zaha Hadid’s team drew on two local ideas: the winding course of the Bouregreg River and the fluid strokes of Arabic calligraphy. In addition, there are echoes of Islamic geometry in the patterns of the façade and interior.

    Inside, the main hall was designed for natural acoustics, so symphonic concerts can be performed without amplification. The orchestra pit also allows full opera and ballet productions, which Morocco could not easily host before.

    The opening night of 22 April 2026

    The public opening was a celebration of Moroccan talent. A cast of 76 musicians and 40 choir members took the stage under conductor and pianist Dina Bensaïd. For the first time, the Philharmonic Orchestra of Morocco and the Royal Symphony Orchestra performed together.

    The programme mixed classical piano, opera arias, Arab-Andalusian music and new compositions with the oud. Princesses Lalla Khadija, Lalla Meryem and Lalla Hasnaa attended, together with France’s first lady, Brigitte Macron.

    What’s on and how to buy tickets

    The official website lists a varied autumn 2026 programme. For example, Iraqi star Kadim Al Sahir performs on 9 October, an orchestral evening of Elgar, Tchaikovsky and Wagner follows on 19 October, and comedian Mo Amer appears on 31 October.

    How to book

    1. Open the official Royal Theatre of Rabat website.
    2. Choose your show and click “Book”.
    3. Complete the purchase on Events.ma.
    4. Keep your e-ticket on your phone and arrive 30 minutes early.

    Concerts by major Arab artists sell out fast. If you are travelling on fixed dates, book your seats before your hotel.

    Practical tips

    • Dress code: smart casual; people dress up for galas and premieres.
    • Language: concerts, opera and dance work for everyone; plays are often in Darija, Arabic or French.
    • Time zone: since 20 September 2026 Morocco has stayed on GMT all year. In winter Rabat matches London time.
    • Ramadan: during the fasting month, shows usually start after the iftar meal, around 9:30pm.

    Getting there and what to combine

    The Royal Theatre of Rabat is a short taxi ride from the medina. Rabat is an easy stop on a tour: Al Boraq high-speed trains link it to Tangier in about 1 hour 20 minutes, and Casablanca is around an hour away.

    Make a day of it with these nearby sights:

    • Hassan Tower and the Mausoleum of Mohammed V: across the river.
    • Mohammed VI Tower: 250 metres high, with a public observation deck; see our Mohammed VI Tower guide.
    • Kasbah of the Udayas: blue-and-white lanes above the river mouth.
    • Rabat medina: calm and easy to explore.

    Other theatres in Morocco worth knowing

    The Royal Theatre of Rabat is the star, but it is not the only stage in the country. Here are the other theatres in Morocco that every culture lover should know.

    Mohammed V National Theatre, Rabat

    Opened in 1962, it was the first major theatre built after Moroccan independence. It stands in central Rabat near the medina and is home to the national theatre company, as well as concerts and dance.

    Gran Teatro Cervantes, Tangier

    Built between 1911 and 1913 by Manuel Peña and Esperanza Orellana, this Art Nouveau theatre was once one of the great stages of North Africa. After decades of neglect, a 2019 agreement saw Morocco take over its restoration while keeping its Spanish name. By November 2025 the work was over 80% complete, with a budget of 25.67 million dirhams, and reopening was planned for 2026. It will seat just over 750. Check its status before you visit.

    Arriving by sea? Read our guide to the ferry from Spain to Morocco. Nearby Tetouan also keeps old Spanish-era cinema-theatres; see our Tetouan Morocco guide.

    Grand Theatre of Casablanca

    Designed by Casablanca-born architect Christian de Portzamparc, it has an 1,800-seat hall and a 600-seat modular hall, and cost about 1.44 billion dirhams. Work finished in 2019. Even so, in May 2026 Moroccan press still described it as closed, with no programme or opening date. You can admire its white, medina-inspired architecture from outside.

    Royal Theatre of Marrakech

    Designed by Charles Boccara, it stands on Avenue Mohammed VI between Gueliz and Hivernage. Its exterior and open-air theatre opened in 2001 after about 25 years of construction. The 800-seat opera hall inside was never finished, but the domed building hosts occasional concerts and exhibitions.

    Morocco’s oldest theatre: the halqa of Jemaa el-Fna

    Long before European-style theatres, Morocco had its own stage: the halqa, a circle of spectators around a storyteller, musician or acrobat. Its greatest venue is Jemaa el-Fna square in Marrakech.

    Unesco proclaimed the square’s cultural space a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2001, and inscribed it on its Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2008. It performs every evening, and it is free.

    • Storytellers: tales in Darija, full of drama and pauses.
    • Gnawa musicians: metal castanets and the guembri lute.
    • Acrobats and dancers: heirs of old travelling troupes.

    If you take photos, leave a tip of 10-20 MAD; see our guide to tipping in Morocco.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who designed the Royal Theatre of Rabat?

    Zaha Hadid Architects. The shape is inspired by the Bouregreg River and Arabic calligraphy.

    When did the Royal Theatre of Rabat open?

    It was officially inaugurated on 29 October 2024 and opened to the public on 22 April 2026.

    How many seats does the Royal Theatre of Rabat have?

    1,800 in the main hall, plus a 7,000-seat open-air amphitheatre and a smaller Petit Théâtre.

    How do I buy tickets for the Royal Theatre of Rabat?

    From the official website, which links to Events.ma for each show.

    Can I visit the Royal Theatre of Rabat without a ticket?

    You can admire the exterior and the riverside area at any time. Inside access is mainly for ticketed shows; check the official site for visits.

    What is the biggest theatre in Morocco?

    By floor area, the Grand Theatre of Casablanca, but it is still closed. Among open venues, the Royal Theatre of Rabat.

    Is the Gran Teatro Cervantes in Tangier open?

    Its restoration was in the final stage, with reopening planned for 2026. Check before you go; the façade can always be seen.

    Why is the Grand Theatre of Casablanca closed?

    Construction ended in 2019, but in May 2026 it still had no programme or announced opening date.

    Is there a theatre in Marrakech?

    Yes: the Royal Theatre of Marrakech hosts occasional events, and Jemaa el-Fna square offers free street theatre every evening.

    What is the best dinner show in Marrakech?

    For families, the Chez Ali fantasia; for couples, a palace dinner with live music in the medina.

    Key takeaways

    • The Royal Theatre of Rabat, by Zaha Hadid Architects, opened to the public on 22 April 2026.
    • It has 1,800 seats and a 7,000-seat amphitheatre; tickets via Events.ma.
    • Tangier’s Gran Teatro Cervantes (1913) is close to reopening after restoration.
    • The Grand Theatre of Casablanca remains closed despite being completed in 2019.
    • Jemaa el-Fna’s halqa is Morocco’s oldest, free, open-air theatre.
    • A Marrakech dinner show is the easiest night of performance for visitors.

    Conclusion: add a show to your Morocco trip

    The Royal Theatre of Rabat marks a new chapter for culture in Morocco. Combined with the restored Cervantes in Tangier and the timeless street theatre of Marrakech, it makes 2026 a great year for a cultural trip.
