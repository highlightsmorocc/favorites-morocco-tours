    Morocco Highlights Tour, Explained: Itinerary, Prices, Luxury & VIP

    Morocco highlights tour: 7 to 15-day private routes, imperial cities, Sahara nights, 2026 prices and what luxury and VIP really include.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 29 September 2026

    A Morocco highlights tour is the trip most first-time visitors are really looking for, even when they cannot name it yet. You want the imperial cities, one night on the dunes, the Atlas passes, a blue town and a kasbah that looks like a film set. In short, you want the country’s greatest hits in one clean loop, without backtracking.

    This guide explains how that trip works. Moreover, it covers what changes on a luxury Morocco highlights tour, and what genuine VIP service adds on the ground. We wrote it from years of driving these roads with our guests at Favorites Morocco Tours, so the details reflect real timings rather than brochure copy.

    What a Morocco Highlights Tour Actually Means

    The phrase sounds vague at first. In practice, however, it has a fairly precise meaning in the Moroccan travel trade.

    The working definition

    A Morocco highlights tour links at least two imperial cities with the Sahara, crossing the High Atlas at least once. Most versions run seven to fifteen days. Furthermore, they almost always finish in a different city from where they started, which saves an entire day of driving.

    How it differs from a desert-only trip

    A three-day desert run from Marrakech is wonderful. Still, it skips Fes, Rabat, Volubilis and the coast entirely. A proper private Sahara desert tour answers one question. A highlights route answers the whole country.

    How it differs from a city break

    For example, four days in Marrakech gives you the souks, the gardens and the food. Yet you never sleep under the stars. Consequently, most travellers who start with a city break come back for the full loop.

    Who books this trip

    Couples on a first visit book it most often. Besides them, we see multigenerational families, groups of friends, and solo travellers who want a driver rather than a bus. Honeymooners tend to choose the luxury tier.

    Why 2026 Is a Particularly Good Year to Book

    Timing matters more than usual right now. Several things changed in Morocco during 2025 and 2026, and all of them affect how a highlights route feels.

    Visitor numbers hit record levels

    Morocco welcomed roughly 9.4 million visitors in the first half of 2026, about 6% more than in 2025. In addition, travel receipts reached around 53.8 billion dirhams by the end of May, up 14.6%. Therefore, the best riads and desert camps sell out earlier than before.

    Marrakech airport is being rebuilt around you

    Meanwhile, ONDA awarded roughly $440 million in contracts at Marrakech Menara and Agadir. For instance, at Menara, capacity climbs from about 7.4 million passengers to 16 million, with terminals one and two reaching some 142,000 square metres by 2028. Expect construction hoardings on arrival, and allow extra time.

    The clock changed permanently

    Morocco dropped GMT+1 and adopted permanent GMT on 20 September 2026, so sunset now falls roughly an hour earlier on the clock. This matters on a highlights route, because your camel ride into the dunes starts earlier. Our article on what time it is in Morocco now explains the change in detail.

    The high-speed rail network keeps growing

    In fact, Al Boraq already links Tangier to Kenitra and Casablanca. In addition, the Kenitra–Marrakech extension passed 30% completion in May 2026 and targets delivery in 2029. For now, though, a private vehicle still beats the train on any Morocco highlights tour, because the mountain and desert sections have no rail line.

    Luxury supply is expanding fast

    The Ritz-Carlton opened its first Moroccan property in Rabat, and Marriott has announced more openings through 2027. Likewise, Royal Mansour now runs houses in Marrakech, Casablanca and Tamuda Bay. Because of that, a luxury Morocco highlights tour has far more five-star anchors than five years ago.

    The Places a Morocco Highlights Tour Should Include

    Not every stop deserves a night. Some deserve two. Below is our honest ranking, based on what guests actually rate highly afterwards.

    Marrakech

    Marrakech anchors most routes. The Koutoubia minaret, the Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs and Jemaa el-Fna form the classic circuit. The Majorelle Garden then fills a relaxed second morning. Our complete Marrakech travel guide covers the city street by street, while the old town and new town guide explains how the medina and Gueliz differ.

    Fes

    Fes el-Bali holds the oldest continuously inhabited medina in the Arab world. Al-Qarawiyyin, founded in 859, still works as a mosque and university. Moreover, the Chouara tanneries remain a working site rather than a museum piece. Therefore, give Fes a full day with a local guide.

    Rabat

    Rabat is quieter, greener and often skipped. That is a mistake. The Kasbah of the Udayas, the Hassan Tower and the Chellah necropolis sit close together, and the city holds UNESCO World Heritage status. Read more in our piece on whether Marrakech is the capital of Morocco.

    Meknes and Volubilis

    Moreover, Volubilis preserves the best Roman mosaics in North Africa. Meanwhile, Moulay Idriss sits on the hill above, and Meknes offers Bab Mansour. These three fit into one day between Fes and Rabat.

    Chefchaouen

    The blue town needs a detour from the north, so it appears mainly on twelve-day and fifteen-day routes. Nevertheless, guests who include it rarely regret it. Our Chefchaouen guide explains when the light is best for photographs.

    Ait Ben Haddou

    This fortified ksar has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987. Films from Lawrence of Arabia to Gladiator used it. Cross the river early, before the coach groups arrive from Marrakech. If you only have a day, our Ait Ben Haddou day trip works well.

    Dades and Todra gorges

    The Dades switchbacks photograph beautifully in late afternoon. Similarly, Todra’s limestone walls rise around 300 metres above a narrow river road. Both sit on the natural driving line between Ouarzazate and Merzouga, so they cost you almost nothing in extra time.

    Merzouga and Erg Chebbi

    Above all, Erg Chebbi holds the tallest accessible dunes in Morocco, reaching roughly 150 metres. Camels leave about ninety minutes before sunset, and dinner follows at camp. Our comparison of Erg Chebbi and Erg Chigaga helps you pick between the two great ergs, and the Merzouga camel trekking guide covers the ride itself.

    The High Atlas

    Tizi n’Tichka tops out at roughly 2,260 metres, on a road opened in 1936 along an old caravan track. For a closer look at the range, see our Atlas Mountains guide. Short walks around Imlil or the Ourika Valley suit travellers who want mountain air without a trek.

    Essaouira and the Atlantic

    By contrast, Essaouira brings wind, gulls, blue boats and grilled sardines, with ramparts framing the ocean. Add it as an Essaouira day trip, or build it into a longer loop.

    Casablanca

    However, Casablanca works best as an arrival or departure city. The Hassan II Mosque, however, justifies a stop alone. In fact, its minaret stands 210 metres tall, and non-Muslim visitors may join guided tours inside.

    How Long Should Your Morocco Highlights Tour Be?

    Length decides everything else. Choose it before you choose hotels.

    Five days

    In short, five days covers Marrakech, the Atlas, Ait Ben Haddou and one desert night. You will not reach Fes comfortably. Instead, treat this as a taster. See our five days in Morocco plan or the 5-day Marrakech to Sahara tour.

    Seven days

    However, seven days is the shortest genuine Morocco highlights tour. In other words, you link Marrakech, the desert and Fes, with the gorges in between. Our 7-day Morocco itinerary lays out the pacing, and the 7-day private tour from Marrakech shows the tour version.

    Eight to nine days

    Above all, this is our most requested length, and for good reason. By contrast, nine days lets you add Rabat, Meknes and Volubilis without rushing. Compare the 8-day private tour from Casablanca and the 9-day private tour.

    Ten to twelve days

    Ten days adds Essaouira or a second desert night. Twelve days brings Chefchaouen within reach. Look at our 10-day Morocco itinerary, the 10-day tour from Casablanca and the 12-day Marrakech to Sahara route.

    Fourteen to fifteen days

    Two weeks covers the north, the Atlantic and the deep south together. Searches for a “14 day highlights of Morocco tour” usually mean this. Our 15-day tour from Casablanca and 15-day tour from Tangier both fit that brief.

    Which start city?

    Casablanca suits long-haul arrivals and gives easy access to Rabat. Marrakech suits European short-haul. Tangier suits ferry arrivals from Spain. Fes suits travellers who want the medina first. Browse tours from Marrakech, from Casablanca, from Fes and from Tangier.

    The Classic Nine-Day Morocco Highlights Tour, Day by Day

    Here is the route we run most often. Distances are real, and so are the driving times.

    Day 1: Arrive Casablanca

    Land, meet your driver and transfer to the hotel. Afterwards, visit the Hassan II Mosque if your flight lands early. Dinner beside the corniche closes the day gently. Total driving: under an hour.

    Day 2: Casablanca to Rabat to Fes

    Leave after breakfast. In Rabat, walk the Kasbah of the Udayas and stop at the Hassan Tower. Continue to Fes in the afternoon. Driving time runs about four and a half hours in total, including stops.

    Day 3: Fes medina

    Spend the full day inside Fes el-Bali with a licensed local guide. See Al-Qarawiyyin, the Bou Inania Madrasa, the Nejjarine fountain and the tanneries. Additionally, allow an hour for a workshop visit. The medina has thousands of lanes, so a guide saves both time and temper.

    Day 4: Fes to Merzouga

    This is the long driving day, roughly seven hours with stops. You cross Ifrane, the cedar forest at Azrou, the Ziz gorges and the palm valley. Then you reach the dunes in time for the camel trek. Dinner and drums follow at camp.

    Day 5: Merzouga to Dades

    Watch sunrise over Erg Chebbi first. Afterwards, drive west through Rissani, Tinghir and the Todra Gorge. You reach the Dades Valley by late afternoon. Driving: about five and a half hours.

    Day 6: Dades to Ait Ben Haddou to Marrakech

    Follow the Road of a Thousand Kasbahs to Ouarzazate. Visit Ait Ben Haddou before lunch, then cross Tizi n’Tichka. You arrive in Marrakech in the evening. This day runs about six hours of driving.

    Day 7: Marrakech monuments

    Explore the Koutoubia, the Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs and the souks. In the evening, Jemaa el-Fna fills with food stalls and musicians. Our trip to Morocco planner covers the evening options too.

    Day 8: Marrakech at your own pace

    Choose gardens, a hammam, a cooking class or the Agafay desert. Many guests book a traditional hammam on this day. Others prefer the Agafay quad and camel experience in the late afternoon.

    Day 9: Departure

    Transfer to Menara Airport. Because of the ongoing terminal works, we now recommend arriving three hours before long-haul departures.

    The Twelve-Day Extended Morocco Highlights Tour

    Twelve days lets the trip breathe. Consequently, nothing feels rushed.

    What gets added

    • Chefchaouen for one night, reached from Fes or Tangier
    • Meknes and Volubilis as a separate half-day rather than a rushed stop
    • A second night at Erg Chebbi, which allows a sunrise and a sunset
    • Essaouira on the Atlantic for two nights
    • Skoura’s palm oasis and the Valley of Roses

    Who should choose twelve days

    Photographers benefit most, since light matters more to them than distance. See our 12-day tour from Tangier for the northern version.

    Luxury Morocco Highlights Tour: What Actually Changes

    The word luxury gets used loosely. Here is what it changes in practice on a Morocco highlights tour.

    Where you sleep in the cities

    A standard tour books good three-star or four-star riads. A luxury tour books restored palace riads with six to twelve suites, plunge pools and a private roof terrace. In Marrakech, that means the Kasbah or the Mouassine quarter. In Fes, it means a dar near Batha with a courtyard fountain.

    Where you sleep in the desert

    The gap is widest here. A standard camp gives you a canvas tent, a shared bathroom block and a mattress on a rug. A luxury desert camp gives you an en-suite tent of 40 to 60 square metres, a proper bed, hot water, solar power, a dressing area and a private terrace facing the dunes. Dinner also comes to a laid table rather than a buffet.

    The vehicle

    Standard tours use a clean minivan or a Land Cruiser shared with your own party. Luxury tours use a newer 4×4 or a Mercedes V-Class with leather seats, cool boxes, phone chargers and, importantly, a second driver on the long days.

    The guide

    Every medina should be walked with a licensed local guide. On a luxury itinerary, though, you also get a national guide travelling with you throughout, plus specialists where they add value. An architectural historian in Fes changes the day completely.

    The food

    Luxury means fewer set menus and more choice. It also means table reservations at places that need booking weeks ahead. Additionally, dietary needs get handled properly, which matters for vegetarian, halal-only, gluten-free and allergy-sensitive travellers. Our guide to traditional Moroccan dishes helps you order confidently.

    The spa and wellness layer

    Hammam rituals, argan-oil massages and hotel spas turn a driving-heavy route into a restorative one. Book the hammam for the evening after the desert. Your muscles will thank you.

    What luxury does not buy

    Luxury does not shorten the drive from Fes to Merzouga. It does not empty the Fes medina. Nor does it make August cool. Be honest with yourself about these limits, because a good agency will be honest with you about them too.

    VIP Service: What It Really Means on the Ground

    VIP is the most abused word in travel marketing. Nevertheless, it does describe a real set of services in Morocco. Here is what a VIP Morocco highlights tour includes when the operator is serious.

    VIP airport meet and greet

    An agent meets you at the aircraft door or immigration hall, handles the paperwork queue and walks you to a lounge, while your luggage arrives separately. The service exists at Casablanca, Marrakech, Fes and Tangier. Given the construction at Menara, it earns its cost right now.

    Fast-track and lounge access

    VIP handling shortens passport control and security. On departure, you clear formalities from a private salon. For families with small children, this single upgrade often justifies the whole VIP tier.

    Private and out-of-hours access

    Some sites open early or stay open late for private groups. A dawn visit to Ait Ben Haddou before the coaches arrive changes the photographs entirely. Similarly, a private after-hours tour of a museum removes the crowds.

    VIP in the desert

    At Erg Chebbi, VIP means a private camp reserved for your party alone, and a 4×4 dune transfer instead of a camel if you prefer. Additionally, expect dinner set on the sand, a telescope for the night sky and breakfast at a dune crest.

    A dedicated concierge

    A single contact answers your messages throughout the trip. That person reconfirms every hotel, adjusts timings when you sleep late, and fixes problems before you notice them. In our experience, this is the most valuable VIP element of all, and the least visible.

    Helicopters, private aviation and yachts

    These exist and we can arrange them. Helicopter transfers link Marrakech with Essaouira or the Agafay, and private aviation connects Marrakech and Merzouga in under ninety minutes. Still, such movements depend on weather and authorisation, so build in flexibility.

    What VIP does not mean

    VIP does not mean skipping the dress and behaviour codes at religious sites. It does not mean photographing people without asking. Above all, it does not mean treating staff as invisible. Real VIP travel in Morocco looks calm and courteous, never loud.

    What a Morocco Highlights Tour Costs

    Prices vary by season, group size and tier. The ranges below reflect 2026 bookings for a private tour with driver, per person, two people sharing.

    Indicative price bands

    • Standard, 8 days: roughly €750 to €1,100 per person
    • Luxury, 8 days: roughly €1,800 to €3,200 per person
    • VIP, 8 days: from about €4,000 per person upward
    • Luxury, 12 days: roughly €2,900 to €4,800 per person

    What is normally included

    Expect private transport with fuel and driver, accommodation with breakfast, desert dinner and breakfast, camel trekking, and local guides in the medinas. Additionally, most operators include bottled water in the vehicle.

    What is normally excluded

    Flights, lunches, monument entry fees, drinks and tips usually sit outside the price. Ask for a written list, because vague inclusions cause most disputes later.

    How group size changes the maths

    A private tour for two costs far more per head than the same tour for six. Consequently, families and friend groups often reach the luxury tier for the price of a standard couple’s trip. See also our comparison of private versus group tours in Morocco.

    Tipping guidance

    Tipping is customary but never aggressive. As a rough guide, allow 100 to 150 dirhams per day for a driver-guide, 50 to 100 dirhams for a city guide, and 20 to 50 dirhams for hotel staff. Keep small notes aside.

    The Best Time for a Morocco Highlights Tour

    Season affects comfort more than availability. Morocco runs all year, yet the experience shifts a lot.

    Spring, March to May

    This is the strongest window. Wildflowers cover the Middle Atlas, the Valley of Roses blooms in late April, and desert nights stay pleasant. However, Easter weeks book out early.

    Autumn, September to November

    Autumn rivals spring. October in particular offers warm days and cool evenings. Remember, though, that permanent GMT now brings sunset forward, so plan dune arrivals earlier. Our best time to visit Morocco article breaks this down month by month.

    Winter, December to February

    Cities stay mild while the desert turns cold at night, sometimes near freezing. Snow closes Atlas passes occasionally. On the other hand, light is superb and crowds thin out. See our December tours guide and the New Year in Morocco article.

    Summer, June to August

    Inland heat can exceed 45°C. Therefore, we redesign summer routes around the coast, the mountains and early starts. Essaouira and Imlil become the anchors instead of the deep south. Our September weather guide covers the shoulder.

    Ramadan and religious holidays

    Ramadan changes rhythms rather than access. Restaurants adapt, and evenings turn festive. Ask us for exact dates when you enquire, since they move each year. Our article on religion in Morocco gives useful background.

    Getting to and Around Your Morocco Highlights Tour

    Access has improved sharply. Morocco’s airports handled more than 22 million passengers in the first seven months of 2026 alone.

    Flights

    Casablanca Mohammed V takes most long-haul traffic. Marrakech Menara, named Africa’s best regional airport by Skytrax in 2026, handles the bulk of European arrivals. Our guides to flights to Morocco and flights to Marrakech cover routes and booking timing.

    Ferries from Spain

    Tarifa to Tangier Ville takes about an hour. Algeciras to Tangier Med takes roughly ninety minutes. As a result, combined Spain and Morocco itineraries work well, especially in summer.

    Trains and roads

    Al Boraq links Tangier, Rabat and Casablanca quickly, while conventional ONCF trains connect Fes, Meknes and Marrakech.

    Entry requirements

    Many nationalities, including EU, UK, US, Canadian and Australian citizens, enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Others use the electronic authorisation system. Always check with your nearest Moroccan consulate before booking, since rules change. Our summary of Morocco travel advisories puts safety guidance in context.

    Packing for a Morocco Highlights Tour

    You will move between 40°C afternoons and 5°C desert dawns within the same week. Layers solve almost everything.

    Clothing essentials

    • Loose trousers or long skirts in natural fabrics
    • Tops that cover the shoulders for medina days
    • One warm fleece or jacket for the desert night, even in June
    • A scarf, useful for sun, dust and mosque courtyards
    • Broken-in walking shoes, since medina cobbles punish new soles

    Practical extras

    Bring a power bank, a European two-pin adaptor, sunglasses, high-factor sunscreen and lip balm. Additionally, pack medication in its original packaging. Our detailed guide on what to wear in Morocco answers the dress-code questions in full, while traditional Moroccan clothing explains what locals actually wear.

    Connectivity

    Local SIM cards and eSIMs are cheap and fast. Coverage is good along the main routes, and patchy in the dunes. See our notes on SIM cards in Morocco.

    Eating Well on a Morocco Highlights Tour

    Food is a highlight in its own right, so plan for it rather than around it.

    Dishes worth seeking out

    Try tagine of course, but go further. Look for pastilla in Fes, tangia in Marrakech, grilled sardines in Essaouira, and medfouna in the Rissani area. Furthermore, ask your guide where drivers eat. That question rarely fails.

    Breakfast, lunch and dinner rhythms

    Breakfast is bread, olive oil, amlou, eggs and mint tea. Lunch runs long and late. Dinner starts after 20:00 in cities. Adjust your expectations, and you will eat better. Our article on cuisine in Morocco goes deeper.

    Water and stomachs

    Drink bottled or filtered water, and eat at busy places, because turnover means freshness.

    Culture, Etiquette and Simple Respect

    Small courtesies transform how Morocco treats you back.

    Greetings and language

    Arabic and Amazigh are official languages, while French serves business and tourism. Spanish still helps in the north. Learn salam, shukran and la shukran. People notice immediately. Our guide on languages spoken in Morocco explains the landscape.

    Photography manners

    Ask before photographing people, especially women and shopkeepers. In markets, buying something small usually turns a refusal into a smile.

    Shopping and bargaining

    Bargaining is normal, friendly and expected in souks. Start around half the opening price, then meet nearer the middle. Fixed-price cooperatives exist too, and their prices are already fair.

    Amazigh heritage

    Much of the route crosses Amazigh-speaking regions. Around a quarter of Moroccans speak an Amazigh language, and Yennayer became a public holiday in 2023. Our article on Berber Morocco covers this properly.

    Health, Safety and Practical Comfort

    Morocco is a settled country with a visible tourism police presence. Nonetheless, sensible habits still apply.

    Everyday safety

    Watch bags in crowded souks and use hotel safes. Agree taxi fares in advance, or ask for the meter. Above all, use licensed guides rather than street offers.

    Altitude, heat and long drives

    Tizi n’Tichka is high but not dangerous, and heat is the real summer risk. Drink more than you think you need, and break long drives with tea stops.

    Travelling with children or older parents

    Both work well privately. Shorten the Fes to Merzouga day by overnighting in Midelt or Erfoud. Our family travel guide offers more ideas.

    Private Versus Group: Which Suits You?

    Nearly all our highlights routes run privately, and there are clear reasons why.

    The case for private

    You set departure times. Moreover, you stop for photographs when the light is right. Likewise, you skip a carpet shop if you want to. Moreover, a private vehicle makes the luxury and VIP tiers possible at all.

    The case for group

    Group departures cost less and build camaraderie. Still, fixed schedules are the trade-off.

    Photography on a Morocco Highlights Tour

    The route is unusually photogenic, so a little planning pays off.

    The five best light windows

    1. Sunrise over Erg Chebbi from a dune crest
    2. Late afternoon on the Dades switchbacks
    3. Early morning at Ait Ben Haddou, before the crowds
    4. Blue hour on Jemaa el-Fna, from a terrace cafe
    5. Mid-morning in Chefchaouen’s upper lanes

    Gear notes

    Dust is the enemy, so change lenses inside the vehicle rather than on the dunes. A polariser also helps enormously with Atlas skies.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    These are the errors we see most often, and all of them are avoidable.

    Trying to do everything in seven days

    Adding Chefchaouen to a one-week route means eight hours of driving on two separate days. Cut a city instead, because depth beats distance.

    Booking the desert camp last

    The best camps at Erg Chebbi sell out months ahead in spring and autumn, so book that night first.

    Underestimating driving times

    Mountain roads are slow, and a 300-kilometre stretch can take six hours with stops. Plan accordingly, and the days feel generous.

    Ignoring the new sunset times

    Because Morocco now stays on GMT permanently, evening light disappears earlier than older guidebooks suggest. Shift camel treks and viewpoint stops earlier by about an hour.

    Choosing the cheapest quote

    A suspiciously low price usually means a shared vehicle, a distant camp and unpaid guides. Ask what a Morocco highlights tour includes, in writing.

    Frequently Asked Questions About the Morocco Highlights Tour

    How many days do I need for a Morocco highlights tour?

    Seven days is the practical minimum, and nine to twelve days is the sweet spot. Anything shorter forces a choice between Fes and the Sahara.

    Is a highlights of Morocco tour suitable for first-time visitors?

    Yes, and first-timers are precisely who it suits best. The route answers a first visit completely, so you leave with a sense of the country rather than one city.

    What makes a luxury Morocco highlights tour different?

    Accommodation, vehicles, guiding and food all move up a level. The desert camp changes most dramatically, from shared facilities to an en-suite tent with a private terrace.

    What does VIP include that luxury does not?

    VIP adds airport meet and greet, fast-track formalities, private or out-of-hours site access, a private desert camp and a dedicated 24-hour concierge. In short, luxury upgrades the things you see, while VIP upgrades the things you do not have to think about.

    Is Morocco safe for tourists in 2026?

    Yes. Morocco records millions of visitors each year with very low rates of serious crime against tourists. Petty theft in crowded souks is the main risk.

    What is the best month for this trip?

    April, May, October and early November are ideal, and March also works well. July and August suit coastal and mountain routes rather than the deep south.

    Can I do a Morocco highlights tour with children?

    Absolutely. Private vehicles and flexible timings make it straightforward, and camels, kasbahs and dunes delight children more than adults expect.

    Do I need a visa for Morocco?

    Most European, British, American, Canadian and Australian passport holders enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Nevertheless, always confirm with a Moroccan consulate before booking, because requirements change.

    How much should I budget in total?

    Allow roughly €900 to €1,200 per person for a standard eight-day private tour, €1,800 to €3,200 for luxury and €4,000 upward for VIP, plus flights, lunches, entries and tips.

    Is one night in the Sahara enough?

    One night works, and most itineraries include exactly that. Two nights, however, gives you a full day in the dunes plus a sunrise and a sunset.

    Where should my tour start and finish?

    Casablanca to Marrakech is the classic direction for a Morocco highlights tour, since it avoids repeating roads. Tangier to Marrakech suits ferry arrivals.

    Can you combine Morocco with Spain or Portugal?

    Yes, and many guests do. Ferries from Tarifa and Algeciras make the connection simple. Still, allow at least eight days in Morocco itself.

    What happens if the weather turns bad?

    Snow can close an Atlas pass in winter, and flash floods occasionally affect gorge roads. Your driver simply reroutes, which is why private travel beats fixed group departures.
