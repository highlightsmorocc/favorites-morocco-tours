    Moroccan African: Is Morocco in Africa? Complete Guide

    Moroccan African identity explained where Morocco sits in Africa Amazigh and Arab roots race questions the African Union AFCON 2025

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 8 September 2026

    Ask a search engine whether Moroccans are African, and you will find millions of people asking the same thing. The Moroccan African question fills forums, comment sections and travel groups every single day. Moreover, it comes up in taxis in Marrakech, in hostels in Chefchaouen and around campfires in Merzouga. So this guide answers it properly, with geography, history, genetics, football and honest cultural context.

    Morocco sits in the far northwest of Africa. Indeed, that fact is simple. However, the Moroccan African identity is layered, and those layers are exactly what make the country fascinating to visit. Below, you will find the short answer first, then the long one, and finally the places where you can see this story with your own eyes.

    Is Morocco in Africa? The short answer

    Yes. Morocco is a country in Africa. It occupies the northwestern corner of the continent, and it has never been anywhere else.

    Is Morocco a country in Africa, officially?

    Officially, absolutely. The Kingdom of Morocco is a sovereign African state. Furthermore, it is a member of the African Union, it competes in the Africa Cup of Nations, and it belongs to every major African economic body that fits its region.

    Is Moroccan African by nationality?

    Every Moroccan citizen is African by nationality and by geography. Therefore, when someone asks “is Moroccan African”, the honest answer is yes, without an asterisk. The debate people are really having is about culture, language and race, not about continents.

    Where is Morocco located in Africa?

    Morocco lies between roughly 21°N and 36°N latitude. It faces the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Mediterranean Sea to the north. Consequently, it is the closest African country to Europe, and the only one with two long coastlines on those two different bodies of water.

    Where is Morocco situated in Africa on a map?

    Find the top left corner of Africa. Morocco is that corner. Algeria sits to the east, Mauritania lies to the south, and Spain is visible across the water to the north. Our detailed article on where Morocco is breaks the map down region by region.

    Why does this question keep coming back?

    Three reasons drive it. First, Morocco looks close to Europe on a map. Second, many Moroccans speak Arabic and French rather than a language people associate with Africa. Third, popular images of Africa rarely include snow, ski lifts or Mediterranean beaches. Nevertheless, Morocco has all three.

    The geography behind the Moroccan African question

    In short, geography settles the debate quickly. Still, the details are worth knowing, because they explain why confusion exists at all.

    A country in the northwest corner of the continent

    In total, Morocco covers about 446,550 square kilometres in its internationally recognised borders. Additionally, it administers the Western Sahara territory to the south, which pushes the total area considerably higher. Either way, the whole landmass sits on the African continental plate.

    Fourteen kilometres from Europe

    At its narrowest point, the Strait of Gibraltar measures roughly 14 kilometres across. In fact, you can stand in Tangier and see Spanish hills clearly on a bright morning. However, proximity is not membership. Sicily sits close to Tunisia too, and nobody calls Sicily African.

    Two seas, one continent

    Overall, Morocco has about 3,500 kilometres of coastline. The Mediterranean side runs from Tangier east towards Saidia. Meanwhile, the Atlantic side runs south past Rabat, Casablanca, Essaouira and Agadir, all the way to Dakhla. That Atlantic coast is pure African seaboard.

    Land borders that are unmistakably African

    Morocco borders Algeria to the east and southeast. It also borders Mauritania in the far south, beyond the Western Sahara. Both neighbours are African states, and the shared frontier runs through desert that looks nothing like Europe.

    The Atlas Mountains as a spine

    The Atlas range crosses Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia across roughly 2,500 kilometres. Mount Toubkal rises to 4,167 metres, making it the highest peak in North Africa. Our full guide to the Atlas Mountains in Morocco covers the valleys, passes and trekking routes in detail.

    The Sahara begins here

    South and east of the Atlas, the Sahara takes over. Erg Chebbi near Merzouga and Erg Chigaga near M’hamid are the two great dune seas of Morocco. Both belong to the same desert that stretches to Sudan. If you want to compare them, read Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga.

    Africa’s oldest human story begins in Morocco

    Above all, here is the fact that ends most arguments. The oldest known fossils of our own species were found in Morocco, not in East Africa.

    Jebel Irhoud and 300,000 years of history

    In June 2017, a research team published findings from Jebel Irhoud, a site between Marrakech and Safi. The fossils were dated to roughly 315,000 years ago. Consequently, they pushed back the origin of Homo sapiens by about 100,000 years. The study appeared in Nature.

    What the discovery changed

    Before Jebel Irhoud, textbooks pointed to a single East African cradle. Afterwards, researchers described a pan-African origin instead. In other words, our species emerged across the whole continent, and Morocco was part of that process from the beginning.

    The Taforalt genomes

    Another Moroccan site changed the picture again. At Taforalt, in the Rif near Berkane, scientists sequenced DNA from individuals who lived about 15,000 years ago. The Max Planck Institute and Morocco’s INSAP published the results in Science in 2018.

    What the genetics actually say

    Roughly two thirds of that ancient ancestry matched populations of the Near East. Meanwhile, about one third matched sub-Saharan Africans, particularly West Africans. Therefore the Moroccan African gene pool has been mixed for at least fifteen millennia. You can read the summary from the Max Planck Institute.

    Why this matters for travellers

    It matters because it reframes the whole trip. You are not visiting a European country that happens to sit in Africa. Instead, you are visiting one of the oldest continuously inhabited corners of the African continent.

    Amazigh roots: the first Moroccan African identity

    In fact, long before Arabs, Romans or Phoenicians arrived, the Amazigh people lived here. They are the indigenous population of North Africa, and their story is the foundation of the Moroccan African identity.

    Who the Amazigh are

    “Amazigh” means free people, and the plural is “Imazighen”. The older term “Berber” comes from the Latin barbarus, so many people now avoid it. Our article on Berber culture in Morocco explains the history behind both words.

    How many Moroccans speak Amazigh

    The 2024 national census counted 36,828,330 inhabitants. Additionally, it found that 24.8% regularly speak an Amazigh variety. Tashelhit leads with 14.2%, followed by Central Atlas Tamazight at 7.4% and Tarifit at 3.2%.

    An official language since 2011

    The 2011 constitution made Tamazight an official language alongside Arabic. Since then, road signs, ministry logos and television channels have carried it. Our guide to the languages spoken in Morocco covers how these languages overlap in daily life.

    Tifinagh, an African script

    Tifinagh is the script used to write Tamazight. Its geometric letters descend from ancient Libyco-Berber writing. Furthermore, you will spot it on official buildings across the country, usually beside Arabic and French.

    Yennayer, a national holiday

    In 2023 the government made Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year, an official public holiday. The country celebrated it for the first time on 14 January 2024. In January 2026, Morocco entered Amazigh year 2976. Consequently, an indigenous African calendar now sits on the national calendar.

    Is Morocco Arab, African, or both?

    Admittedly, this is where most confusion lives. Fortunately, the answer is not complicated once you drop the either/or framing.

    Morocco joined the Arab League in 1958

    To begin with, Morocco belongs to the Arab League, and Arabic is an official language. Therefore Morocco is genuinely Arab in a political and linguistic sense. That membership, however, says nothing about geography.

    How Arabic arrived

    Arab armies and scholars reached North Africa from the late seventh century onwards. Language, religion and law spread gradually over centuries. Meanwhile, Amazigh communities kept their own tongues, especially in the mountains and the south.

    Darija is its own thing

    Notably, Moroccan Darija is not standard Arabic. It borrows heavily from Amazigh vocabulary, plus French, Spanish and older Mediterranean words. As a result, speakers from the Gulf often struggle to follow it.

    Both, not either

    Morocco is African by continent, Amazigh by root, Arab by language and faith, and Mediterranean by trade. All four are true at once. Consequently, the Moroccan African label is not in competition with the Arab one.

    What Moroccans say themselves

    A 2021 survey found that 68% of Moroccans identified as Arab, 25.6% as Amazigh, 3.6% as Sahrawi and 2.7% as other. Notably, almost nobody rejects being African. The identity people debate is ethnic, not continental.

    Are Moroccans Black? Talking about morocco race honestly

    “Are Moroccans Black” is one of the most searched questions about the country. Even so, it deserves a careful answer rather than a slogan.

    Morocco does not collect ethnic statistics

    The High Commission for Planning, the national statistics office, does not record ethnicity or race. Consequently, no official percentage exists for any group. Anyone quoting exact racial figures is guessing.

    The real range of appearances

    In practice, Moroccans range from very fair to very dark. A family in Tangier may look southern European. Meanwhile, a family in Zagora or Dakhla may look West African. Both families are equally Moroccan, and both belong to the same Moroccan African mosaic.

    Haratin and Gnawa communities

    In the oases of the Draa, Ziz and Tafilalet valleys, Haratin communities have farmed the palm groves for centuries. Separately, Gnawa communities descend largely from West Africans brought north across the Sahara. Both groups are central to the Moroccan African story.

    The morocco race question on official forms

    In the United States, Moroccans were long counted as White on federal forms. That changed in March 2024, when new federal standards added a Middle Eastern or North African category. The revision was published in the Federal Register.

    Why forms cause confusion

    For decades, official paperwork placed North Africans in a box that matched neither how they saw themselves nor how others saw them. Therefore many diaspora Moroccans grew up hearing that they were not really African. The paperwork was wrong; the continent was not.

    How to talk about it as a visitor

    Ask about region, city or family origin rather than race. “Where is your family from?” opens a warm conversation. By contrast, “what race are you?” tends to land badly. Moroccans discuss origins constantly, just in their own terms.

    The trans-Saharan link that built Moroccan African wealth

    In truth, Morocco did not simply sit beside Africa. For a thousand years, it was the northern terminal of the greatest trade network on the continent.

    Sijilmassa, the desert port

    Founded in the eighth century near modern Rissani, Sijilmassa controlled the caravan routes to the Sahel. Gold moved north. Salt, cloth and books moved south. As a result, the city grew rich enough to mint its own coins.

    Gold, salt and books

    West African gold financed Moroccan dynasties for centuries. Meanwhile, Saharan salt slabs travelled south to be traded almost weight for weight. Manuscripts moved in both directions, linking libraries in Fes with libraries in Timbuktu.

    Timbuktu and the campaign of 1591

    In 1591 a Saadian army crossed the Sahara and defeated the Songhai Empire at Tondibi. The campaign is remembered very differently on each side of the desert. Nevertheless, it shows how directly Morocco was involved in West African history.

    Ahmed Baba, scholar of two shores

    Ahmed Baba al-Timbukti, the great Timbuktu scholar, was deported to Marrakech after that conquest. He taught there for years before returning home. Today a research institute in Timbuktu carries his name.

    Where you can still see it

    Walk the ruins at Sijilmassa outside Rissani. Visit the Monday and Thursday souk there. Afterwards, watch the caravan route continue south into the dunes at Merzouga. Our guide to camel trekking in Merzouga follows the same tracks.

    Ait Ben Haddou, the caravan stop

    The ksar of Ait Ben Haddou guarded the route between Marrakech and the Sahara. UNESCO inscribed it on the World Heritage list in 1987. You can visit it on an Ait Ben Haddou day trip from Marrakech, or on any desert tour that crosses the Tizi n’Tichka pass.

    Gnawa: the Moroccan African sound

    If you want to hear the continent in Morocco, listen to Gnawa music. It is African heritage in its purest audible form.

    Where Gnawa comes from

    Gnawa traditions descend from West Africans brought across the Sahara, many from the regions of modern Mali, Senegal, Ghana and Guinea. Their spiritual practices merged with Moroccan Sufism over generations. Consequently, the music carries both Islamic and sub-Saharan elements.

    The instruments

    For instance, the guembri is a three-string bass lute covered in camel skin. Qraqeb are heavy iron castanets that drive the rhythm. Together they produce a hypnotic pulse that sounds nothing like Middle Eastern music.

    UNESCO recognition

    In 2019, UNESCO inscribed Gnawa on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The official listing describes it as a set of musical, spiritual and therapeutic practices. Therefore its African lineage is formally recognised.

    The lila ceremony

    A lila is an all-night ritual of music, incense and colour. Each colour calls a different spirit, and each spirit has its own rhythm. Visitors are sometimes invited, though a lila is not a show.

    Essaouira, capital of Gnawa

    Essaouira hosts the Gnaoua and World Music Festival every June. Hundreds of thousands of people attend, and the fusion concerts pair maalems with jazz and funk musicians. You can reach the city on an Essaouira full day trip from Marrakech.

    Morocco and the African Union

    The political side of the Moroccan African story is dramatic. It includes a founding role, a walkout and a return.

    A founding member in 1963

    Morocco helped create the Organisation of African Unity in Addis Ababa in 1963. King Hassan II attended, and Morocco supported liberation movements across the continent during that era.

    Why Morocco left in 1984

    The OAU admitted the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic as a member. Morocco objected and withdrew in 1984. Consequently, it spent more than three decades as the only African state outside the continental body.

    The return in 2017

    Morocco rejoined on 31 January 2017, at the African Union summit in Addis Ababa. Most member states voted in favour. Since then, the African Union has included every country on the continent, as the African Union member list shows.

    What changed after 2017

    Royal visits to African capitals increased sharply. Additionally, Morocco signed hundreds of cooperation agreements covering agriculture, banking, training and religious affairs. The country also applied to join ECOWAS, the West African economic bloc.

    Why this matters to visitors

    Because it shaped the country you will travel through. African students fill Moroccan universities. African business travellers fill Casablanca hotels. Consequently, the Moroccan African connection is visible in ordinary daily life, not only in history books.

    The Moroccan African economy today

    Likewise, modern economics answers the identity question in its own way. Morocco has spent two decades building itself into a hub for the continent.

    Tanger Med, Africa’s biggest port

    Tanger Med handled more than 11 million containers in 2025, an increase of 8.4% on the previous year. It also moved over 161 million tonnes of goods. Consequently, it ranks as the largest container port in Africa and one of the busiest in the Mediterranean.

    Banks across West Africa

    Moroccan banks operate in more than twenty African countries. Insurance groups, telecoms and fertiliser companies followed the same path. As a result, Morocco became one of the largest African investors in West Africa.

    Royal Air Maroc’s African network

    Casablanca works as a connecting hub between Africa, Europe and the Americas. Dozens of African cities appear on the departure boards at Mohammed V airport. If you are planning your route, see our guide to flights to Morocco.

    The Atlantic Initiative

    Morocco has offered landlocked Sahel states access to the Atlantic through its southern ports. The plan centres on Dakhla Atlantic Port. Meanwhile, the proposed Nigeria–Morocco Atlantic gas pipeline would run along the West African coast through more than a dozen countries.

    Al Boraq, Africa’s first high-speed train

    Al Boraq has linked Tangier and Kenitra since 2018 at speeds up to 320 km/h. It remains the only high-speed rail line on the continent. Furthermore, the extension towards Marrakech is under construction ahead of the 2030 World Cup.

    Phosphates and food security

    Morocco holds the world’s largest phosphate reserves. Fertiliser from those reserves supplies farms across Africa, often through customised blends for local soils. Therefore Moroccan exports affect harvests far beyond its borders.

    Football made the Moroccan African question mainstream

    Sport pushed the Moroccan African debate into living rooms worldwide. Three moments stand out.

    Qatar 2022 and a continental first

    At the 2022 World Cup, Morocco reached the semi-finals. No African team had ever done that before. Consequently, millions of people watched an African side beat Belgium, Spain and Portugal in the same tournament.

    Hosting the Africa Cup of Nations

    Morocco hosted the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations from 21 December 2025 to 18 January 2026. Twenty-four teams played across Rabat, Casablanca, Tangier, Marrakech, Fes and Agadir. Total attendance reached 1,340,022 spectators over 52 matches.

    A final that went to arbitration

    Senegal won the final 1-0 after extra time in Rabat on 18 January 2026. However, the CAF Appeal Board ruled on 17 March 2026 that Senegal had forfeited by leaving the pitch, and awarded the match 3-0 to Morocco. Senegal then appealed to the Court of Arbitration for Sport, and that case remained open through mid-2026.

    What the tournament left behind

    Stadiums, tram extensions and airport upgrades all arrived early. Additionally, hotels in Agadir and Tangier expanded capacity. Travellers in 2026 are benefiting from infrastructure built for African football.

    The 2030 World Cup

    Morocco will co-host the 2030 World Cup with Spain and Portugal. It will be the second World Cup held on African soil after South Africa in 2010. Our article on the Morocco World Cup 2030 covers the stadiums and the timeline.

    Agadir, Morocco, Africa: the city that confuses everyone

    Search interest in “Agadir Morocco Africa” is enormous, and the reason is simple. Agadir looks like a Mediterranean resort, yet it sits firmly on the African Atlantic. It is a perfect example of the Moroccan African puzzle.

    Where Agadir actually sits

    Agadir lies at roughly 30°N on the Atlantic coast, south of Essaouira. It is closer to the Canary Islands than to Tangier. Moreover, it is the gateway to the Souss valley and the Anti-Atlas mountains.

    A city rebuilt from nothing

    An earthquake destroyed Agadir in February 1960. The city was rebuilt on a modern grid, which explains why it feels so different from Fes or Marrakech. Consequently, visitors expecting medieval alleys are often surprised.

    Amazigh heartland

    The Souss region is deeply Tashelhit-speaking. Agadir itself means “fortified granary” in Tamazight. Therefore the city name is a reminder that this coast is indigenous African territory, not a colonial invention.

    Argan country

    The argan tree grows nowhere else on earth in significant numbers. Women’s cooperatives around Agadir and Taroudant produce oil for cooking and cosmetics. Our article on SIM cards and artisan cooperatives explains how to buy responsibly.

    What to do around Agadir

    Surf at Taghazout. Walk the Souss-Massa national park. Drive up to Paradise Valley. Alternatively, head inland to Taroudant, sometimes called “little Marrakech” for its earthen walls.

    How Morocco compares with the rest of Africa

    Morocco is African, yet Africa is enormous. Comparing regions honestly helps travellers set expectations.

    Climate

    Northern Morocco has a Mediterranean climate with wet winters. The interior is continental, and the south is desert. Meanwhile, equatorial Africa is tropical and humid. Our guide to the best time to visit Morocco breaks the seasons down month by month.

    Languages

    Morocco uses Arabic, Tamazight, French and increasingly English. Much of West Africa uses French, English or Portuguese plus local languages. Therefore travellers who speak French find both regions manageable.

    Food

    Moroccan cooking blends Amazigh, Andalusian, Arab and Saharan influences. Couscous, tagine and harira anchor the table. See our guides to traditional Moroccan dishes and cuisine in Morocco for the full picture.

    Wildlife

    Morocco has no lions, elephants or giraffes in the wild today. Instead, it has Barbary macaques in the cedar forests, desert foxes, and one of the richest bird migration corridors in the world. Consequently, a Moroccan safari means dunes and birds rather than big game.

    Infrastructure

    Motorways link the main cities, and trains connect the Atlantic corridor. Additionally, mobile coverage is strong in populated areas. Travellers who have driven elsewhere in Africa usually find Moroccan roads easy.

    Where the Moroccan African identity is most visible

    Different regions carry different layers of the story. Here is where to look for each one.

    The north: Mediterranean Africa

    Tangier, Tetouan and Chefchaouen face Europe and show Andalusian influence. Spanish is still widely spoken here. Our guide to Chefchaouen covers the blue city in detail.

    Fes: the Arab and Islamic layer

    Fes holds the Qarawiyyin, founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri and often described as the world’s oldest continuously operating university. The medina is a UNESCO World Heritage site. Tours departing from here are listed on our tours from Fes page.

    Marrakech: the Saharan gateway

    Marrakech was founded by the Almoravids, an Amazigh dynasty from the Sahara. Its name comes from Tamazight. Read our complete Marrakech travel guide and our guide to the old town and new town.

    The Souss and Anti-Atlas

    This is Tashelhit country, with fortified granaries, argan groves and saffron fields near Taliouine. The pace slows noticeably. Furthermore, the architecture uses stone rather than the pink pisé of the south-east.

    The Draa and Tafilalet oases

    Palm groves, ksour and irrigation systems shaped by centuries of desert farming define this region. The Moroccan African mixture is most visible here, in music, faces and food. Zagora’s famous sign once pointed to Timbuktu, fifty-two days away by camel.

    The deep south and Dakhla

    Beyond Guelmim, the landscape becomes Saharan and Hassani culture takes over. Camel markets, tea rituals and desert poetry dominate. Consequently, this region feels closer to Mauritania than to Casablanca.

    Six myths about the Moroccan African identity

    In any case, some claims circulate endlessly online. Let us deal with them one by one.

    Myth 1: Morocco is in the Middle East

    It is not. Morocco is roughly 5,000 kilometres from the Gulf. It shares a language family and a religion with the Middle East, yet it sits on the African continent.

    Myth 2: North Africa is not “real” Africa

    Africa is one continent with 55 African Union member states. Dividing it into “real” and “not real” reflects old colonial habits rather than geography. Furthermore, the Sahara has always been a highway, not a wall.

    Myth 3: Moroccans reject being African

    Most Moroccans are proud of both African and Arab belonging. The national football team is celebrated precisely as an African team. Consequently, the Moroccan African identity is a source of pride, not embarrassment.

    Myth 4: Everyone in Morocco speaks Arabic only

    Roughly a quarter of the population speaks an Amazigh language daily. Many households mix Darija, Tamazight and French in a single conversation. Additionally, English is spreading fast among younger Moroccans.

    Myth 5: Morocco is entirely desert

    The Sahara covers only part of the country. Morocco also has ski slopes at Oukaimeden, cedar forests near Ifrane, and 3,500 kilometres of coast. Therefore packing for one climate is a mistake, as our guide on what to wear in Morocco explains.

    Myth 6: You need vaccinations for tropical diseases

    Morocco is not a yellow fever or malaria zone. Standard travel vaccinations are usually enough, though a yellow fever certificate may be requested if you arrive from an endemic country. Always check current advice before you fly.

    Practical notes for your trip

    Understanding the Moroccan African context is useful. Still, you also need practical details before you land.

    Time zone: a change in September 2026

    Morocco returned to permanent GMT on 20 September 2026, ending eight years of year-round GMT+1. Consequently, sunset now falls an hour earlier on the clock than it did in previous summers. Our page on what time it is in Morocco keeps the details current.

    Currency

    In practice, the dirham is a closed currency, so you cannot buy it easily abroad. Withdraw cash on arrival at the airport or in town. Additionally, keep small notes for tips, parking guards and rural markets.

    Entry rules

    Many nationalities, including citizens of the EU, the UK, the US, Canada and Australia, enter visa-free for ninety days. However, rules change, so verify with an official consulate before booking. Our article on Morocco travel warnings explains how to read government advisories sensibly.

    Getting around

    For instance, trains serve the Atlantic corridor between Tangier, Rabat, Casablanca and Marrakech. Buses cover the rest. Nevertheless, a private driver is far more efficient for mountain and desert routes, as we explain in private vs group tours in Morocco.

    Etiquette that travels well

    Above all, greet people before asking questions. Accept mint tea when it is offered. Furthermore, ask before photographing anyone, especially performers, market traders and women.

    Dress and modesty

    In general, cities are relaxed, while villages are conservative. Shoulders and knees covered works everywhere. Our guide to traditional Moroccan clothing also explains the djellaba, the caftan and the babouche.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Morocco a country in Africa?

    Yes. Morocco is a sovereign country in northwest Africa, and it is a member of the African Union.

    Is Moroccan African or Middle Eastern?

    Moroccans are African by continent. Culturally, Morocco belongs to the Arab world too, but it is not part of the Middle East geographically.

    Where is Morocco located in Africa?

    Morocco occupies the northwestern corner of Africa, bordered by Algeria, Mauritania, the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea.

    Are Moroccans Black?

    Some are, and many are not. Morocco is ethnically mixed, with Amazigh, Arab, Haratin, Gnawa, Sahrawi and Andalusian roots. No official racial statistics exist.

    Is Morocco Arab or African?

    Both. Morocco is geographically African and culturally Arab and Amazigh at the same time.

    What race are Moroccans?

    There is no single answer. Since March 2024, United States federal forms include a Middle Eastern or North African category, which fits most Moroccans better than earlier options.

    Is Agadir in Africa?

    Yes. Agadir is a Moroccan city on the Atlantic coast of Africa, about 250 kilometres south of Essaouira.

    Do Moroccans consider themselves African?

    Overwhelmingly yes, especially in sport, music and business. The Moroccan African identity is celebrated openly across the country.

    What language do Moroccans speak?

    Darija and Tamazight dominate daily life, while Modern Standard Arabic and French are used in administration. English is growing quickly among young people.

    Is Morocco safe for travellers?

    Morocco is one of the most visited countries in Africa and is generally safe for tourists. Standard precautions against petty theft and scams apply in busy medinas.

    Does Morocco have a desert and mountains?

    Yes. The Atlas Mountains rise above 4,000 metres, and the Sahara begins on their southern side. Many itineraries cover both in three or four days.

    When is the best time to visit?

    Spring and autumn are ideal for most itineraries. Summer suits the coast, while winter suits the desert by day and requires warm layers at night.

    Final thoughts on the Moroccan African identity

    Morocco is African, and it always has been. The oldest fossils of our species were found here. The oldest genomes on the continent came from a cave in the Rif. Furthermore, the Amazigh language, the Gnawa drums, the caravan routes and the African Union seat all point the same way.

    At the same time, Morocco is Arab, Mediterranean, Andalusian and modern. That combination is not a contradiction. Instead, it is the reason the country feels unlike anywhere else on the continent.

    The best way to understand the Moroccan African story is to travel through it. Cross the Atlas, sleep under Saharan stars, and listen to a guembri at midnight in Essaouira.
