    Merzouga Camel Trekking: The Complete 2026 Guide to a Camel Ride in the Sahara

    Merzouga camel trekking guide: routes, prices, best time, desert camps and tips for the best camel ride in Morocco, in the Erg Chebbi dunes.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 30 September 2026

    Quick answer: Merzouga camel trekking means riding a dromedary into the Erg Chebbi dunes at sunset. Then you sleep in a desert camp and ride back at sunrise. In total, the ride lasts about one to two hours each way. Above all, the best months are October to April. In practice, most travellers reach Merzouga on a 3-day tour from Marrakech or Fes.

    Summary in 30 seconds

    • Merzouga sits at the edge of Erg Chebbi, the most famous sand sea in Morocco.
    • A classic Merzouga camel trek lasts 1–2 hours to camp and 1 hour back at sunrise.
    • Short sunset rides cost roughly €20–40; overnight treks with a camp start around €50 per person.
    • Luxury camps with private bathrooms cost about €120–300 per person per night.
    • Marrakech is about 560 km away (9–10 hours); Fes is about 470 km (8 hours).
    • October to April is the most comfortable season; summer rides happen only at dawn and dusk.
    • Camels in Morocco are dromedaries, with one hump, and walk at about 4–5 km/h.
    • Finally, book with a local operator who treats the animals well and limits group size.

    What is Merzouga camel trekking?

    Without doubt, Merzouga camel trekking is the most iconic experience in the Moroccan Sahara. In short, you sit on a dromedary, a local guide leads the caravan on foot, and you cross the orange dunes of Erg Chebbi. Along the way, the landscape changes colour with the light. Then, at the end of the ride, a camp of Berber tents waits between the dunes.

    For many visitors, therefore, this is the moment that defines their trip. In fact, a camel ride in Morocco is one of the most searched travel experiences in the country. Merzouga is the easiest place to do it well, because the dunes are tall, close to the road and well served by camps. Our Sahara Desert Morocco guide gives the wider picture of the region.

    Why Merzouga camel trekking is worth the journey

    Merzouga is far from the big cities, so some travellers wonder if the long drive is worth it. In our experience, the answer is almost always yes. First, the road itself is spectacular, since it crosses the High Atlas, the Dades valley and the Todra gorges. Second, Erg Chebbi has the tallest and most photogenic dunes that are easy to reach in Morocco.

    Moreover, the silence of the desert is something you cannot find anywhere else. When the sun goes down and the camels kneel beside the camp, the whole trip suddenly makes sense. That is why Merzouga camel trekking appears on almost every list of things to do in Morocco. In short, it is the kind of memory that stays with you for years.

    Where is Merzouga and how do you get there?

    Merzouga is a small village in south-eastern Morocco, close to the Algerian border. Specifically, it lies in the province of Errachidia, right at the foot of Erg Chebbi. Meanwhile, the dunes stretch about 22 km from north to south and reach roughly 150 metres at their highest point.

    Getting there takes time, however. There are no trains, so most travellers arrive by road on an organised tour. Alternatively, you can fly to Errachidia and drive the last two hours.

    FesAbout 470 km7–8 hours3-day tour via Ifrane and the Ziz valley
    ErrachidiaAbout 130 km2 hoursFlight plus private transfer

    For a short trip, the 3-day tour from Marrakech to the desert is the most popular option. From the north, however, the 3-day tour from Fes to Merzouga works best.

    Types of Merzouga camel trekking

    Of course, not every camel trek is the same. Before you book, it helps to know the four main formats.

    Sunset camel ride (1–2 hours)

    First, this is the shortest option. You leave the village in the late afternoon, climb a dune to watch the sunset and ride back. Therefore, it suits travellers who sleep in a hotel in Merzouga or Hassi Labied.

    Overnight camel trek

    This is the classic Merzouga camel trekking experience. You ride to a desert camp at sunset, have dinner under the stars and return on camelback after sunrise. In fact, most 3-day desert tours include this format.

    Sunrise camel ride

    However, some travellers prefer the morning. Sunrise rides start in the dark, so you reach the top of a dune just as the sun appears. At that hour, the air is cool, and the sand shows sharp ridges and shadows.

    Multi-day camel trek

    Alternatively, for a deeper adventure, you can trek for two or three days across Erg Chebbi. Along the way, you sleep in simple nomad camps, walk part of the way and see a quieter side of the desert. However, this format needs good fitness and a flexible schedule.

    A typical overnight Merzouga camel trek, hour by hour

    Below, you can see how a standard overnight trek usually unfolds. Times shift with the season, of course, because everything follows the sun.

    • 4:30 pm: you arrive at the auberge in Merzouga, leave your large luggage and pack a small bag.
    • 5:00 pm: the guide helps you mount, and the caravan heads into the dunes.
    • 6:15 pm: you stop on a high dune to watch the sunset over Erg Chebbi.
    • 7:00 pm: arrival at camp, with mint tea and time to settle into your tent.
    • 8:30 pm: dinner, usually soup, tagine and fruit, followed by drums around the fire.
    • 6:00 am: wake-up call, then a short climb to watch the sunrise.
    • 7:00 am: camel ride back to the village, followed by breakfast and a shower.

    In winter, the whole schedule moves about one hour earlier. In summer, by contrast, it moves later to avoid the heat.

    Standard vs luxury desert camps in Merzouga

    Of course, where you sleep shapes the whole experience. For example, standard camps have simple Berber tents with mattresses and blankets. In addition, bathrooms are often shared, and electricity may be limited. Still, they feel authentic, and the stars are exactly the same.

    Luxury camps, on the other hand, offer real beds, private bathrooms with hot showers and full dinners. Some even have heating in winter. As a result, for couples and honeymooners, they are worth the extra cost. Our private Sahara desert tour from Marrakech can include either option.

    Best time for Merzouga camel trekking

    The best time for Merzouga camel trekking is October to April. During these months, days are warm and rides are therefore comfortable. However, nights in December and January can drop close to freezing, so bring warm layers.

    What to wear and pack for a camel trek

    Pack light, because you only take a small bag to camp. In addition, choose clothes that protect you from sun, wind and sand.

    • Long, loose trousers to avoid chafing on the saddle.
    • A scarf or cheche to cover your head and face in the wind.
    • Sunglasses and sunscreen, even in winter.
    • A warm jacket for the evening, especially from November to March.
    • Closed shoes or sandals that you can empty of sand easily.
    • A head torch, since camps have little light at night.
    • Water, wet wipes and a power bank.

    For more advice on clothing, see what to wear in Morocco.

    Is camel trekking ethical? How to choose a responsible operator

    For centuries, camels have carried people and goods across the Sahara. Even so, animal welfare depends on the owner. For instance, a good operator gives the animals rest, water and shade, and never overloads them.

    Before you ride, look at the camels. For example, healthy animals have a good body condition, clean saddles and no open wounds. Also, check that each guide leads a small caravan. If something looks wrong, choose another operator. Most camels can comfortably carry an adult of average weight; if you weigh more than about 100 kg, tell your agency in advance so they can choose a strong animal.

    Tips for riding a camel comfortably

    Riding a camel is easy, yet the first minutes surprise most people. When the camel stands up, it lifts its back legs first. So, lean back as it rises, then lean forward as it kneels at the end.

    • Hold the saddle handle with both hands during take-off and landing.
    • Relax your hips and move with the rhythm of the walk.
    • Keep your phone on a strap, because a dropped phone disappears in the sand.
    • Ask the guide to stop if you feel sore; walking for ten minutes helps.
    • Do not stand behind a camel, and never feed it without the guide’s permission.

    Other things to do around Merzouga

    A camel trek is the highlight, but Merzouga offers much more. If you stay an extra night, you can easily fill a full day.

    • Khamlia: a village of Gnawa musicians who play traditional music for visitors.
    • Sandboarding: most camps lend boards for the steep dune faces.
    • Quad biking and 4×4 tours: a fast way to see the edges of Erg Chebbi.
    • Dayet Srji: a seasonal lake that sometimes attracts flamingos after rain.
    • Rissani: a historic market town, especially lively on market days.

    Frequently asked questions about Merzouga camel trekking

    How long is the camel ride in Merzouga?

    Usually one to two hours to reach the camp, and about one hour back at sunrise. Multi-day treks are also possible.

    How much does Merzouga camel trekking cost?

    A short sunset ride costs roughly €20–40. An overnight trek with a standard camp starts around €50 per person, while luxury camps cost €120–300.

    Is a camel ride in Morocco safe?

    Yes. This is because the camels walk slowly, and a guide leads them on foot. Just hold on firmly when the camel stands up and kneels down. For general advice, read whether Morocco is safe.

    Can children do a camel trek?

    Yes. In general, children from about four years old enjoy it, often sharing a camel with a parent. However, shorter sunset rides suit young families best.

    What is the best time for camel trekking in Morocco?

    October to April is best. In summer, rides still run, but only at sunrise and sunset because of the heat.

    Do I need to book in advance?

    For spring, autumn and the Christmas holidays, yes. In particular, luxury camps fill up weeks ahead.

    Is Merzouga better than Agafay for a camel ride?

    For real sand dunes, yes. Agafay is a stony desert near Marrakech, so it is convenient but very different from the Sahara.
