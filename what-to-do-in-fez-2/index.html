    What to Do in Fez: 30 Unmissable Things to Do in Fez Morocco (2026 Guide)

    What to do in Fez in 2026: 30 top experiences, from Bou Inania and the Chouara tannery to the souks, night shows hot springs and Sahara trips

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 16 September 2026

    Quick answer: The best of what to do in Fez fits into two or three days. Walk the UNESCO medina from Bab Boujloud, enter Bou Inania Madrasa, watch the Chouara tannery from a terrace, shop the souks, eat pastilla, relax in a hammam and see the city from the Merinid Tombs at sunset. Add Volubilis, Meknes or the Sahara if you have longer.

    What to do in Fez: the 30-second summary

    • Fez el Bali has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1981. Its lanes are closed to cars.
    • Two full days cover the headline sights. A third day adds Volubilis and Meknes.
    • Bou Inania and Al-Attarine madrasas charge roughly 20 to 30 MAD. The Al Batha Museum charges foreign visitors 60 MAD and closes on Tuesdays.
    • Viewing the Chouara tannery is free. A tip of 10 to 30 MAD is normal if you buy nothing.
    • Since 26 March 2026, free shows run at Bab Boujloud from Friday to Sunday, starting at 5 p.m.
    • On 20 September 2026 Morocco returned to GMT for good, so sunset in Fez now comes about an hour earlier on the clock.
    • Merzouga lies about 470 km from Fez, a drive of seven to eight hours. Plan at least three days for the desert.
    • In September 2026, 1 euro buys about 10.95 dirhams and 1 US dollar about 9.48 dirhams.

    How this list of things to do in Fes works

    Search engines treat “Fez” and “Fes” as the same city. Road signs use Fès, while English speakers often write Fez. We use Fez in this article. It is a practical list of what to do in Fez, with time, cost and a local tip for each item. In other words, it answers the question guests ask us most: what to do in Fez Morocco once you arrive?

    Classic sights: what to do in Fez on day one

    Start with the medina. It holds the monuments that make Fez city famous, and most of them sit within a 20-minute walk of each other.

    1. Enter the medina through Bab Boujloud

    The Blue Gate dates from 1913. Its outer face is blue, while the inner face is green, the colour of Islam. Arrive around 9 a.m., before the tour groups. Photograph the gate from the inside too, because it helps you find the exit later.

    2. Walk down Talaa Kebira

    Talaa Kebira, “the big climb”, is the main artery of the old city. It runs downhill from Bab Boujloud towards the Quaraouiyine. Along the way you pass bakeries, butchers, sweet stalls and cedar workshops. Keep walking downhill and you reach the centre. Walk uphill and you return to a gate. That single rule solves most navigation problems.

    3. Step inside Bou Inania Madrasa

    The Marinid sultan Abu Inan Faris built this college between 1350 and 1355. It is one of the few religious buildings in Fez that non-Muslims may enter. Look for the carved cedar, the stucco and the zellige. Entry costs about 20 MAD. It usually closes on Friday and during prayer times. Across the street, the facade of the 14th-century water clock still shows its twelve window recesses.

    4. Look into Al Quaraouiyine from Place Seffarine

    Fatima al-Fihri founded Al Quaraouiyine in 859. It is widely described as the oldest continuously operating educational institution in the world. Non-Muslims cannot enter the prayer hall. However, you can look through the open doors and admire the green-tiled roofs. The restored library reopened in 2016.

    5. Climb the roof of Al-Attarine Madrasa

    Al-Attarine is smaller and older than Bou Inania. It dates from the 1320s. Many visitors actually prefer it, because the detail feels closer. Moreover, the upper floor gives a rare view over the Quaraouiyine roof. Budget 30 minutes and about 20 MAD.

    6. Watch the Chouara tannery from above

    Chouara is the largest and oldest of the Fez tanneries. Men still work hides by hand in stone vats of lime and natural dye. You watch from the terraces of the leather shops around it. Entry is free, and a sprig of mint helps with the smell. Go in the morning, when the colours are brightest. If you buy nothing, a tip of 10 to 30 MAD is fair.

    7. See the Nejjarine fountain and museum

    Place Nejjarine holds one of the most photographed fountains in the medina. Next to it stands an 18th-century funduq, an old merchants’ inn. Today it houses the Museum of Wooden Arts and Crafts. The building itself is the highlight. Also, the rooftop café is a calm place to rest your feet.

    8. Visit the Al Batha Museum of Islamic Arts

    Dar Batha began as a royal summer palace between 1873 and 1894. After a restoration that started in 2019, it reopened on 26 February 2025 as the Al Batha Museum of Islamic Arts. The collection traces calligraphy, woodwork and dynasties from the 8th century onward. According to the National Museums Foundation, it opens from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., closes on Tuesdays and charges foreign visitors 60 MAD.

    The bazaar in Fes: how to shop the souks

    Visitors often ask where the bazaar in Fes is. In fact, there is no single building. The whole medina works as one giant market, split into specialised souks. Each trade has its own street. That makes shopping easier once you know where to look.

    9. Smell your way through Souk el Attarine

    This is the spice and perfume souk beside the madrasa of the same name. Stalls sell cumin, ras el hanout, saffron, amber and rose water. Ask to smell before you buy. Also, buy saffron only from a stall that lets you check the threads.

    10. Find the henna souk

    Souk el Henna is a small square shaded by a large old tree. Shops sell henna, kohl, ghassoul clay and hand-painted pottery. It is quieter than the main streets, so it makes a good break.

    11. Listen to the coppersmiths of Seffarine

    Place Seffarine is the sound of Fez. Craftsmen beat copper trays, cauldrons and lanterns on the square. You can watch for free. In addition, many workshops will engrave a small plate while you wait.

    12. See the dyers’ souk

    In Souk Sebbaghine, near the river, dyers dip skeins of wool and silk into vats. The skeins then hang above the lane to dry. Ask before you photograph the workers, and a small tip is polite.

    13. Buy fruit and olives at the Rcif market

    The Rcif area, at the bottom of the medina, is where Fassi families shop. You will find olives, dates, preserved lemons, fresh herbs and fish. Prices here reflect local life, not tourism. As a result, it is the best place to learn what things should cost.

    14. Watch zellige being cut in Ain Nokbi

    The potters’ quarter lies just outside the walls. Here, craftsmen fire the famous blue-and-white Fassi ceramics. They also chip each piece of zellige mosaic by hand. A taxi from Bab Ftouh takes a few minutes. Most workshops welcome visitors and explain the full process.

    Fes el Jdid and the hills

    After a day in the old medina, the “new” Marinid city of 1276 feels calm and spacious. Moreover, the hills around the walls give you the best views in Fez.

    15. Photograph the Royal Palace doors

    You cannot enter Dar al-Makhzen, the Royal Palace. Still, its seven polished brass doors on Place des Alaouites are among the most famous sights in the country. Come early for soft light and fewer people.

    16. Walk the Mellah and visit Ibn Danan Synagogue

    The Mellah, created in the 15th century, was the first Jewish quarter in Morocco. Its houses still show open wooden balconies facing the street. The restored Ibn Danan Synagogue dates from the 17th century. Nearby, the white Jewish cemetery climbs the hill. Together they tell a story many visitors never hear. Our guide to religion in Morocco adds useful context.

    17. Rest in Jnan Sbil garden

    Jnan Sbil sits between Fes el Jdid and the old medina. It has palm alleys, bamboo, fountains and a large pond. Entry is free. In June, it also hosts concerts of the Fes Festival of World Sacred Music. The 29th edition ran from 4 to 7 June 2026.

    18. Watch sunset from the Merinid Tombs

    The ruined 14th-century tombs stand on the northern hill. Nobody comes for the ruins. Everybody comes for the panorama over Fez el Bali as the call to prayer rises. Take a taxi up, then walk down with a guide or a group. Leave before full darkness.

    19. Explore the Ville Nouvelle

    The French-built new town has wide avenues, cafés and the train station. Sit on a terrace on Avenue Hassan II and watch Fassi life go by. It is also the easiest place to find a pharmacy, an ATM or a SIM card.

    Food experiences you should not skip

    Food is a big part of what to do in Fez. Fassi cooking is the most refined in Morocco. It is sweeter and more delicately spiced than the food of Marrakech. So plan at least one experience around eating.

    20. Eat a street-food breakfast

    Order sfenj, the ring-shaped doughnut, and a bowl of bissara, a fava bean soup with olive oil and cumin. Together they cost only a few dirhams. Follow the locals: the longest queue is usually the best stall.

    21. Take a cooking class

    A half-day class usually starts with a market walk and ends with the tagine you cooked. Expect roughly 350 to 600 MAD per person. It teaches you more about daily life than any museum.

    22. Try pastilla at a Fassi dinner

    Pastilla is a crisp pie of chicken or pigeon, almonds and cinnamon, dusted with sugar. Fez is its home. Order it in a riad or a family restaurant. Our guide to traditional Moroccan dishes lists what else to try.

    23. Drink mint tea on a rooftop

    From a medina rooftop you see satellite dishes, minarets and the green hills beyond. Mint tea costs about 15 to 30 MAD. It arrives sweet, so ask for “shwiya sukar” if you want less sugar.

    Moroccan water in Fez: fountains, hammams and hot springs

    Water shaped this city. The Oued Fes river was split into channels that fed homes, mosques, workshops and public fountains. That makes Fez one of the best places to understand Moroccan water culture.

    24. Hunt for the medina’s fountains

    Look for the tiled public fountains, called sqaya, on almost every street corner. The Nejjarine fountain is the grandest. Others are simple taps set in zellige. Counting them turns a walk into a treasure hunt, and children love it.

    25. Relax in a hammam

    A hammam is the best end to a long walking day. In a public hammam, entry costs about 15 to 30 MAD, plus 50 to 100 MAD for a scrub. Spa hammams in riads cost more but explain every step. Read our Moroccan hammam guide before you go.

    26. Visit the hot springs of Sidi Harazem or Moulay Yacoub

    Sidi Harazem lies about 10 to 15 km east of Fez. Its spring flows at around 35 °C, and its water is bottled under the same name across Morocco. Moulay Yacoub, to the north-west, is known for its hot sulphur baths. Both make an easy half-day trip.

    Can you drink tap water in Fez?

    Tap water in Fez is treated. Since 15 July 2025, the Société Régionale Multiservices Fès-Meknès has managed water, electricity and sanitation in the region. Most residents drink it. Even so, many short-stay visitors prefer bottled water to avoid stomach upsets. Common brands include Sidi Ali, Ain Saiss and Sidi Harazem. Our Morocco safety guide covers water, ice and food hygiene in detail.

    What to do in Fez at night

    Fez is not a party city. Instead, evenings are slow, social and centred on food. Here is what to do in Fez at night without feeling bored.

    27. Catch the weekend shows at Bab Boujloud

    Since 26 March 2026, the commune of Fez and the Fes-Saïss Association have run a weekly programme on the square. Storytelling, theatre, music and street parades take place from Friday to Sunday, starting at 5 p.m. Morocco World News reported the launch. It is the liveliest free evening in the city.

    Book a rooftop dinner with music

    Several riads serve dinner with Andalusian or Sufi music. Book in the morning, because tables are limited. As a bonus, the medina lights look magical from above.

    Take a guided night walk

    After dark, the main streets stay busy until about 9 p.m. A guide can show you bakeries firing their ovens and quiet squares under lanterns. Avoid wandering into empty alleys alone.

    Where to have a drink

    Alcohol is served in licensed hotels and some restaurants, mostly in the Ville Nouvelle and a few upscale riads. It is rarely available inside the medina. Therefore, plan your evening drink before you set out.

    Staying safe after dark

    Carry a charged phone, save your riad’s number and ask staff to meet you at the gate. With those habits, what to do in Fez at night becomes a relaxed choice rather than a worry.

    Morocco time zone: why Fez gets dark earlier from 20 September 2026

    This is the change most guides have not caught up with. At 2 a.m. on Sunday 20 September 2026, Morocco set its clocks back 60 minutes. Decree 2.26.530, published in the Official Bulletin on 29 June 2026, made GMT the country’s only legal time, as Médias24 explains. No seasonal switch back to GMT+1 is planned.

    • Visit the tanneries early, since shops start closing sooner.

    Day trips from Fez

    Once you have covered what to do in Fez itself, look beyond the walls. Fez is the best base in northern Morocco. Within two hours you reach Roman ruins, another imperial city and a cedar forest.

    28. Explore Volubilis and Moulay Idriss

    The Roman city of Volubilis, a UNESCO site since 1997, keeps its mosaics in the open air. It lies about 70 km from Fez. Ten minutes away, the holy town of Moulay Idriss Zerhoun covers two green hills. Together they fill a relaxed half day.

    29. Discover Meknes

    Meknes is about 60 km away, roughly an hour by road. See the monumental Bab Mansour gate, the royal granaries and a medina where nobody rushes you. Many travellers combine it with Volubilis in one full day.

    30. Drive to Ifrane and the Azrou cedar forest

    Ifrane, about 65 km south, looks like an alpine village. Beyond it, the cedar forest near Azrou shelters Barbary macaques. Please do not feed them. Bring a jacket, because the plateau sits above 1,600 metres.

    More ideas for longer stays

    Sefrou, 30 km away, has a cherry festival listed by UNESCO. Chefchaouen, the blue city, is three and a half to four hours north. It works much better as an overnight, as our Chefchaouen guide explains. For more options, see our guide to excursions in Morocco.

    Merzouga Desert vs Sahara: are they the same thing?

    Many travellers type “Merzouga desert vs Sahara” into Google. The short answer: Merzouga is a village, and the dunes beside it belong to the Sahara.

    What Merzouga really is

    Merzouga sits on the edge of Erg Chebbi, a sea of dunes in south-east Morocco. Erg Chebbi is one of the Saharan ergs, and some of its dunes rise to around 150 metres. So when you sleep in a Merzouga camp, you sleep in the Sahara.

    How it compares with other desert options

    Erg Chigaga, beyond Zagora, is wilder but much farther from Fez. The Agafay area near Marrakech is a stony plateau, not the Sahara. Compare the two big dune fields in our Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga guide, and read the full Sahara Desert Morocco guide.

    Is Western Sahara part of Morocco? What travellers should know

    This question often comes up when people plan a long route. It is a political subject, so here are the facts without spin.

    The situation on the ground

    Morocco administers most of the territory and calls it its Southern Provinces. The United Nations still lists Western Sahara as a non-self-governing territory. The Polisario Front, backed by Algeria, seeks independence. Both sides therefore give different answers to the question “Is Western Sahara part of Morocco?”

    The latest UN decision

    In late October 2025 the Security Council adopted Resolution 2797 by 11 votes to none. China, Pakistan and Russia abstained, and Algeria did not take part. The text backs negotiations based on Morocco’s autonomy plan. It also refers to self-determination and extends the MINURSO mission until 31 October 2026.

    What it means for a trip

    Laayoune and Dakhla receive domestic flights, and Dakhla is a known kitesurfing spot. However, official advisories warn against the areas near the berm and the borders. Dakhla is also close to 2,000 km from Fez by road. Our travel warnings explainer and our guide to where Morocco is give more detail.

    What to do in Fez for free

    Fez rewards curious walkers, and many of its best moments cost nothing. Here is what to do in Fez without spending a dirham.

    • Watch the coppersmiths at work on Place Seffarine.
    • Look through the doors of Al Quaraouiyine.
    • Stroll through Jnan Sbil garden.
    • Photograph the brass doors of the Royal Palace.
    • Catch the Bab Boujloud shows from Friday to Sunday.
    • Climb to the Merinid Tombs for sunset.
    • Count the tiled fountains on your way through the medina.

    In addition, entry to the tannery terraces is free, although a small tip is expected. So a budget traveller can fill a full day with free sights and pay only for food.

    What to do in Fez with children

    Families often worry that the medina will be too intense. In practice, children usually love it. Mules, cats, workshops and hidden fountains keep them busy.

    Tips for families

    Keep walking sessions under two hours, then rest in the riad. Also, choose a riad with a gate pick-up, since strollers do not work on the steep lanes. A baby carrier is far more practical. Finally, a pottery workshop in Ain Nokbi or a bread lesson at a communal oven makes history hands-on.

    Best family picks

    Jnan Sbil, the Nejjarine museum rooftop, the macaques near Azrou and a cooking class suit most ages. Likewise, the hot springs of Moulay Yacoub are popular with Moroccan families at weekends.

    What to do in Fez in summer, winter or rain

    The weather changes what to do in Fez more than most people expect, because the city has a continental climate.

    In summer

    Start at dawn, rest from 1 p.m. to 5 p.m., and go back out when the heat drops. Consequently, a riad with a plunge pool is worth the extra cost in July and August.

    In winter

    Days are mild, but nights can fall close to freezing. Check that your riad has heating. On the bright side, the medina is quiet and prices are lower.

    On a rainy day

    Move indoors. Spend the morning in the Al Batha Museum, then book a hammam and a cooking class. Wet cobbles get slippery, so wear shoes with good grip.

    On a Friday

    Friday is the weekly day of prayer. Many shops close around midday, and Bou Inania usually closes to visitors. Therefore, use Friday for Fes el Jdid, Jnan Sbil or a day trip, then enjoy the evening shows at Bab Boujloud.

    Mistakes to avoid in Fez

    Treating Fez as a half-day stop

    Groups that arrive at noon and leave the next morning only see a gate and a tannery. That is not the city.

    Shopping on the first afternoon

    Prices only make sense after you have seen the same item in several shops. Look first, then buy on your last day.

    Following an unofficial guide

    A friendly stranger’s tour often ends in a relative’s carpet shop. Instead, book a licensed guide through your riad or agency.

    Book a licensed guide through your riad or your agency. Unofficial guides still approach visitors near Bab Boujloud. A polite “la, shukran” (no, thank you) usually ends the conversation. Likewise, ignore anyone who says a street is closed; it is an old trick to lead you to a shop.

    Frequently asked questions about what to do in Fez

    What is the best thing to do in Fez?

    Walking the medina with a licensed guide is the best thing to do in Fez. It links Bou Inania, the Chouara tannery, the souks and Al Quaraouiyine into one story.

    What to do in Fez Morocco in one day?

    Enter at Bab Boujloud, walk down Talaa Kebira, visit Bou Inania and Al-Attarine, see the Chouara tannery, then eat dinner on a rooftop.

    What to do in Fez at night?

    Watch the free shows at Bab Boujloud from Friday to Sunday, book a rooftop dinner with music, or take a guided night walk. Bars exist mainly in hotels.

    Where is the main bazaar in Fes?

    There is no single building. The bazaar in Fes is the medina itself, with souks for spices, henna, copper, dyes and leather along Talaa Kebira and around the Quaraouiyine.

    Can non-Muslims enter Al Quaraouiyine?

    No, the prayer hall is for Muslims only. However, you can look through its doors, and non-Muslims can enter Bou Inania and Al-Attarine madrasas.

    What time zone is Morocco in?

    Since 20 September 2026, Morocco uses GMT all year. Before that date, the country used GMT+1.

    Is Merzouga part of the Sahara?

    Yes. Merzouga sits beside Erg Chebbi, a dune field of the Sahara Desert, about 470 km from Fez.

    Is Western Sahara part of Morocco?

    Morocco administers most of it, while the UN lists it as a non-self-governing territory. In 2025, the Security Council backed talks based on Morocco’s autonomy plan.

    Is tap water safe in Fez?

    It is treated, and locals drink it. Still, short-stay visitors often choose bottled water to avoid stomach upsets.

    How many days do you need in Fez?

    Two days for the main sights, three with Volubilis and Meknes, and at least three more for the Sahara. That gives you time for what to do in Fez without rushing.

    Is Fez worth visiting?

    Yes. It holds one of the best-preserved medieval Islamic cities in the world, and it is still a working craft town.

    How much does a guide cost in Fez?

    A licensed guide costs roughly 300 to 500 MAD for half a day in 2026. Agree the price before you start.
