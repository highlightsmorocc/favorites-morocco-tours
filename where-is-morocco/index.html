    Where Is Morocco? The Complete Location Guide: Maps, Borders, Distances and How to Get There

    Where is Morocco, exactly? Morocco sits in the far northwest corner of Africa, directly across the Strait of Gibraltar from Spain. Only 14 kilometres of water separate the two countries. That single fact explains almost everything about the country: the food, the languages, the architecture, and the reason a traveller can leave Europe after breakfast and watch the sun set over Saharan dunes.

    Yet the question keeps coming up. Some people place Morocco in the Middle East. Others assume it sits somewhere near Egypt. A few imagine it as one endless desert. None of that is accurate.

    We run tours across this country every week of the year. Therefore, we wrote this guide to answer the location question properly, with real coordinates, real distances and real drive times. Above all, we wanted to explain what Morocco’s position actually means once you land.

    Where Is Morocco? The Short Answer

    Morocco occupies the northwestern tip of the African continent. The Atlantic Ocean forms its western edge. The Mediterranean Sea forms its northern edge. Algeria lies to the east, and Mauritania lies to the south. To the north, across a narrow strait, sits Europe.

    In short, Morocco is an African country with a European neighbour visible from its beaches. On a clear day you can stand in Tangier and see the hills of Spain.

    Where Is Morocco in Latitude and Longitude?

    Morocco stretches between roughly 27° and 36° north latitude. It also spans about 1° to 13° west longitude. As a result, the country sits at the same latitude as Georgia and South Carolina in the United States. It also lines up with Cyprus, Crete and southern Japan.

    That position matters. Morocco sits far enough north to enjoy a Mediterranean climate along its coasts. Meanwhile, it reaches far enough south to hold genuine Saharan desert. Consequently, one country delivers snowfields and sand seas in the same week.

    Where Is Morocco Compared With Places You Already Know?

    Morocco lies southwest of Spain and Portugal. It sits west of Algeria and northwest of Mali. Furthermore, it lies about 2,000 kilometres west of Egypt, with Libya and Tunisia in between. Anyone who pictures Morocco beside the pyramids has the map badly wrong.

    The Canary Islands, which belong to Spain, sit roughly 100 kilometres off Morocco’s southern Atlantic coast. Portugal’s Algarve lies about 500 kilometres north of Casablanca across open water.

    Where Is Morocco on the World Map?

    Picture the African continent. Now look at its top-left shoulder, the point that reaches toward Europe. That shoulder is Morocco. The country forms the western end of North Africa and the western end of the Arab world at the same time.

    Where Is Morocco Placed? Northwest Africa, Not the Middle East

    Morocco belongs to Africa geographically, politically and geologically. The country joined the Organisation of African Unity as a founding member. Later it left, and in January 2017 it rejoined the African Union. Morocco also belongs to the Arab League, which is where the confusion begins for many travellers.

    However, membership of a cultural or political body does not move a country. Morocco has never been part of Asia. It has no border with any Middle Eastern state. In fact, Morocco sits closer to London than Cairo does.

    Where Is Morocco’s Coastline? Two Seas, One Country

    Morocco holds a distinction that surprises most visitors. It is the only African country with coastline on both the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea. Roughly 1,300 kilometres of Atlantic shore run down the west. Just over 500 kilometres of Mediterranean shore run along the north.

    Because of this, the country produces two completely different coastal cultures. Essaouira and Taghazout face Atlantic swell and cool trade winds. Meanwhile, Al Hoceima and Saidia sit on calm, warm Mediterranean water.

    Where Is Morocco Closest to Europe?

    The Strait of Gibraltar separates Morocco from Spain. At its narrowest point the strait measures about 14.2 kilometres, or roughly 8.9 miles. Its depth ranges between 300 and 900 metres. Ships pass through it constantly, because the strait connects the Atlantic Ocean to the entire Mediterranean.

    For travellers, that gap creates a remarkable option. You can board a fast ferry in Tarifa and step ashore in Tangier in about an hour. Consequently, Morocco is the easiest intercontinental journey most Europeans will ever make.

    What Continent Is Morocco In?

    Where is Morocco in continental terms? The answer is Africa, and that answer is not disputed. Still, the reasons behind the confusion deserve a proper explanation, because they reveal a lot about the country.

    Where Is Morocco According to Official Bodies?

    Morocco sits on the African tectonic plate. It occupies African soil. It holds a seat in the African Union, and it competes in African football competitions. Moreover, its national team reached the World Cup semi-final in 2022 as an African side, the first from the continent to do so.

    Morocco will also co-host the 2030 FIFA World Cup alongside Spain and Portugal. That tournament will span two continents, and Morocco supplies the African half. You can read more in our guide to the Morocco World Cup 2030.

    Why People Confuse Morocco With the Middle East

    Several threads create the confusion. Arabic serves as an official language. Islam is the dominant religion. The architecture uses arches, zellij tilework and courtyards that resemble buildings in Damascus or Isfahan. In addition, Morocco belongs to the Arab League.

    Nevertheless, the ground beneath your feet stays African. Morocco’s oldest layer of identity is Amazigh, often called Berber, and it predates the Arab arrival by thousands of years. Our detailed guide to Berber culture in Morocco explains that heritage in depth.

    Where Is Morocco Within the Maghreb?

    Geographers group Morocco with Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Mauritania in a region called the Maghreb. The word comes from Arabic and means “the place of sunset” or “the west”. Arab geographers named it that because it sat at the western edge of the medieval Arab world.

    Morocco holds the far western position within that group. Its own Arabic name, Al Maghrib, literally means “the west”. Therefore the country’s very name is a statement about where it sits.

    Which Languages Reflect That Location?

    Morocco’s position produced a rare linguistic mix. Moroccans speak Darija, several Amazigh languages, French, Spanish in the north, and increasingly English. Few countries pack that many working languages into one market street. Our article on the languages spoken in Morocco covers each one and where you will hear it.

    Which Countries Border Morocco?

    Morocco shares land borders with three entities: Algeria, Mauritania, and Spain. The Spanish border is unusual, because it exists inside Africa rather than across the sea.

    Algeria: The Long Closed Border

    Algeria forms Morocco’s eastern neighbour along roughly 1,550 kilometres of frontier. The two countries closed that border to travellers in 1994, and it has stayed shut ever since. As a result, you cannot drive from Morocco into Algeria as a tourist.

    This matters practically. Travellers sometimes plan a North African overland route from Morocco to Tunisia. That route does not work by land. You must fly instead.

    Mauritania: The Southern Gateway

    Mauritania lies to the south, beyond the Sahara. The Guerguerat crossing near Dakhla stays open, and overlanders use it to continue toward Senegal. However, the drive is long, remote and demanding. Most visitors never go near it.

    Ceuta and Melilla: The European Union in Africa

    Two Spanish cities sit on the Moroccan mainland. Ceuta faces the Strait of Gibraltar, and Melilla sits further east on the Mediterranean. Their land borders with Morocco measure about 6.3 kilometres and 9.6 kilometres respectively.

    Both cities belong to Spain and therefore to the European Union. Consequently, Morocco is one of the very few countries outside Europe with a land border with the EU. Travellers can walk across, though the crossings involve full passport control.

    Western Sahara: What Travellers Should Know

    South of the Draa River lies Western Sahara, a territory whose status remains disputed internationally. Morocco administers most of it and treats it as its Southern Provinces. Consequently, published figures for Morocco’s area vary widely.

    Without Western Sahara, Morocco covers about 446,000 square kilometres. With it, the figure rises to roughly 712,000 square kilometres. Tourist routes rarely reach that far south, so the distinction matters more to statisticians than to visitors. That said, travellers heading to Dakhla for kitesurfing should read current government advice first. Our breakdown of the US and Canadian Morocco travel warnings explains what those advisories actually say.

    How Big Is Morocco, and What Does That Mean for Travellers?

    Knowing where is Morocco on a map is one thing. Grasping its scale is another. Maps flatten distance, and Morocco looks compact beside Algeria, its enormous eastern neighbour. In reality the country is large, and its mountains make journeys longer than the kilometres suggest.

    How Long Does It Take to Cross Morocco?

    Morocco proper measures about 900 kilometres from the northern tip near Tangier to the southern boundary at the Draa. Including the Southern Provinces, that figure stretches beyond 1,700 kilometres. Driving from Tangier to Dakhla takes several days.

    Population sits unevenly across that space. About 38.8 million people live in Morocco, and roughly 68 percent of them live in cities. Most of them cluster in the northwest quarter, while the southeast holds oases, dunes and very few residents.

    The Four Moroccos: Where Each Landscape Actually Sits

    The question “where is Morocco?” only takes you halfway. A more useful question asks where things sit inside Morocco. In practice, the country divides into four broad zones, and they run in parallel bands from northwest to southeast.

    Zone One: The Atlantic Coast

    The Atlantic shore runs from Tangier in the north down past Rabat, Casablanca, El Jadida, Essaouira and Agadir. This strip holds most of Morocco’s population and nearly all of its industry. The water stays cool, and the wind blows hard, which is why surfers love it.

    Casablanca sits at 33.6° N. Essaouira lies further south at 31.5° N, yet it often feels colder because of the Atlantic breeze. Our Essaouira day trip from Marrakech crosses from the hot interior to that windy coast in under three hours.

    Zone Two: The Mediterranean North and the Rif

    North of Fes, the Rif Mountains rise steeply and run about 250 kilometres from Tangier east toward Nador. This is Morocco’s greenest region. Rain falls generously, cork oak and cedar cover the slopes, and the blue town of Chefchaouen sits in a fold of the hills at around 600 metres.

    The Mediterranean beaches below the Rif feel completely different from the Atlantic. The sea stays warm and calm. Meanwhile, Spanish influence lingers strongly, since Spain governed this zone until 1956. The coastal town of Asilah, just south of Tangier, shows that Andalusian character clearly.

    Zone Three: The Atlas Mountains

    Three mountain chains cross the country diagonally. The Middle Atlas rises behind Fes and Ifrane. Further south, the High Atlas forms the great spine below Marrakech. Beyond it, the Anti-Atlas runs toward the desert edge.

    Meanwhile, Mount Toubkal crowns the High Atlas at 4,167 metres, which makes it the highest peak in North Africa. Snow covers these summits from roughly December to April. Therefore travellers can genuinely ski in the morning and reach palm groves the same afternoon. The Imlil Valley day trip takes you to the foot of Toubkal in about ninety minutes from Marrakech.

    Zone Four: The Sahara and the Pre-Sahara

    Beyond the Atlas, the land dries out fast. Rivers such as the Draa, the longest in Morocco at about 1,100 kilometres, carve oasis valleys through the rock. Kasbahs guard the old caravan routes. Then, finally, the sand seas begin.

    Two great dune fields draw visitors. Erg Chebbi rises near Merzouga in the southeast, with dunes reaching around 150 metres. Erg Chigaga lies further west beyond Zagora, and it feels wilder because no paved road reaches it.

    Where Is Morocco’s Sahara? Not Where Most People Assume

    Here is the detail that reshapes most itineraries. The Sahara does not start outside Marrakech. Merzouga sits roughly 560 kilometres southeast of the city, and the drive crosses a 2,260-metre mountain pass called Tizi n’Tichka. Expect eight to ten hours behind the wheel.

    Because of that distance, a genuine Erg Chebbi trip needs three days minimum. Our 3-day tour from Marrakech to the desert is the shortest honest version. The 4-day Sahara tour adds breathing room, and the 5-day desert itinerary lets you slow down properly.

    Why Marrakech Is Not the Capital

    Marrakech attracts the most visitors, so many people assume it governs the country. It does not. Rabat has held that role since 1912, and every ministry and embassy sits there. Marrakech did rule Morocco under the Almoravids and Almohads, which is where the legend comes from. Our article on whether Marrakech is the capital of Morocco untangles the whole story.

    Which City Should You Fly Into?

    Geography should decide this, not price alone. Marrakech works best for the High Atlas, the southern kasbah routes and Erg Chebbi. Fes suits travellers heading to the Middle Atlas and the desert by the northern route. Casablanca offers the widest long-haul network, while Tangier serves anyone arriving from Spain.

    From Europe: A Short Hop

    Morocco sits closer to most European capitals than many European capitals sit to each other. Madrid to Marrakech takes less time than Madrid to Berlin. Budget carriers therefore serve Marrakech, Fes, Agadir and Tangier heavily.

    Our complete guide to flights to Morocco covers airlines, seasons and airports. For Marrakech specifically, see our Marrakech airport and booking guide.

    From North America: One Flight, No Change

    Royal Air Maroc operates non-stop services from New York, Washington, Miami and Montreal into Casablanca. The crossing takes about seven hours eastbound. Consequently, Morocco is often the fastest way for North Americans to reach Africa.

    From the Gulf, Asia and Australia

    Travellers from the Gulf usually connect through Doha, Dubai or Istanbul. Visitors from East Asia and Australia normally route through Europe or the Gulf. Total journey time from Sydney runs to around 24 hours, since Morocco sits almost exactly on the opposite side of the world.

    What Time Is It Where Morocco Is?

    Where is Morocco on the clock? The country handles time differently from its neighbours, and that catches out even frequent visitors.

    GMT+1 All Year, Except Ramadan

    Morocco abolished seasonal clock changes in 2018 and adopted GMT+1 permanently. However, the country steps back to GMT during Ramadan each year, then returns to GMT+1 afterwards. In 2026, for example, the clocks moved back on 15 February.

    As a result, the gap between Morocco and the UK shifts through the year. Sometimes London matches Marrakech. Sometimes Marrakech runs an hour ahead. Our live guide to what time it is in Morocco now keeps the current answer in one place.

    Why the Time Zone Feels Strange at Sunset

    Morocco sits well west on the globe, yet it runs on a clock an hour ahead of Greenwich. Because of that, sunset arrives late. In June the sun can stay up past 8.30 pm in Marrakech. Plan dinner accordingly, and expect the medina to stay busy long after dark.

    How Do You Actually Get to Morocco?

    Where is Morocco reachable from, in practice? Its position gives it three genuine entry routes. Very few countries outside Europe offer that much choice.

    By Air: The Standard Route

    In addition, Morocco operates several international airports. The national tourist board, Visit Morocco, lists them all. Casablanca Mohammed V handles the widest network. Marrakech Menara serves the largest number of tourists. Fes, Tangier, Agadir, Rabat and Ouarzazate all take international flights as well.

    By Ferry: Europe to Africa in an Hour

    Fast ferries link Spain with northern Morocco daily. Tarifa to Tangier Ville takes about one hour. Algeciras to Tanger Med takes roughly ninety minutes, and it carries cars. Additional routes connect Almeria and Motril with Nador and Al Hoceima.

    Longer crossings run from Genoa in Italy and from Sete in France. Those take a day or more, but they let travellers bring a vehicle without driving through Spain.

    By Land: Limited but Real

    You can walk into Morocco from Ceuta or Melilla, both Spanish cities on African soil. The Algerian border stays closed. The Mauritanian border at Guerguerat remains open for overlanders heading south. In practice, therefore, almost everyone arrives by plane or ferry.

    Do You Need a Visa to Get There?

    Citizens of the European Union, the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and the Gulf states enter without a visa for up to 90 days. Officials expect a passport valid well beyond your stay, and six months of validity remains the safe standard. Rules change, so always confirm with a Moroccan consulate before booking.

    The Train Changes the Northern Half

    Morocco runs Al Boraq, the first high-speed rail line in Africa. It opened in November 2018 and reaches 320 kilometres per hour. Tangier to Casablanca now takes about two hours and ten minutes instead of nearly five.

    An extension toward Marrakech is under construction ahead of the 2030 World Cup. Once it opens, the northern corridor will shrink further. Meanwhile, the south still belongs to the road, and that is where a private driver earns his keep.

    Choosing a Starting City Based on Geography

    Think of Morocco as a triangle. Marrakech, Fes and Tangier sit at the corners, and the desert sits below the southern edge. Your entry point therefore decides the shape of your loop.

    Arrive in Marrakech and the Atlas plus Erg Chebbi become the natural circuit. Land in Fes instead, and the Middle Atlas cedar forests plus Merzouga open first.     Short trips work, provided they stay regional. Three or four days suit Marrakech plus the Atlas or a fast desert run. Seven days let you link Marrakech, the Sahara and Fes without rushing. Ten days or more allow the full circuit, coast included.

    Climate: Why Location Changes the Weather So Much

    Morocco’s position between ocean, mountain and desert produces enormous variety. Two towns 200 kilometres apart can differ by twenty degrees on the same afternoon.

    Four Climates in One Country

    The north and the Atlantic coast enjoy a Mediterranean climate, with mild wet winters and warm dry summers. Rainfall peaks in the Rif, which stays green all year. Higher up, the Atlas ranges turn alpine above 2,000 metres, complete with winter snow. Beyond the mountains, the southeast turns arid and then fully Saharan.

    Altitude Beats Latitude

    Travellers often assume that heading south means heading into heat. In Morocco, height matters more than latitude. Ifrane, in the Middle Atlas, has recorded some of Africa’s coldest temperatures. Meanwhile, Marrakech sits further south at only 466 metres and bakes in July.

    Pack for both. Desert nights drop sharply once the sun sets, even in summer, because dry air holds no heat. Our guide to the best time to visit Morocco breaks the year down region by region.

    What the Ocean Does to the Coast

    The cold Canary Current runs down Morocco’s Atlantic shore. It cools the water and feeds the fog that rolls into Essaouira and Agadir. Therefore Atlantic beaches stay refreshing in August while Marrakech, only three hours inland, climbs past 40°C. Consequently, Moroccan families use that gap every single summer.

    The Cultural Consequences of Sitting Between Worlds

    Where is Morocco culturally, then? Its position shaped the culture as decisively as it shaped the climate. Trade routes crossed here for two thousand years, and each one left something behind.

    A Crossroads, Not a Corner

    Phoenicians landed on the Atlantic coast first. Later, Rome built Volubilis inland. Arab armies arrived in the seventh century. Andalusian refugees crossed from Spain after 1492 and rebuilt whole quarters in Fes, Tetouan and Rabat. Later, France and Spain both governed parts of the country.

    Meanwhile, camel caravans crossed the Sahara from Timbuktu carrying gold, salt and manuscripts. Marrakech grew rich as the northern terminus of that trade. Consequently, Morocco absorbed African, Arab, Amazigh, Mediterranean and European influences at once.

    You Can Taste the Geography

    Moroccan cooking records every one of those arrivals. Saffron and preserved lemon came through Mediterranean trade. Couscous is Amazigh. Sweet-savoury pastilla carries Andalusian fingerprints. Sub-Saharan spice routes supplied the rest. Our guide to cuisine in Morocco follows those threads dish by dish.

    Nine UNESCO Sites in One Country

    Morocco holds nine UNESCO World Heritage sites, including the medinas of Marrakesh and Fes, Roman Volubilis, and the earthen kasbah of Ait Ben Haddou. Their spread across the map mirrors the country’s zones. You can reach Ait Ben Haddou on a single day trip from Marrakech.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Where Morocco Is

    Where is Morocco located on the world map?

    Morocco lies in the northwest corner of Africa, between roughly 27° and 36° north latitude and 1° to 13° west longitude. The Atlantic Ocean borders it to the west and the Mediterranean Sea to the north. Algeria sits to the east, Mauritania to the south, and Spain lies 14 kilometres across the Strait of Gibraltar.

    Where is Morocco in relation to Spain?

    Morocco sits directly south of Spain, separated only by the Strait of Gibraltar. The narrowest crossing measures about 14.2 kilometres. Fast ferries from Tarifa reach Tangier in roughly one hour, so travellers can move between the two continents in a single morning.

    Is Morocco in Africa or the Middle East?

    Morocco is in Africa. It sits on the African continent, belongs to the African Union, and shares no border with any Middle Eastern country. It does belong to the Arab League culturally, which explains why some people place it in the Middle East by mistake.

    Where is Morocco’s capital city?

    Rabat serves as the capital, and it sits on the Atlantic coast at about 34° N, 6.8° W. Casablanca, roughly 90 kilometres south, is the larger city and the main economic hub. Marrakech, despite its fame, has not governed the country since the 1500s.

    Which countries border Morocco?

    Algeria borders Morocco to the east along roughly 1,550 kilometres, though that frontier has stayed closed since 1994. Mauritania borders the far south. Spain holds two small enclaves, Ceuta and Melilla, which give Morocco a rare land border with the European Union.

    How far is Morocco from the United Kingdom?

    London sits about 2,300 kilometres from Marrakech in a straight line. Non-stop flights take roughly three and a half hours. That makes Morocco one of the closest genuinely different destinations available to British travellers.

    How far is Morocco from the United States?

    New York lies about 5,800 kilometres from Casablanca. Royal Air Maroc flies the route non-stop in around seven hours and fifteen minutes. Morocco is therefore among the quickest African destinations to reach from the East Coast.

    Where is Morocco’s Sahara Desert?

    The famous dunes sit in the southeast. Erg Chebbi lies beside Merzouga, about 560 kilometres from Marrakech and 470 kilometres from Fes. Erg Chigaga lies further west beyond Zagora. Neither is a day trip, so plan at least three days. Our Merzouga camel trekking guide explains what happens once you arrive.

    Is Morocco close to Egypt?

    No. Roughly 4,000 kilometres separate Marrakech from Cairo, with Algeria, Tunisia and Libya in between. As a result, flights between them take around five to six hours. The two countries sit at opposite ends of North Africa.

    Where is Morocco’s highest mountain?

    Mount Toubkal rises to 4,167 metres in the High Atlas, about 60 kilometres south of Marrakech. It is the highest peak in North Africa and the wider Arab world. Trekkers therefore start from the village of Imlil, and most reach the summit in two days.

    What time zone is Morocco in?

    Morocco runs on GMT+1 for most of the year. During Ramadan the country shifts back to GMT, then returns afterwards. In 2026 that change took effect on 15 February.

    Do I need a visa to visit Morocco?

    Most Western nationalities, including EU, UK, US, Canadian, Australian and Japanese citizens, enter visa-free for up to 90 days. However, officials expect a passport with at least six months of validity. Always confirm current rules with a Moroccan consulate before you travel.

    Where is Morocco best entered from, if you start in Europe?

    Tangier is the natural gateway for anyone travelling from Spain by sea. Marrakech suits travellers flying in for the Atlas and the desert. Casablanca offers the widest choice of long-haul connections.

    Where Is Morocco? A Final Word Before You Book

    Where is Morocco? It sits at the meeting point of the Atlantic and the Mediterranean, the Atlas and the Sahara, Africa and Europe. Very few countries occupy such a productive crossroads, and even fewer let a traveller experience all of it within a single week.

    That location is not trivia. It explains why you can surf at breakfast and reach snow by lunch. Equally, it explains why a two-hour flight from Madrid delivers a landscape that feels a continent away, because it is. Above all, it explains why itineraries need honest planning, since Morocco is the size of Sweden and its mountains slow every journey down.
