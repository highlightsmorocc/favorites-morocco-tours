    Morocco in 10 Days: Day-by-Day Itinerary Covering Imperial Cities, the Sahara Desert, and the Atlantic Coast (2026)

    The best 10-day Morocco itinerary for 2026: Casablanca, Chefchaouen, Fes, Sahara desert, Dades, Ait Ben Haddou, Marrakech and Essaouira, with distances, costs and tips.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 30 September 2026

    A 10-day Morocco itinerary is the sweet spot for a first trip. In ten days you can see the imperial cities of Fes and Marrakech, the blue streets of Chefchaouen, a night in the Sahara at Merzouga, the Todgha and Dades gorges, Ait Ben Haddou and the Atlantic breeze of Essaouira. Moreover, the pace stays human: you will never drive more than one long day in a row.

    People who search for a morocco itinerary 10 days, a 10 day morocco tour or simply 10 days in Morocco usually want the same thing. They want a realistic plan with kilometres, hours on the road, where to sleep and what is worth paying for. That is exactly what this guide gives you, updated for 2026.

    At Favorites Morocco Tours we drive this route every week of the year. In addition, every tip below comes from our own drivers and guides, not from a desk. If you prefer a ready-to-book version, see our 10 Days Morocco Itinerary from Casablanca or the northern 10 days tour from Tangier.

    Quick answer: the best 10-day Morocco itinerary for a first visit is Casablanca → Rabat → Chefchaouen → Volubilis and Meknes → Fes (2 nights) → Merzouga desert camp → Todgha Gorge and Dades Valley → Ait Ben Haddou → Marrakech (2 nights) → Essaouira → Casablanca. It covers about 2,100 km, four UNESCO sites and one night in the Sahara. The best months are March to May and late September to November.

    Why a 10-Day Morocco Itinerary Works So Well

    Morocco rewards travelers who give it time. A five- or seven-day trip usually forces you to choose between the desert and the north. With ten days, however, you can follow a natural story: modern Casablanca, the calm Rif Mountains, the spiritual medina of Fes, the silence of the dunes, the kasbahs of the south, the energy of Marrakech and, finally, the sea air of Essaouira.

    Enough time for the “big five” experiences

    A well-paced 10-day Morocco itinerary includes the five experiences almost every first-time visitor wants:

    • A full guided day inside the medina of Fes, the largest car-free old city in the world.
    • A camel ride and one night in a camp in the Erg Chebbi dunes.
    • The High Atlas crossing over the Tizi n’Tichka pass at 2,260 m.
    • A full day in Marrakech, including the evening show of Jemaa el-Fna.
    • A slow afternoon by the ocean in Essaouira or Rabat.

    Why the pace matters

    Distances in Morocco look short on a map, yet mountain roads are slow. For that reason, this route alternates long driving days with “stay” days. You sleep two nights in Fes and two nights in Marrakech. As a result, you unpack only four times in ten days, which makes a huge difference to how you feel.

    For shorter trips, see our 7 days in Morocco itinerary or five days in Morocco. If you have more time, our 2 weeks in Morocco itinerary adds Chefchaouen hikes, the Atlas and more coast.

    Pro tip: book your desert camp and your riads in Fes and Marrakech two to four months ahead for April, May, October and the Christmas weeks. These are the busiest periods for any 10 days Morocco itinerary.

    Best Time to Visit Morocco for 10 Days

    Morocco has four very different seasons. Because a 10-day Morocco itinerary mixes coast, mountains and desert, you need a season that works everywhere at once. For a full month-by-month view, read our guide to the best time to visit Morocco.

    Spring (March to May): the favourite

    Days are warm, nights in the desert are fresh and the Atlas is still green. In May, the Valley of Roses celebrates its harvest in Kelaat M’Gouna. This is the most popular window, so book early.

    Autumn (late September to November): the secret favourite

    The summer heat is gone, the light is golden and the dates are harvested in the Ziz and Draa valleys. In addition, prices are slightly lower than in spring. Since Morocco returned to GMT on 20 September 2026, the sun now sets about one hour earlier than in previous autumns, so plan your desert sunset around 18:00 in October.

    Winter (December to February)

    Days are sunny and pleasant in Marrakech and Merzouga. Nights, however, drop close to 0°C in the desert and the Tichka pass can close for a few hours after snow. Pack a warm jacket and choose a camp with heated tents.

    Summer (June to August)

    The Sahara often passes 40°C (104°F). A 10-day tour still works if you visit sites early, rest at midday and add more coast. Chefchaouen, Essaouira and the Atlas feel much cooler.

    Ramadan and holidays

    Ramadan 2027 is expected to start around 8 February 2027. During the holy month, some cafés close in daytime, yet tourist restaurants stay open and the evenings are festive. It is a wonderful time to travel if you stay flexible.

    Complete Day-by-Day 10-Day Morocco Itinerary

    This plan starts in Casablanca, the main international airport. A Tangier start is explained later. Each day includes the highlights, the driving time, where to sleep and one local tip that most guides forget to mention.

    Day 1: Arrival in Casablanca – The Hassan II Mosque

    Your adventure begins at Mohammed V International Airport. Your driver meets you at arrivals and takes you to your hotel. If you land before 14:00, visit the Hassan II Mosque, one of the largest mosques in the world and one of the few in Morocco open to non-Muslims. Its ocean-front setting, carved cedar and zellige tiles are a perfect first lesson in Moroccan craftsmanship.

    Afterwards, walk the Corniche promenade at sunset and enjoy a fresh orange juice. Casablanca is modern and business-like, so one evening here is enough.

    • Driving: airport to city centre 30–45 minutes.
    • Overnight: Casablanca.
    • Local tip: guided mosque visits run several times a day except Friday morning; the ticket costs about 140 MAD.

    Day 2: Casablanca – Rabat – Chefchaouen, the Blue Pearl

    After breakfast, drive north along the Atlantic to Rabat, the calm capital. Visit the Hassan Tower, the Mausoleum of Mohammed V and the white-and-blue lanes of the Kasbah of the Udayas above the Bou Regreg river.

    Then climb into the green Rif Mountains. You reach Chefchaouen in the late afternoon, just as the blue walls start to glow. Wander the medina, sit in the Outa el Hammam square and watch the town light up. Our guide to Chefchaouen, Morocco lists the best viewpoints and cafés.

    • Driving: Casablanca–Rabat 1 h 15; Rabat–Chefchaouen 3.5–4 h.
    • Overnight: riad in Chefchaouen.
    • Local tip: photograph the famous blue stairs before 9:00, when the lanes are still empty.

    Day 3: Chefchaouen – Volubilis – Meknes – Fes

    Enjoy a slow morning. If you like walking, climb 30 minutes to the Spanish Mosque for a view over the whole blue town. Then head south to Volubilis, the best-preserved Roman city in Morocco and a UNESCO site. Its mosaics of Orpheus and the Labours of Hercules are still in their original place.

    Next, stop in Meknes, the imperial city of Sultan Moulay Ismail. Admire Bab Mansour, one of the finest gates in Africa, and the huge royal granaries. You reach Fes in the early evening.

    • Driving: Chefchaouen–Volubilis 2.5–3 h; Volubilis–Meknes 30 min; Meknes–Fes 1 h.
    • Overnight: riad in the Fes medina.
    • Local tip: Volubilis has little shade; visit with a hat and water, especially from May to September.

    Day 4: Full-Day Guided Tour of the Fes Medina

    Today belongs to Fes el-Bali, a UNESCO World Heritage medina with more than 9,000 alleys. A licensed local guide is essential here. Highlights include the Bou Inania Madrasa, the Al Quaraouiyine mosque and university (founded in 859), the Chouara tanneries seen from a terrace, the Nejjarine fountain and the workshops of potters, weavers and coppersmiths.

    In the afternoon, drive up to the Merinid Tombs for a sunset view over the whole medina. Fes is also a great place to taste the dishes in our guide to traditional Moroccan dishes, especially pastilla and harira.

    • Overnight: Fes.
    • Local tip: the tanneries smell strong; shops give you a sprig of mint to hold under your nose.

    Day 5: Fes – Middle Atlas – Ziz Valley – Merzouga Desert

    This is the longest day of your 10-day Morocco itinerary, but also one of the most beautiful. First, stop in Ifrane, the “Switzerland of Morocco”, then look for Barbary macaques in the cedar forest near Azrou. After Midelt, the land turns red and dry until you reach the palm groves of the Ziz Valley.

    In Merzouga, you leave the car and ride a camel into the Erg Chebbi dunes for sunset. Dinner is a tagine under the stars, followed by Berber drums around the fire. Not sure which desert to pick? Compare them in Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga.

    • Driving: 470 km, 7–8 h with stops.
    • Overnight: desert camp in Erg Chebbi.
    • Local tip: leave your big suitcase in the car and take only a small bag on the camel.

    Day 6: Merzouga – Rissani – Todgha Gorge – Dades Valley

    Wake up early for sunrise over the dunes, the most magical moment of many trips. After breakfast, ride back to Merzouga and continue west through Rissani, an old caravan town with a lively market on Sundays, Tuesdays and Thursdays. Near Erfoud, you can visit a fossil workshop.

    Then comes the Todgha Gorge, where limestone walls rise about 300 m above a clear river. Walk into the narrowest part, only 10 m wide. Finally, follow the Dades Valley, with its “Monkey Fingers” rocks and the famous zig-zag road of Tisdrine.

    • Driving: Merzouga–Todgha 3–3.5 h; Todgha–Dades 1 h.
    • Overnight: kasbah hotel in the Dades Valley.
    • Local tip: ask your driver to stop at the Tisdrine viewpoint café for the best photo of the winding road.

    Day 7: Dades – Skoura – Ait Ben Haddou – Tizi n’Tichka – Marrakech

    Drive through the Valley of Roses and the palm oasis of Skoura, home of the Amridil kasbah shown on the old 50-dirham note. Continue past Ouarzazate, the “Hollywood of Africa”, to Ait Ben Haddou. This UNESCO-listed ksar of mud brick appears in Gladiator, Game of Thrones and many other productions. Climb to the old granary for a wide view over the Ounila river.

    In the afternoon, you cross the High Atlas by the Tizi n’Tichka pass (2,260 m). The road winds past Berber villages and walnut trees before dropping to the red plains of Marrakech. Learn more about this range in our guide to the Atlas Mountains of Morocco.

    • Driving: Dades–Ait Ben Haddou 3 h; Ait Ben Haddou–Marrakech 3.5–4 h.
    • Overnight: riad in Marrakech.
    • Local tip: entering the ksar of Ait Ben Haddou is free; some families charge a small fee to see inside their houses.

    Day 8: Full-Day Guided Tour of Marrakech

    Marrakech, the Red City, is pure energy. With a guide, you visit the Koutoubia Mosque (outside), the Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs, the Ben Youssef Madrasa and the souks of dyers, leather and spices. Enter by one of the historic gates of the Marrakech medina, such as Bab Agnaou.

    At dusk, go to Jemaa el-Fna, listed by UNESCO for its storytellers, musicians and food stalls. Alternatively, relax in the Majorelle Garden or book a traditional hammam. Our guide to the Moroccan hammam explains how it works.

    • Overnight: Marrakech.
    • Local tip: buy Majorelle tickets online in advance; slots often sell out in high season.

    Day 9: Marrakech – Essaouira, the Windy City

    Leave the noise of Marrakech for the relaxed port of Essaouira. On the way, you pass argan forests where goats sometimes climb the trees, and a women’s argan cooperative. Essaouira’s white-and-blue medina, UNESCO-listed ramparts and busy fishing harbour offer a total change of rhythm.

    Walk the Skala ramparts, watch the blue boats unload the catch and choose your own fish to grill at the port stalls. The town is also famous for thuya wood, Gnawa music and kitesurfing.

    • Driving: Marrakech–Essaouira 2.5–3 h.
    • Overnight: Essaouira.
    • Local tip: bring a windbreaker; the Alizé wind blows most afternoons from April to September.

    Day 10: Essaouira – Casablanca – Departure

    Enjoy a last coffee on the beach, then drive to Casablanca along the coastal motorway. Depending on your flight time, you can stop in El Jadida, whose Portuguese cistern is another UNESCO site, or go straight to the airport.

    This completes the classic 10-day Morocco itinerary: a modern city, a blue mountain town, two imperial medinas, the Sahara, the gorges, the High Atlas and the Atlantic. Few countries offer so much variety in such a short trip.

    • Driving: about 4 h to Mohammed V airport.
    • Local tip: with an early flight, book an open-jaw ticket and fly home from Marrakech Menara airport instead; visit Essaouira as a day trip on day 9.

    Starting Points: Casablanca, Tangier or Marrakech

    Where you land changes the shape of your 10-day Morocco itinerary more than anything else. Check our guide to flights to Morocco to compare airports.

    Starting in Casablanca

    Most long-haul flights from North America, the Gulf and Africa land in Casablanca. For that reason, the loop above is the most practical. See all our tours from Casablanca, from 6 to 15 days.

    Starting in Tangier

    Tangier is ideal if you arrive by ferry from Tarifa or Algeciras in Spain, or by the Al Boraq high-speed train. The route becomes Tangier → Asilah → Chefchaouen → Fes → Merzouga → Dades → Ait Ben Haddou → Marrakech, with a return flight from Marrakech. Our 10 days tour from Tangier follows exactly this line, and our Morocco tours from Spain explain the ferry step by step.

    Starting in Marrakech

    Many European low-cost flights land in Marrakech. In that case, simply reverse the loop: Marrakech → Ait Ben Haddou → Dades → Merzouga → Fes → Chefchaouen → Rabat → Casablanca, then fly home from Casablanca or return to Marrakech by train.

    Three Alternative 10-Day Routes

    The classic loop is not the only good 10-day Morocco itinerary. These three variations suit different travelers.

    1. The slow south loop from Marrakech

    Marrakech (3 nights) → Ait Ben Haddou → Dades → Merzouga (2 nights in the desert) → Draa Valley → Ouarzazate → Imlil in the Atlas → Marrakech. Less driving, more walking, and no imperial cities of the north. Perfect for a second trip or for families.

    2. North and Sahara for culture lovers

    Tangier → Tetouan → Chefchaouen (2 nights) → Fes (2 nights) → Merzouga → Todgha → Fes → Rabat → Casablanca. This version adds Tetouan’s UNESCO medina and more time in Chefchaouen, yet skips Marrakech.

    3. Morocco with a beach finish

    Follow days 1–8 of the classic route, then spend two full nights in Essaouira or continue to Agadir or Taghazout for surf and sun. Ideal for couples who want to end the trip slowly.

    Riads are traditional houses built around a central courtyard. They are quiet, beautiful and often family-run, so breakfast on the roof terrace becomes a daily highlight. Note that cars cannot enter most medinas; your driver will park at the nearest gate and a porter will carry your bags. Finally, in the desert, a luxury camp costs more but offers private bathrooms, hot showers and proper beds, which many travelers find worth every dirham on a 10-day Morocco itinerary.

    7 Common Mistakes to Avoid on a 10-Day Morocco Itinerary

    After years of planning trips, we see the same errors again and again. Avoid them and your 10-day Morocco itinerary will feel twice as relaxed.

    1. Trying to see everything. Adding Agadir, Dakhla or the Mediterranean coast turns ten days into a driving marathon. Keep them for your next trip.
    2. Only one night in Fes or Marrakech. Arriving at night and leaving the next morning means you see almost nothing of the medina.
    3. Going to the desert and back in two days from Marrakech. That is 18 hours in a car for one night in the dunes. Instead, cross the desert as part of a loop, as in this plan.
    4. Forgetting the time change. Since 20 September 2026, Morocco uses GMT all year, one hour behind Spain in summer. Check your transfer and flight times twice.
    5. Carrying only cards. Villages, camps and tea stops need cash, and card terminals often fail in the south.
    6. Following unofficial guides. Politely say “no, shukran” to people who offer to show you the way; book licensed guides through your riad or agency.
    7. Packing for summer only. Even in May, the desert and the Atlas can be cold at night.

    Who Is This 10-Day Morocco Itinerary For?

    First-time visitors

    This is the perfect first route. It shows you the four faces of the country — cities, mountains, desert and ocean — so you can decide which region to explore more deeply next time.

    Families with children

    Kids love the camels, the monkeys near Azrou and the fishing port of Essaouira. We suggest adding one more night in Merzouga, choosing riads with a pool and breaking the Fes–Merzouga day with longer stops.

    Couples and honeymooners

    Choose a luxury desert camp, a rooftop dinner in Marrakech and a boutique riad in Essaouira. A private tour lets you set the rhythm, with late breakfasts and sunset stops.

    Photographers

    Chefchaouen at dawn, the dunes at sunrise, the Todgha walls at noon and Ait Ben Haddou in the late light are a dream. Ask your driver to adjust the timing of each stop to the best light.

    What to Pack for 10 Days in Morocco

    Packing smart matters on a multi-stop trip, because you move between sea level, 2,260 m passes and the Sahara. For outfit ideas, see what to wear in Morocco.

    • Layers: T-shirts for the day, a fleece and a light down jacket for desert nights.
    • Modest clothes: shoulders and knees covered in medinas and villages; a light scarf is useful for everyone.
    • Good shoes: closed walking shoes for the medinas and sandals for the dunes.
    • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses and SPF 50 sunscreen.
    • Power adapter: Morocco uses type C and E plugs, 220 V.
    • Reusable bottle: buy large water bottles and refill; many riads offer filtered water.
    • Small backpack: for the camel ride and guided walks.
    • Cash in small notes: for tips, markets and tea stops.

    Soft bags are easier than hard suitcases, since riads often have narrow stairs and cars have limited space.

    Practical Tips: Visa, Money, SIM Card and Safety

    Visa and passport

    Citizens of the USA, Canada, the UK, the EU and Australia do not need a visa for stays of up to 90 days. Your passport should be valid for at least six months. Always check the latest rules with your government before booking.

    Money and cards

    The dirham (MAD) is a closed currency, so you get it in Morocco. ATMs are common in cities, but rare between Midelt and Dades. Withdraw enough cash in Fes before the desert day.

    SIM card and internet

    A local SIM from Maroc Telecom, Orange or Inwi costs very little and gives good 4G, even in parts of the desert. Our article on the SIM card in Morocco explains how to buy one at the airport.

    Safety

    Morocco is one of the safest countries in Africa for travelers. The main issues are pickpockets in crowded souks and fake guides near medina gates. Read our honest analysis in Is Morocco safe?

    Etiquette

    Ask before photographing people, eat with your right hand when sharing a dish, and bargain with a smile. A few words of Darija, such as salam (hello) and shukran (thank you), open many doors.

    Frequently Asked Questions About a 10-Day Morocco Itinerary

    1. Is 10 days enough time to see Morocco?

    Yes. Ten days is enough to see Fes, Marrakech, Chefchaouen, the Sahara, the Todgha and Dades gorges, Ait Ben Haddou and the Atlantic coast without rushing. You will not see the far south or the Mediterranean coast, but you will cover the country’s most iconic places. For this reason, most travel experts call ten days the sweet spot for a first visit.

    2. Should I start in Casablanca, Tangier or Marrakech?

    Start where your flight is cheapest. Casablanca suits long-haul arrivals, Tangier suits travelers coming from Spain by ferry, and Marrakech suits European low-cost flights. The route simply rotates around the same loop. We can adapt any version, like our 10 Days Morocco Itinerary from Casablanca or the 10 days tour from Tangier.

    3. How much does a 10-day Morocco tour cost?

    A mid-range private 10-day Morocco tour usually costs 1,200 to 2,000 USD per person for two people sharing, including driver, riads, desert camp and some meals. Budget travelers can spend about 600 USD, while luxury trips start around 3,000 USD. Group size, season and hotel level change the final price.

    4. Do I need a guide in Fes and Marrakech?

    A licensed guide is strongly recommended in Fes and useful in Marrakech. The Fes medina is a maze of thousands of alleys, and a guide saves you hours while explaining history, crafts and daily life. Besides, an official guide keeps unofficial “helpers” away. Private tours with Favorites Morocco Tours include guided visits in both cities.

    5. What is the desert night like?

    You ride a camel for about one hour into the Erg Chebbi dunes, watch the sunset, eat a tagine dinner and listen to Berber music by the fire. Camps range from simple tents with shared bathrooms to luxury tents with private showers and real beds. Next, you wake up for sunrise over the dunes. For most guests, it is the highlight of the whole 10 days in Morocco.

    6. Is Morocco safe for a 10-day trip?

    Yes, Morocco is generally safe for tourists, including families and women traveling together. Normal precautions are enough: watch your bag in the souks, use licensed guides and agree on taxi prices first. Traveling with a private driver adds local support at every step of your 10-day Morocco itinerary.

    7. Can I do this itinerary by train?

    Only partly. Trains connect Tangier, Rabat, Casablanca, Meknes, Fes and Marrakech, and the Al Boraq high-speed line links Tangier and Casablanca in about two hours. However, no train reaches Chefchaouen, Merzouga, Dades or Essaouira. So a mix of train and a private desert tour, such as a 3-day trip from Fes to Marrakech, is the best budget combination.

    8. Can I customize a 10-day Morocco itinerary?

    Absolutely. Private tours are fully flexible: you can add a cooking class, a hike in the High Atlas, a second desert night, a hot-air balloon over Marrakech or more beach time. Families often prefer shorter driving days, and couples like romantic riads and luxury camps. Simply tell us your wishes on our contact page.

    Key Takeaways

    • A 10-day Morocco itinerary covers the imperial cities, Chefchaouen, one Sahara night, the gorges, the High Atlas and the Atlantic coast.
    • The classic loop from Casablanca is about 2,100 km, with only one very long driving day (Fes to Merzouga).
    • Sleep two nights in Fes and two in Marrakech to keep the pace comfortable.
    • Travel in spring or autumn for the best weather; remember that Morocco uses GMT all year since September 2026.
