    What Year Is It in Morocco? 3 Calendars and 2027 Holidays

    What year is it in Morocco? 2026, Islamic year 1448 and Amazigh year 2976. The 3 calendars, Morocco public holidays 2027 and Ramadan dates explained.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 19 November 2026

    What year is it in Morocco? It sounds like a trick question, and in a way it is. Morocco runs on three calendars at the same time, and each one counts a different year. So the same day can be 2026, 1448 and 2976 all at once.

    This guide explains what year it is in Morocco under each calendar, which one you will actually use on your trip, and how the others shape your travel dates. You will also find the full list of Morocco public holidays for 2027, the expected Ramadan dates and the mistakes travellers make most often.

    Quick answer: what year is it in Morocco?

    In November 2026, it is the year 2026 in Morocco on the Gregorian calendar, the year 1448 on the Islamic (Hijri) calendar and the year 2976 on the Amazigh (Berber) calendar. The Gregorian calendar is the official one for government, banks, flights and hotels, exactly as in the UK or the US. The other two set the religious and cultural holidays.

    What year is it in Morocco? The full explanation

    Many visitors assume Morocco is “behind” because they hear people mention the year 1448. That is a myth. The country lives in 2026, just like you do. What happens is that Moroccan society blends several layers of identity: Islamic, Amazigh, Arab, Andalusian and Hebrew.

    Each layer keeps its own way of counting time. In fact, the 2011 Constitution explicitly recognises this diversity. So when someone asks what year it is in Morocco, the right answer depends on the context: a train ticket, a wedding or a village festival in the mountains.

    The calendar you will see on your trip

    For a traveller, the answer to what year it is in Morocco is simple: 2026. Your hotel booking, your flight, ONCF train timetables and every receipt use dates like 15/03/2027. Newspapers, banks and shops use it too.

    However, the Islamic calendar decides when Ramadan and the big festivals fall. Those dates can change your experience: restaurant hours, packed transport or closed museums.

    The Gregorian calendar: Morocco’s everyday calendar

    Morocco adopted the Gregorian calendar for administrative life during the twentieth century. Today it is the state’s reference calendar. Contracts, official documents and public timetables all follow it.

    The Moroccan weekend is Saturday and Sunday, as in Europe. Friday is a working day, although many shops close for a while at midday for prayers. So if you plan visits on a Friday, allow for that break.

    Month names in Morocco

    In Darija, Moroccan Arabic, the Gregorian months have Latin-based names. January is yennayer, February fbrayer, March mars and April abril. In fact, the name of the Amazigh festival Yennayer comes from the same root. You will hear these names in the souk, in taxis and on the radio.

    The Islamic calendar: Morocco is in the year 1448

    The Islamic, or Hijri, calendar starts counting in the year 622. That year the Prophet Muhammad migrated from Mecca to Medina. It is a lunar calendar: each month begins with the new moon.

    In Morocco, the Hijri year 1448 began on Wednesday 17 June 2026, as announced by the Ministry of Habous and Islamic Affairs and reported by Médias24. So if you ask what year it is in Morocco by the Islamic calendar, the answer is 1448.

    How the start of each month is decided

    Morocco keeps a traditional method. The ministry organises naked-eye sightings of the crescent moon from observation points across the country. Units of the Royal Armed Forces also take part. Astronomical calculations are used only as a guide.

    If nobody sees the moon on the evening of the 29th, the month runs to 30 days. That is why Morocco sometimes celebrates a festival one day after Saudi Arabia or Algeria. For example, in 2026 Algeria started the year 1448 on 16 June, and Morocco on 17 June.

    Why the Islamic year is shorter

    A lunar year lasts about 354 or 355 days, roughly 11 days less than a solar year. As a result, Ramadan and the other festivals move about 11 days earlier every year on the Gregorian calendar. In 2026 Ramadan began in February; within a decade it will fall in December.

    The 12 months of the Islamic calendar

    • Muharram: the first month. Day 1 is the Islamic New Year, a public holiday in Morocco.
    • Safar: in 1448 it began on 16 July 2026.
    • Rabi al-Awwal: the month of the Mawlid, the Prophet’s birthday.
    • Rabi al-Thani.
    • Jumada al-Ula.
    • Jumada al-Thaniya: the month running in November 2026.
    • Rajab.
    • Sha’ban: the month of preparation before the fast.
    • Ramadan: the month of fasting.
    • Shawwal: it opens with Eid al-Fitr.
    • Dhu al-Qa’da.
    • Dhu al-Hijja: the month of the pilgrimage and Eid al-Adha.

    A quick conversion trick

    Here is a handy rule of thumb: Hijri year ≈ (Gregorian year − 622) × 1.0307. Apply it to 2026 and you get 1447.2. Since 1448 began in mid-2026, the result fits. It works as a rough guide, but the exact date always depends on the moon sighting.

    The Amazigh calendar: Morocco is in the year 2976

    The third calendar is the Amazigh one, also called the Berber or agricultural calendar. In January 2026 Moroccans celebrated the year 2976. On 14 January 2027 the year 2977 begins.

    This calendar is solar. For centuries it set the rhythm of sowing, harvests and rural festivals in the Rif, the Atlas and the Souss. If you want to know more about this culture, read our guide to Berber Morocco.

    Why it starts in 950 BC

    The Amazigh count takes 950 BC as its starting point. That date is linked to Sheshonq I, a leader of Libyan-Berber origin who became pharaoh of Egypt. So if you add 950 to 2026, you get 2976. It is a modern convention, but it is now widely used across North Africa.

    Why Yennayer falls on 13 or 14 January

    In practice, the Amazigh farming calendar follows the old Julian calendar. Today the Julian calendar runs 13 days behind the Gregorian one. That is why 1 Yennayer lands on 14 January, although some families celebrate on the evening of the 13th.

    Yennayer: a national holiday since 2024

    King Mohammed VI announced on 3 May 2023 that the Amazigh New Year would become an official paid holiday. The Government Council approved the decrees on 23 November 2023, according to Hespress. As a result, 14 January 2024 was the first Yennayer public holiday in the country’s history.

    How Yennayer is celebrated

    Families gather around dishes made with grains and pulses. In many regions people cook couscous or a thick soup, and sometimes a date stone is hidden in the food: whoever finds it will be lucky all year. The traditional greeting is Assegas Ambarki, “happy new year”.

    The Hebrew calendar: the fourth layer

    Morocco has the oldest and largest Jewish community in the Arab world. That community uses the Hebrew calendar, which entered the year 5787 in September 2026. In cities such as Fes, Essaouira and Casablanca you will find synagogues, cemeteries and festivals of their own, including the hilloula pilgrimages to the tombs of revered rabbis.

    The Moroccan Constitution names the Hebrew component as part of national identity. So in a single old quarter you may pass a mosque, a mellah and a street with an Amazigh name. We explore this mix further in our article on religion in Morocco.

    What time is it in Morocco? The other thing that changed in 2026

    Alongside what year it is in Morocco, many travellers look up the time. Here there was a big change. On the night of 19 to 20 September 2026, Morocco returned to permanent GMT and dropped the GMT+1 it had used since 2018.

    In practice, Morocco now shares the same clock as the UK in winter and runs one hour behind London in summer. It also gets dark an hour earlier on the clock than in previous years. You will find every time difference in our guide what time is it in Morocco now.

    Morocco public holidays 2027: the full calendar

    Knowing what year it is in Morocco is useful, but for trip planning the holidays matter more. Morocco has civil holidays on fixed dates and religious holidays that move every year. Since 2026 there is also a new one: 31 October, Unity Day (Aid al-Wahda), decreed by the King in November 2025, according to the official portal maroc.ma.

    What closes on a public holiday

    On civil holidays, government offices, banks and many businesses close. By contrast, tourist souks, restaurants and hotels usually stay open. During the two days of Eid al-Adha and Eid al-Fitr, however, many shops shut and families travel to their home towns. Trains and coaches fill up several days ahead.

    Ramadan 2027: what changes for travellers

    Ramadan 2027 should begin around 8 or 9 February 2027 and end with Eid al-Fitr around 10 March. During that month Muslims do not eat or drink from dawn until sunset.

    Travelling during Ramadan is perfectly possible and has a special charm. The evenings are lively and the ftour, the meal that breaks the fast, is a beautiful moment to share. Still, keep a few things in mind:

    • Many local cafés and restaurants close during the day. Those in hotels and tourist areas usually stay open.
    • Museum and monument opening hours may be shorter.
    • Alcohol sales to Muslims are suspended. We explain the rules in our guide to alcohol in Morocco.
    • Eating or smoking in the street during daylight is considered disrespectful.
    • At sunset the streets empty for a few minutes, and traffic is heavy just before.

    How the Moroccan calendar affects your trip

    Knowing what year it is in Morocco under each calendar helps you pick better dates. These are the situations that matter most for visitors from the UK, the US and beyond.

    Eid al-Adha: packed transport

    During Eid al-Adha millions of Moroccans head back to their home towns, so train and coach tickets are hard to find. On a private tour with a driver, by contrast, you will barely notice. Our tours from Marrakech run all year with their own vehicle.

    August: holidays, heat and an eclipse

    August brings four national holidays and, in 2027, the Mawlid. On top of that, on 2 August 2027 a total solar eclipse will be visible in northern Morocco. It will be one of the great sky events of the decade. If you want to see it, read our solar eclipse 2026-2027 Morocco guide and book well in advance.

    Throne Day: festive cities

    On 30 July Morocco celebrates the anniversary of Mohammed VI’s accession to the throne in 1999. Expect flags, lights and public events in every city.

    Spring, autumn and Ramadan

    If you combine weather and calendar, spring and autumn remain the best seasons. In 2027, however, part of spring overlaps with Ramadan, so check your dates. For a broader overview of the country, see our guide to tourism in Morocco.

    New Year’s Eve in Morocco

    31 December is celebrated much as it is at home, especially in Marrakech and in desert camps. It is a peak date. Find out how to enjoy it in our article on New Year in Morocco.

    What year is it in Morocco at a wedding, a contract or a moussem?

    To fully understand what year it is in Morocco, real-life examples help. The calendar changes with the situation.

    On a contract or an invoice

    You will always see the Gregorian date. Sometimes official or religious documents add the Hijri date in brackets, for example: “19 November 2026, corresponding to Jumada al-Thaniya 1448”.

    At a wedding

    Families pick the date on the Gregorian calendar, usually in summer. However, they avoid Ramadan and often prefer the weeks after Eid. So the Islamic calendar shapes the choice even when it is not written down.

    At a moussem

    Many moussems, the big popular festivals with horseback fantasia shows, follow the farming cycle or the feast of a local saint. As a result, some move from month to month. To see tbourida, the charge of riders firing old rifles, read our guide to the fantasia show in Marrakech.

    At the weekly market

    In the countryside, many souks are named after the day they take place: Souk el Had (Sunday), Souk Tnine (Monday) or Souk Khemis (Thursday). In villages, the week is organised around market day more than around the number of the year.

    Common mistakes about the year in Morocco

    “Morocco is in the year 1448”

    That is only part of the truth. Ask a Moroccan in the street what year it is in Morocco and they will say 2026. The year 1448 is the religious year. Civil life, work and tourism all run on 2026, and nobody will ask you to write 1448 on a form.

    “Ramadan is always on the same dates”

    Wrong. Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, Ramadan moves about 11 days earlier every year. Do not copy dates from a previous trip.

    “Religious holidays are fixed months in advance”

    Not quite. Apps and calendars give estimated dates. The official date is announced the evening before, after the moon sighting. If your trip depends on an Eid, leave a day of margin.

    “The Amazigh year is a recent invention”

    Counting from 950 BC is modern, yes. But the farming calendar and the Yennayer festival have existed for centuries in rural areas. What is new is its status as a national holiday.

    Frequently asked questions

    What year is it in Morocco right now?

    In November 2026 it is the year 2026 on the Gregorian calendar, which is the official one. At the same time, it is the year 1448 on the Islamic calendar and 2976 on the Amazigh calendar.

    Which calendar does Morocco officially use?

    The Gregorian calendar is official for government, work and travel. The Islamic calendar sets the religious holidays and the Amazigh calendar marks Yennayer.

    When does the Islamic year 1449 begin?

    It is expected around 6 June 2027. The Ministry of Habous will confirm the exact date after sighting the new moon.

    What Amazigh year is it in 2027?

    The Amazigh year 2977 begins on 14 January 2027. That day is a national holiday in Morocco.

    When is Ramadan 2027 in Morocco?

    It should begin around 8 or 9 February 2027 and end with Eid al-Fitr around 10 March. The exact dates depend on the moon sighting.

    How many public holidays does Morocco have?

    Morocco has eleven civil and national holidays on fixed dates, including the new Unity Day on 31 October. On top of these come seven days of religious holidays that move every year.

    Is it a good idea to visit Morocco during a religious festival?

    Yes, if you plan well. Hotels and tourist restaurants stay open. The main changes are crowded public transport and some closed shops. A private tour avoids both problems.

    Is Morocco on the same time as the UK?

    In winter, yes. Since 20 September 2026 Morocco uses permanent GMT, so it matches London in winter and is one hour behind it in summer.

    Key takeaways

    • Morocco uses three main calendars at once: Gregorian (2026), Islamic (1448) and Amazigh (2976).
    • The Gregorian calendar is official for everything practical: flights, hotels, trains and documents.
    • The Islamic year 1448 began in Morocco on 17 June 2026.
    • Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year, has been a national holiday since 14 January 2024.
    • Since 2026 there is a new holiday: Unity Day on 31 October.
    • Ramadan 2027 is expected to start around 8-9 February.
    • Morocco has used permanent GMT since 20 September 2026.
