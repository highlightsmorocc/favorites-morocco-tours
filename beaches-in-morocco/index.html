    Beaches in Morocco: The 20 Best for 2026 (Atlantic & Mediterranean)

    Discover the best beaches in Morocco for 2026: 33 Blue Flag beaches, Mediterranean coves and surf in Taghazout, Essaouira and Dakhla.

    Favorites Morocco Tours · Updated: 29 September 2026

    Quick answer: the best beaches in Morocco are found in three areas. On the Mediterranean, Saïdia, Al Hoceima and the Tetouan coast stand out. On the northern Atlantic, Asilah and Oualidia. In the south, Essaouira, Taghazout, Mirleft and Dakhla. In 2026 the country has 33 Blue Flag beaches, a national record.

    Summary in 30 seconds

    • Morocco has around 3,500 km of coastline on the Mediterranean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean.
    • In 2026 the Mohammed VI Foundation for Environmental Protection awarded the Blue Flag to 38 sites: 33 beaches, 4 marinas and one mountain lake.
    • The five new Blue Flag beaches of 2026 are Oualidia Grande Plage, Tamaris II, Contrebandiers, Sable d’Or and Amsa.
    • The Mediterranean is warmer (up to 24-25 °C in August). The Atlantic is cooler (18-22 °C in summer).
    • Official lifeguard cover by the Civil Protection runs from 1 May to 30 September.
    • The south (Agadir, Taghazout, Dakhla) offers beach weather almost all year.
    • Bikinis are normal on tourist and hotel beaches. Topless sunbathing is not accepted.
    • It is easy to combine the coast with Marrakech, Fes or the Sahara Desert on one trip.

    Why the beaches in Morocco deserve a place on your trip

    Of course, most travellers come to Morocco for medinas, souks and dunes. However, few realise the country has one of the most varied coastlines in North Africa. In a few hours you can move from a turquoise Mediterranean cove to a wild Atlantic beach with perfect surf.

    In addition, the beaches in Morocco offer something rare in southern Europe: space. What is more, outside July and August, many beaches are almost empty. Even in high season, a short drive away from the cities brings you to open sand.

    Moreover, prices remain reasonable. Besides, grilled fish at the port of Essaouira or in Oualidia costs far less than on the Costa del Sol or the Algarve. Likewise, seafront stays range from simple guesthouses to five-star resorts.

    Finally, access is easy. In addition, direct flights from London, Manchester, Paris and other European cities land in Agadir, Essaouira, Tangier and Casablanca. If you are still shaping your route, our guide to planning a trip to Morocco covers the big picture.

    Two seas, 3,500 km of coast: how the shoreline works

    To understand the beaches in Morocco, it helps to split the country into two coastlines. Moreover, each one has a different climate, water temperature and atmosphere.

    The Mediterranean coast

    It runs from Tangier to Saïdia, next to the Algerian border. In general, the water is calmer and warmer. Beaches are either long stretches of fine sand or small coves under the Rif Mountains. Above all, it is the favourite summer area for Moroccan families. On the other hand, activity drops sharply out of season.

    The Atlantic coast

    It is much longer. It starts at Cape Spartel near Tangier and runs south to Dakhla and beyond. Here, however, the water is cooler and the swell stronger. That is why it is a paradise for surfing, kitesurfing and windsurfing. In addition, the trade winds keep the climate mild all year.

    Which area suits your trip

    • For warm, calm water: choose the Mediterranean in July or August.
    • Looking for surf or wind? Choose Essaouira, Taghazout or Dakhla.
    • For a winter escape: choose the southern Atlantic, especially Agadir and Dakhla.
    • To combine with imperial cities: choose Asilah, Rabat or El Jadida.

    To see how these areas link with the inland cities, read our guide to the cities in Morocco.

    Blue Flag 2026: a record year for the beaches in Morocco

    The Blue Flag is the international label for water quality, safety, services and environmental education. In Morocco it has been run since 1999 by the Mohammed VI Foundation for Environmental Protection, chaired by Princess Lalla Hasnaa.

    In July 2026 the foundation announced a new national record of 38 Blue Flag sites. Specifically, the list includes 33 beaches, 4 marinas and one natural mountain lake. Furthermore, all 28 beaches awarded in 2025 kept their label. In total, 55 applications were reviewed.

    The best Mediterranean beaches in Morocco

    The Mediterranean shore is short but beautiful. First of all, the Rif Mountains drop almost straight into the sea. As a result, you find hidden coves, clear water and fishing villages.

    Saïdia, the “blue pearl”

    Saïdia sits at the far eastern end, near the mouth of the Moulouya River. In addition, it has a fine sandy beach of about 14 km. In addition, it offers a large resort area, a marina and hotels in several categories. Moreover, the water stays shallow for many metres, which is ideal for children.

    Nearby, the Moulouya estuary nature reserve is excellent for birdwatching. In fact, it is one of the most important wetlands on the Moroccan Mediterranean.

    Al Hoceima and its coves

    For many visitors, Al Hoceima has the prettiest bay in northern Morocco. For example, Quemado beach lies right by the town. However, the real gems are a little further away: Cala Bonita, Sfiha and Tala Youssef. Offshore you can see the Peñón de Alhucemas, a small rock under Spanish sovereignty.

    Further west lies Cala Iris, inside Al Hoceima National Park. The water is so clear that it is a perfect snorkelling spot. That said, the winding Rif roads make access slow.

    Martil, Cabo Negro and M’diq

    This stretch between Tetouan and Ceuta is the liveliest in summer. First, Martil has a long promenade with cafés and restaurants. By contrast, Cabo Negro offers quieter resorts and golf courses. M’diq keeps its fishing port and a family feel.

    This area is also home to Tamuda Bay, where several luxury hotels have opened over the last decade. Moreover, the blue city of Chefchaouen is just over an hour away by car.

    Amsa, the 2026 newcomer

    Amsa lies east of Tetouan on the coastal road to Oued Laou. In fact, it is a small beach surrounded by green hills. In 2026 it flies the Blue Flag for the first time. That makes it a good choice if you want something quieter than Martil.

    Oued Laou and the Chefchaouen coast

    Oued Laou is a fishing village with a wide beach and a very local atmosphere. Further east, almost untouched coves appear. However, facilities are basic, so bring water and some food.

    Northern Atlantic beaches in Morocco: from Tangier to Larache

    Once you round Cape Spartel, the Atlantic begins. By contrast, the landscape changes to dunes, cliffs and a more open sea. In addition, these beaches sit very close to Spain, which matters if you arrive by ferry from Tarifa.

    Achakar and the Caves of Hercules

    About 15 km from Tangier lies Achakar beach, next to the famous Caves of Hercules. Besides, it is a wide beach with great sunsets. On the other hand, currents can be strong, so swim only where lifeguards are on duty.

    Asilah: a white medina and quiet beaches

    Asilah is a small walled town painted white and blue. Its town beach is convenient, but the best ones lie to the south. Paradise Beach (Sidi Mghait) can only be reached on foot, by 4×4 or by horse-drawn cart. Precisely for this reason, it keeps a wild feel. Find every detail in our Asilah guide.

    Larache and Ras R’mel beach

    Larache still shows its Spanish past in its architecture. Across the Loukkos River lies Ras R’mel, a long golden beach. Close by, you can explore the Roman ruins of Lixus, one of the oldest ancient cities in the country.

    Moulay Bousselham and the Merja Zerga lagoon

    This village has an Atlantic beach and a large protected lagoon. Moreover, in winter thousands of migrating birds arrive, including flamingos. Therefore it is an excellent choice for nature lovers.

    If you want to explore the north with a private driver, see our tours from Tangier. For example, the 7-day tour from Tangier combines the coast, Chefchaouen, Fes and the desert.

    Rabat and Casablanca: the city beaches in Morocco

    The strip between Rabat and Casablanca holds many of the Blue Flag beaches. In general, they are urban or semi-urban, with good facilities. In addition, they are perfect if you land in Casablanca and want an easy start.

    Sable d’Or and Contrebandiers, south of Rabat

    Both beaches lie in the Skhirat-Temara prefecture and received the Blue Flag for the first time in 2026. Sable d’Or is wide and family friendly. Contrebandiers, named after old-time smugglers, has rocks and small natural pools. However, both get busy on summer weekends.

    Bouznika and Dahomey

    Bouznika is a sheltered bay popular with beginner surfers. Dahomey, next door, is quieter. In August, however, traffic from Rabat and Casablanca can be heavy.

    Ain Diab and the Casablanca Corniche

    The Casablanca Corniche is the most famous seafront in the country. Moreover, it has beach clubs, pools, restaurants and nightlife. Moreover, from here you can see the Hassan II Mosque, built partly over the ocean. For swimming, many people prefer the beaches further south, such as Tamaris.

    Tamaris II, a new Blue Flag

    Tamaris is about 25 km south of Casablanca, in Nouaceur province. In 2026, Tamaris II earned the Blue Flag for the first time. It is a long beach, handy if you stay near the airport or in the south of the city.

    If you fly into Casablanca, browse our tours from Casablanca. For instance, the 8-day private tour from Casablanca lets you add a beach night at the end.

    El Jadida and Oualidia: Portuguese forts and oysters

    South of Casablanca begins an area that international tourism barely knows. However, Moroccans love it.

    El Jadida and the Portuguese City of Mazagan

    El Jadida has a long town beach and a historic jewel: the Portuguese City of Mazagan, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Indeed, its underground cistern is one of the most photogenic places in Morocco. In addition, north of town, a large golf resort faces the ocean.

    Oualidia, the finest lagoon on the Atlantic

    Oualidia is a small town with a lagoon sheltered by a rocky barrier. Inside the lagoon, however, the water is calm, which is rare on the Atlantic. That makes it ideal for swimming, kayaking or learning to windsurf.

    In addition, Oualidia is famous for its oysters. In fact, they are farmed in the lagoon itself and served in simple restaurants by the water. Since 2026, Oualidia Grande Plage flies the Blue Flag for the first time. In short, it is one of the beaches in Morocco that gained the most visibility this year.

    Safi and Souiria Kdima

    By contrast, Safi is an industrial and pottery town. Even so, to the south lies Souiria Kdima, a quiet beach with a small Portuguese fort. Furthermore, the Safi coast has waves that are famous among expert surfers.

    Essaouira and its coast: wind, history and fresh fish

    Essaouira is probably the coastal town travellers love most. Its walled medina, the old Mogador, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2001. In addition, it is about three hours from Marrakech by road.

    Essaouira beach

    The main beach forms a long bay next to the medina. However, the trade wind blows hard for much of the year. So it is better for walking, horse riding or kitesurfing than for lying in the sun. On calm days, on the other hand, it is lovely.

    Sidi Kaouki and Moulay Bouzerktoun

    Meanwhile, Sidi Kaouki is 25 km south. It is a small village with a surf vibe and nearly empty beaches. Similarly, Moulay Bouzerktoun, to the north, is a world reference for wave windsurfing. Both are perfect if you want authenticity and few tourists.

    If you only have one free day in Marrakech, our Essaouira full-day trip is the easiest way to see this coast. You can also browse all our excursions in Morocco.

    Taghazout, Imsouane and Agadir: the sunny surf south

    South of Essaouira, the coast becomes warmer and sunnier. Here you find some of the beaches in Morocco best known in the surfing world.

    Imsouane, the longest wave

    Imsouane is a fishing village whose bay produces one of the longest right-hand waves in Africa. As a result, it is perfect for learners and longboarders. In addition, the port sells fish straight off the boats.

    Taghazout and Taghazout Bay

    Taghazout went from fishing hamlet to the surf capital of the country. It has schools, surf houses and cafés with terraces. Next to it, Taghazout Bay grew into a modern resort with international hotel brands, a golf course and a promenade. As a result, two styles live side by side: the backpacker surfer and the luxury traveller.

    The best-known breaks are Anchor Point, Killer Point, Hash Point and Panorama. However, some are for experienced surfers only. Therefore, ask at a local school before paddling out.

    Agadir, the year-round beach

    Agadir was rebuilt after the 1960 earthquake. Today it is a modern city with a bay of about 10 km of sand. It enjoys more than 300 days of sunshine a year and mild temperatures even in winter. Likewise, the promenade, the marina and the hilltop kasbah round off a visit.

    Therefore, Agadir is ideal for families and for long winter stays. Moreover, its international airport has direct flights from the UK and much of Europe.

    The wild south: Mirleft, Legzira, Sidi Ifni and Plage Blanche

    If you want beaches in Morocco far from everything, this is your place. The coast between Tiznit and Guelmim is harder to reach but truly spectacular.

    Mirleft and its coves

    Mirleft is a quiet village with several coves under the cliffs: Imin Turga, Sidi Mohamed Ou Abdellah and Marabout beach. In short, it is ideal for switching off for a few days. In addition, it is a good spot for paragliding over the sea.

    Legzira and its arches

    Legzira became famous for its natural red rock arches. However, one of the two great arches collapsed in 2016. Even so, the scenery is still stunning, especially at sunset and at low tide. For this reason, keep away from the base of the cliffs.

    Sidi Ifni, the former Spanish enclave

    Sidi Ifni was Spanish territory until 1969. It still has white and blue Art Deco buildings. Moreover, its beach is wide and almost always quiet. For history lovers it offers a rare colonial-era atmosphere.

    Plage Blanche

    South of Guelmim stretches Plage Blanche, a practically untouched beach tens of kilometres long. You can only explore it by 4×4 with a guide, because the tide rises fast. Indeed, it is one of the wildest coastal landscapes on the African Atlantic.

    Dakhla: lagoon, kitesurfing and desert by the sea

    Dakhla sits on a long peninsula in the far south. Its huge lagoon, about 40 km long, has almost constant wind and shallow water. That is why it has become one of the best kitesurfing destinations in the world.

    In addition, Dakhla offers unique scenery: the White Dune in the middle of the lagoon, Dragon Island and beaches where the desert meets the ocean. On top of that, the climate is mild all year. In short, it is perfect for active travellers and anyone looking for something truly different.

    Most visitors fly in via Casablanca, although some European cities have direct flights.

    Luxury beaches in Morocco: where to stay in style

    Premium travellers have more seafront options every year. In addition, many resorts combine beach, golf, spa and a traditional Moroccan hammam.

    • Tamuda Bay (Mediterranean): five-star hotels with private beaches, including Royal Mansour Tamuda Bay.
    • El Jadida: a large resort with golf and casino on the Atlantic.
    • Taghazout Bay: international hotel brands, golf and surf in one place.
    • Agadir: thalassotherapy hotels on the bay.
    • Dakhla: eco-lodges and luxury camps on the lagoon.

    Surfing, kiting and activities on the beaches in Morocco

    The Atlantic coast receives open-ocean swell almost all year. That is why the beaches in Morocco are among the most complete surf destinations near Europe.

    Surfing

    In general, the best months are October to April. Taghazout, Imsouane, Anza and Safi are the big names. Meanwhile, for beginners, Imsouane, Bouznika and Tamraght are the best choices.

    Kitesurfing and windsurfing

    Dakhla and Essaouira lead the way. In Dakhla, for example, the wind blows almost daily. In Essaouira, by contrast, it is strongest from spring to autumn.

    Other activities

    • Horse or camel rides on the beach in Essaouira and Agadir.
    • Kayaking on the Oualidia lagoon.
    • Snorkelling in Cala Iris and Al Hoceima.
    • Birdwatching at Merja Zerga and the Moulouya estuary.
    • Paragliding in Mirleft and Aglou.
    • Quad biking in the coastal dunes north of Agadir.

    Safety on the beaches in Morocco

    In general, the beaches in Morocco are safe for tourists. However, the main risk is not crime but the sea. The Atlantic has strong currents, and many beaches have no lifeguards.

    However, the Civil Protection patrols the main beaches from 1 May to 30 September, with professional and seasonal lifeguards. In addition, drone surveillance was expanded in several areas in 2026. Even so, official figures show why caution matters: between 1 May and 15 July 2025, 49 people drowned, and two thirds of those deaths happened in unsupervised areas.

    Practical tips

    • Swim only where there are lifeguards and respect the flags (green, yellow, red).
    • Do not plunge into the water after hours in the sun.
    • Avoid swimming alone or at dusk.
    • Ask about currents before swimming on open Atlantic beaches.
    • Watch your belongings on crowded city beaches.
    • The Civil Protection emergency number is 15.

    Cultural etiquette: what to wear and how to behave

    Morocco is a Muslim country, although it is very used to tourism. On tourist beaches and at hotels, bikinis and swimsuits are completely normal. On the other hand, topless and nude sunbathing are not accepted on any public beach.

    Meanwhile, on very local beaches, some Moroccan women swim fully dressed. You are not required to do the same, but discretion helps. In addition, once you leave the sand, cover up with a T-shirt or sarong. Our guide on what to wear in Morocco has all the details.

    During Ramadan, beaches stay open. However, it is respectful not to eat or drink openly in public during the day. Also, alcohol is served only in licensed hotels, bars and restaurants.

    Getting to the beaches in Morocco from the UK, Europe and North America

    From the UK and Europe

    Low-cost and national airlines fly direct to Agadir, Essaouira, Tangier, Rabat, Casablanca and Marrakech, depending on the season. For example, flights from London take about three and a half hours. See our guide to flights to Morocco.

    From the USA and Canada

    Royal Air Maroc flies direct to Casablanca from several North American cities. From there, the Rabat and El Jadida coast is less than ninety minutes away. British, US, Canadian and Australian passport holders do not need a visa for tourist stays of up to 90 days. Always check the latest rules before you travel.

    Money and connectivity

    However, small beach villages do not always have cash machines. So carry some dirhams. Read our guide to the Moroccan dirham and pick up a SIM card in Morocco to use maps easily.

    If you want a concrete plan, this route blends the best of the Atlantic coast with a night in the dunes. Moreover, it works very well in spring and autumn, when inland heat is gentle.

    • Days 1-2: Casablanca and Rabat. Arrival, Hassan II Mosque and an afternoon at Sable d’Or beach.

    What to eat on the Moroccan coast

    Coastal food is one of the great pleasures of the trip. In the ports of Essaouira, Oualidia, Safi or Al Hoceima, you pick your fish at the stall and it is grilled on the spot.

    • Grilled sardines: cheap, tasty and typical of Safi and Essaouira.
    • Oysters from Oualidia and Dakhla: among the best in the country.
    • Fish tagine: with tomato, pepper and chermoula.
    • Seafood pastilla: the coastal version of the famous pie.
    • Sea urchins: very popular on beaches in the north and south.

    In addition, beach cafés serve mint tea, fresh juices and simple sandwiches. For the full menu, see our guide to traditional Moroccan dishes.

    Tips for enjoying the beaches in Morocco with children

    The beaches in Morocco are very family friendly. In summer, for instance, you will see Moroccan families with parasols, home-cooked food and games on the sand. Even so, choose carefully:

    • Saïdia and the Oualidia lagoon have shallow, calm water.
    • Blue Flag beaches have lifeguards and facilities.
    • Agadir has a long promenade, ideal for pushchairs.
    • Avoid the midday hours in July and August.
    • Bring your own parasol: small beaches do not always rent them.

    Hidden beaches in Morocco worth the detour

    Beyond the famous names, the coast hides quieter spots that reward travellers with a car or a private driver. Most of them have few facilities, so plan ahead.

    Tifnit and Sidi Rbat

    South of Agadir lies Souss-Massa National Park. Inside it, the fishing hamlet of Tifnit and the beach of Sidi Rbat offer empty sand, dunes and rare birds such as the northern bald ibis. In addition, the park is a peaceful break from busy Agadir.

    Aglou

    Aglou is the beach of Tiznit, about 15 km from the old walled town. Moreover, it has troglodyte fishermen’s huts carved into the cliffs and a relaxed local feel. Moreover, it is a good base between Agadir and Mirleft.

    Tarfaya

    Tarfaya, far to the south, is where the Aéropostale pilots once landed, including Antoine de Saint-Exupéry. Its beach faces the ruins of Casa del Mar, a fort built in the sea. In other words, it is a place for history lovers rather than sunbathers.

    Sidi Bouzid

    Just south of El Jadida, Sidi Bouzid is a small resort with a sheltered beach, seafood restaurants and fine sunsets. In summer it attracts families from Casablanca, while in spring it is almost empty.

    Packing list for the beaches in Morocco

    • High-factor sunscreen and a hat: the breeze hides how strong the sun is.
    • A light long-sleeved top or sarong for walking into town.
    • A windbreaker for Essaouira and the Atlantic evenings.
    • Water shoes for rocky coves in the Rif and Mirleft.
    • Cash in small notes for parking, parasols and snacks.
    • A reusable water bottle: see our guide on whether Morocco is safe for advice on drinking water.

    Common mistakes when visiting the beaches in Morocco

    • Thinking Agafay or the desert has a beach. The desert is many hours from the coast.
    • Expecting calm in Essaouira. The wind is part of its character.
    • Choosing the Mediterranean in winter. The water is cold and many services close.
    • Swimming on unguarded Atlantic beaches. The currents are dangerous.
    • Forgetting sunscreen. The Atlantic breeze is deceptive and the sun burns hard.
    • Not booking in August. Northern hotels fill up weeks ahead.
    • Trying to see everything in a few days. Distances are long. Pick one or two coastal areas.

    Frequently asked questions about the beaches in Morocco

    What is the most beautiful beach in Morocco?

    In short, it depends on what you want. For coves and clear water, Cala Iris and Al Hoceima. For a wild beach, Paradise Beach in Asilah or Plage Blanche. If you prefer a calm lagoon, Oualidia. For kitesurfing, Dakhla.

    How many beaches in Morocco have a Blue Flag in 2026?

    In 2026 there are 33 Blue Flag beaches, out of 38 awarded sites in total. It is the national record so far.

    Are the beaches in Morocco better on the Mediterranean or the Atlantic?

    The Mediterranean has warmer, calmer water in summer. By contrast, the Atlantic offers more variety, surf and pleasant weather all year.

    Can you visit the beaches in Morocco in winter?

    Yes, especially in Agadir, Taghazout and Dakhla. Air temperatures there reach around 20-25 °C in winter, although the sea is cool.

    Which beaches in Morocco are closest to Marrakech?

    Marrakech is inland. The nearest beach is Essaouira, about three hours by car. Similarly, Agadir is about three hours away by motorway.

    Can you wear a bikini on the beaches in Morocco?

    Yes, on tourist beaches and at hotels. Topless sunbathing is not allowed. On very local beaches, however, be more discreet.

    Are the beaches in Morocco safe?

    Yes for tourists, as long as you respect the flags and swim in supervised areas. In fact, the main danger is Atlantic currents.

    Where is the best surf in Morocco?

    Taghazout is the surf capital. Imsouane is best for learning. Safi and Anchor Point are for experts.

    When is the best time for the northern beaches in Morocco?

    From June to September. However, June and September are quieter and cheaper than July and August.

    Can I combine beach and desert in one week?

    Yes. A 7-8 day route from Marrakech lets you see the Merzouga dunes and finish with two nights in Essaouira.
